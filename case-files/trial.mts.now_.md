# Musk v. Altman

## Case summary

_**Musk v. Altman**_ — sometimes styled _**Musk v. OpenAI**_ — is a federal civil trial currently being conducted before **[Hon. Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers](https://trial.mts.now/character/yvonne-gonzalez-rogers)** in the **United States District Court for the Northern District of California**. Filed by **[Elon Musk](https://trial.mts.now/character/elon-musk)**, a co‑founder of [OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI), against the company, its CEO **[Sam Altman](https://trial.mts.now/character/sam-altman)**, its president **[Greg Brockman](https://trial.mts.now/character/greg-brockman)**, and **[Microsoft](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Microsoft)**, the case asks a jury whether OpenAI's 2019 conversion to a "capped‑profit" structure — and the multi‑billion‑dollar partnership with Microsoft that followed — broke promises made to Musk when he donated roughly $38 million to the original 501(c)(3) nonprofit.

The trial opened on **April 28, 2026** and, at the close of business on **May 1, 2026**, plaintiffs' case‑in‑chief is in its second witness. So far the courtroom has heard live testimony from Musk himself and from his family‑office head **[Jared Birchall](https://trial.mts.now/character/jared-birchall)**; **[Altman](https://trial.mts.now/character/sam-altman)** and **Microsoft CEO** **[Satya Nadella](https://trial.mts.now/character/satya-nadella)** will be examined during the defense cases. Expected witnesses include UC Berkeley AI safety researcher **[Stuart Russell](https://trial.mts.now/character/stuart-russell)**, **[Brockman](https://trial.mts.now/character/greg-brockman)**, former OpenAI board members **[Helen Toner](https://trial.mts.now/character/helen-toner)** and **[Tasha McCauley](https://trial.mts.now/character/tasha-mccauley)** (by video), and Columbia Law dean emeritus **[David Schizer](https://trial.mts.now/character/david-schizer)**.

This wiki is a working record of the trial built from the **official daily transcripts** filed by the court reporter. Every quotation in these pages cites a transcript file and line number such as `(042826TT.txt:1234)` so each claim can be checked against the underlying source.
