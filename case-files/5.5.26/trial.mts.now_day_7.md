### 2e. Robert Wu — OpenAI 30(b)(6) video deposition

The court played the **Robert Wu** deposition immediately after Brockman stepped down. Wu is OpenAI's designated Rule 30(b)(6) corporate representative on the Microsoft agreements and the corporate-structure history. The judge's instruction:

> The Court: "Members of the jury, part of the evidence that you will hear comes from depositions. … When a person is unavailable to testify at trial, **the deposition of that person may be used at trial**. … You should consider deposition testimony presented to you here in court in lieu of live testimony in the same way as if the witness had been here present to testify." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:50 PT)_

The deposition is the day's first organized exposition of the **economics** of OpenAI's restructuring.

#### The 2015 Delaware certificate and "the mission"

> Plaintiffs (deposition Q): "The specific purpose of this corporation is to provide funding for research, development, and distribution of technology related to artificial intelligence. The resulting technology will benefit the public and the corporation will seek to open source technology for the public benefit when applicable. **The corporation is not organized for the private gain of any person.** Does this language accurately state OpenAI's mission?" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:53 PT)_

> Wu: " **The language in the certificate of incorporation does reflect what was approved by the board at that time** as the mission reflected in the Delaware charter." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:54 PT)_

> Q: "Is that still OpenAI's mission today?" — A: " **The exact language of the mission as stated in the Delaware charter has changed over time.**" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:54 PT)_

#### The 2018 LP, the 2019 IP transfer, the assets that stayed at the nonprofit

> "OpenAI Inc. did contribute certain assets in 2019 into OpenAI LP in an exchange and receive economic interest in OpenAI LP both through its first close limited partnership interest and what we refer to as the **residual interest**." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:56 PT)_

> Q: "Which intellectual property did OpenAI Inc. transfer to OpenAI LP in 2019?" — A: " **My understanding is that was all of the IP that was developed at the nonprofit at that point in time was contributed down to OpenAI LP.**" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:57 PT)_

> Q: "What did OpenAI Inc. retain?" — A: " **Trademarks to OpenAI**, and perhaps there were other assets." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:57 PT)_

#### Microsoft — investment-by-investment

> Q: "Microsoft invested $1,000,000,000, and it has a target redemption amount of $20,000,000,000." _(2019 deal — 5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~14:58 PT)_

> Wu, on the OpenAI board's process: " **The board took into consideration as always what was in the best interest of the mission**, and taking into consideration all of that — kind of the need for capital to purchase compute and attract talent, ultimately took into account all of those considerations and made a determination that this was a fair deal that was in the best interest of the mission." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:01 PT)_

> "Microsoft did make an additional capital commitment of **$10,000,000,000**. … A portion of that was actual cash attributed, and a portion of that was for compute." _(2023 deal — 5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:11 PT)_

> Q: "The target redemption amount for Microsoft's $10,000,000,000 investment is **$60,000,000,000**. Is that a six times return on investment?" — A: " **Correct.**" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:11 PT)_

> Q: "What did the OpenAI board do to determine whether that six x return was reasonable and fair to the nonprofit?" — A: paraphrased "best interests of the mission … timing of the partnership with Microsoft … made the determination that a six x return … was in the best interest of the mission." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:12 PT)_

> Q: "Did the board do any **quantitative analysis** to determine whether the six x return was appropriate?" — A: " **I don't know.**" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:13 PT)_

> Q: "Did the board do any **financial modeling** to determine whether the six x return was appropriate?" — A: " **I don't know about specific financial modeling.**" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:13 PT)_

#### The big number — and the 20% escalator

> Wu: " **Back-of-the-napkin math here, that the total target redemption amounts for all of the limited partners, including the nonprofit, and Microsoft, rounded to $250,000,000,000.** And the residual, the nonprofit specific residual interest **would not be paid until those target redemption amounts**." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:16 PT)_

> Q: "And from 2025 forward, the target redemption amount escalates?" — A: "Correct. **The target redemption amount is increased starting at $202.5 \[billion\] for each full fiscal year by 20%.** For the members." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:16 PT)_

#### The AGI carveout and the Watershed MOU

> Wu: "Based kind of on a review of this definition of licensed IP, and the definitions of IP and technology in this agreement, **it does appear that OpenAI Inc., OpCo, licensed them all IP and technology as defined in this agreement excluding only AGI**." _(2023 second amended JDCA — 5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:17 PT)_

> Wu: "This is the **Watershed MOU** entered into between OpenAI and Microsoft laying out at a high level the governance and capital structure for **a proposed recapitalization of the for-profit subsidiary**." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:18 PT)_

> Q: "Under these new terms, would Microsoft now have rights in OpenAI's intellectual property even after OpenAI achieves AGI?" — A: "Under the current proposed construct under the Watershed MOU … upon a determination of AGI, **Microsoft would not receive certain intellectual property, including those AGI research IP and the other categories listed here. But would receive certain product IP, subject to certain conditions.**" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:19 PT)_

> Q: "This provision — would Microsoft get new rights in OpenAI's post-AGI intellectual property?" — A: "Under the Watershed construct, **Microsoft would receive — once there is a determination that there is AGI through this third-party panel — certain access to certain product IP**." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:20 PT)_

#### The September 2024 plan to invert the LLC waterfall

> Q: "Was around this time in September 2024, was OpenAI's board considering converting the for-profit subsidiary into an uncapped for-profit structure?" — A: " **There were discussions … on potential structures … to put the for-profit entity in the best position to … attract investors again to raise the capital needed to purchase compute and attract talent.**" _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:14 PT)_

> Wu: "A structure that was **not a bespoke capped-profit structure, but more of a traditional stock corporation**." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:15 PT)_

> Wu: "One of the considerations was **converting the LLC waterfall into a more traditional corporate stock ownership capital structure**. And then that structure — stock that investors, employees, or others hold — **would not be subject to a particular cap on profit distributions**." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:15 PT)_

> Q: "When was this first discussed?" — A: "Don't have the exact date on when it was discussed, but … in **April '24, or perhaps May '24** when the **mission strategy committee** was first formed." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:15 PT)_

#### Who works at OpenAI Inc. (the nonprofit)

> Q: "Does OpenAI Inc. have any full-time employees?" — A: " **I don't believe it has any full time employees** \[other than\] the board of directors, and Sam is the CEO." _(5/5/2026 Testimony @ ~15:09 PT)_
