# The Hits from Day 6

\*\*Day 6 of _Musk v. Altman_ — Monday, May 4, 2026.\*\* First day of plaintiffs' AI‑safety case and first day the courtroom audio was opened to the public via the court's livestream under Local Civil Rule 77‑3.

> **→ [Read the full Day 6 digest](https://trial.mts.now/day/6)** — speaker‑attributed transcript of every line of testimony, witness‑by‑witness breakdown, every exhibit, every notable judicial moment.

## In two paragraphs

The morning belonged to **Stuart Russell**, the UC Berkeley AI safety pioneer and co‑author of the _Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach_ textbook used in 1,500+ courses across 135 countries. Plaintiffs called him to give the jury a tour of the AI‑risk landscape: discrimination, AI psychosis, biorisk, cyber misuse, disinformation, the "cancer‑cure" misalignment example, and the winner‑take‑all dynamics of AGI. The judge held him strictly to his pre‑filed expert report — sustaining roughly half a dozen scope objections — and OpenAI counsel **William Frentzen** used cross to lock in that Russell was offering _no_ opinions about OpenAI specifically: he had not reviewed the complaint, not reviewed any case witness's testimony, not analyzed OpenAI's safety practices, not compared OpenAI to peer labs, and not opined on whether nonprofit structure affects safety. His total compensation in the case (~$235,000) is roughly 20% of his annual income. Frentzen also surfaced the awkward fact that Russell sits on the advisory board of an organization (Americans for Responsible Innovation) that ran a 2025 campaign to make Musk White House AI adviser — a campaign Russell, on deposition, said he would _not_ have supported. Microsoft's **Jay Gerrado** ran a brief, surgical cross to confirm Russell offers no opinion on the OpenAI/Microsoft partnership.

The afternoon's centerpiece — and arguably the most damaging witness exam plaintiffs have run since Musk's own direct on Day 2 — was **Greg Brockman's cross by Mr. Kry**. Brockman, OpenAI's co‑founder and current president, was walked through his own emails and a contemporaneous personal journal he has kept on his laptop since 2010. The journal contains, in his own words, the line that he "cannot say we are committed to the nonprofit. … if three months later we're doing a B Corp, that it was a lie"; the line that converting OpenAI to a B Corp without Musk would be "morally bankrupt"; and the line, written six days after telling Musk he wanted to keep fundraising for the charity, that "this is the only chance we have to get out from under Elon. … take me to $1 billion." Brockman conceded a $30 billion personal stake in OpenAI today (acquired in 2018 for $0), a hidden $10 million side‑equity grant from Sam Altman in 2017 that he never disclosed to Musk, and a longstanding Cerebras investment that he failed to disclose to Musk while pushing OpenAI toward a Cerebras transaction — a conflict that has carried through to OpenAI's December 2025 deal to buy ~$10 billion in Cerebras chips. He also admitted he never paid the $100,000 personal donation he had publicly told prospective donors he was making. On Wachtell's redirect, Brockman flatly denied ever committing to Musk that OpenAI would stay a nonprofit, said Musk left in 2018 to build an AGI competitor inside Tesla "in secret" because "the shareholders wouldn't like it," and pointed to the OpenAI Foundation's current ~$150–200B equity stake as proof that the mission survived. The OpenAI for‑profit's most recent post‑money valuation, disclosed in passing today: **$840 billion**. Microsoft's stake: ~$200 billion. An IPO is under exploration.

### Brockman's own journal — 2017

> "This is the only chance we have to get out from under Elon. … We truly have a chance to make this happen financially, **take me to $1,000,000,000**."
>
> — Brockman, personal journal, Aug 21, 2017 (PX 151)

> "It'd be wrong to steal the nonprofit from him. To convert to B Corp without him. They'd be pretty **morally bankrupt**. And he's really not an idiot."
>
> — Brockman, personal journal, Nov 6, 2017 (PX 161)

> "Cannot say that we are committed to the nonprofit. Don't wanna say that we're committed if three months later we're doing a B Corp, **that it was a lie**."
>
> — Brockman, personal journal, Nov 6, 2017 (PX 161)

> "His story will correctly be that **we weren't honest with him** in the end about still wanting to do the for‑profit just without him."
>
> — Brockman, personal journal, Nov 6, 2017 (PX 161)

> "It would be nice to be making the billions. … We've been thinking that maybe we should just **flip to a full profit**. Making money for us sounds great."
>
> — Brockman, personal journal, Nov 12, 2017 (PX 163) — six days after telling Musk in person that he wanted to keep fundraising for the nonprofit

### Brockman on the stand — cross by Mr. Kry

> Q: "So that was a false statement." — A: "I disagree with that characterization."
>
> — on the $100,000 personal donation publicly claimed to Marissa Mayer in October 2015 but never paid (PX 8)

> Q: "Did you think it was morally bankrupt to say that you were personally donating $100,000 and then not do that?" — A: "No."

> "Elon is sometimes hard to get a hold of, but telling Shivon, telling Sam et al., in general, **we view that as telling Elon**."
>
> — Brockman's stated reason for never disclosing the Cerebras conflict to Musk while pushing OpenAI toward a Cerebras transaction

> A: "It's an expression of frustration, not a plan." — Q: "How many times did you rehearse that?"
>
> — on a 2017 journal entry

> A: "I offered, and still would do it today." — Q: "Oh, you offered? It won't take your money?" — A: "I actually asked Sam right as we got started that, hey, so how do I actually donate this. He said he'd let me know when the time was." — Q: "He hasn't let you know yet?" — A: " **He has not.**"
>
> — on the never‑paid $100K pledge, eleven years later

> A: "It takes $30 billion to get you out of bed in the morning, but a billion dollars doesn't get you out of bed in the morning. Is that what you're saying?" — Q: "That is not what I'm saying."
>
> — Brockman trying to walk back his "I'd be good with a billion" journal entry against his ~$30B current stake

> "Kind of like the guy that robs a bank and says, you know, I only took a million dollars. He's still got $100 million in the bank, so there's no big deal."
>
> — Mr. Kry, analogizing the "but the nonprofit has $150B" defense (objection — argumentative)

> "I've honestly **never really been certain what I'm being sued for**."
>
> — Brockman, when shown the December 2023 "Charter 2.0" draft that removed "primary fiduciary duty is to humanity" and added "we've grown to regard capitalism not as a constraint, but instead as a positive force"
