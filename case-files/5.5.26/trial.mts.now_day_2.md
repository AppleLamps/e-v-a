# Day 2 — April 28, 2026 — Volume 2 (pages 198–385)

## 1\. Summary

Day 2 was the substantive opening of trial. The morning began with pretrial housekeeping: motions in limine over courtroom-sequestered witnesses (Joshua Achiam allowed to stay as OpenAI's corporate rep), discovery of a newly-amended Microsoft/OpenAI contract reported the prior day, and a remarkable on-the-record colloquy in which Judge Gonzalez Rogers personally questioned Elon Musk about a "barrage of tweets" he posted during jury selection — securing his agreement (and Altman's and Brockman's) to "clean slate" social-media restraint going forward. The judge then read preliminary jury instructions and the parties delivered their three openings: Steven Molo for plaintiffs (the "stolen charity" theme, museum-store analogy, AI-safety framing through Stuart Russell), William Savitt for the OpenAI defendants (Musk as a sore-loser competitor who "picked up his marbles and went home," "tale of two Elons," statute-of-limitations attack), and Russell Cohen for Microsoft (due-diligence story, "virtuous cycle," and a five-year delay before suing). After lunch Musk was sworn (line 4165) and began direct examination by Mr. Molo, covering biography (South Africa → Canada → Penn → Stanford), his other companies (SpaceX, Tesla, Neuralink, Boring Company), his decade-plus AI-safety concerns (including a striking account of Larry Page calling him a "speciesist"), the founding of OpenAI as a 501(c)(3) "counterweight to Google," and the beginning of the 2017 negotiation breakdown over the for-profit equity split. Direct stopped mid-thread on Exhibit 153; cross-examination is teed up for Day 3.

## 4\. Musk testimony highlights (direct examination by Mr. Molo)

### The lawsuit and the stakes

> "FUNDAMENTALLY, I THINK IT -- THEY'RE GOING TO TRY TO MAKE THIS LAWSUIT SEEM VERY COMPLICATED BUT I THINK IT'S ACTUALLY VERY SIMPLE, WHICH IS THAT IT'S NOT OKAY TO STEAL A CHARITY." (042826TT.txt:4186)

> "IF THE VERDICT COMES UP THAT EFFECTIVELY MAKES IT OKAY TO LOOT A CHARITY, THE ENTIRE FOUNDATION OF CHARITABLE GIVING IN AMERICA WILL BE DESTROYED. THAT'S MY CONCERN." (042826TT.txt:4204)

### Background and work ethic

> "I WAS BORN AND RAISED IN SOUTH AFRICA, AND I LEFT WHEN I WAS 17, JUST BY MYSELF.... I ARRIVED IN MONTREAL WITH ABOUT $2,500 IN CANADIAN TRAVELERS CHECKS AND ONE BAG OF CLOTHES AND ONE BAG OF BOOKS, AND TRAVELLED AROUND AND DID A BUNCH OF ODD JOBS." (042826TT.txt:4231)

> "I WORKED IN A VARIETY OF JOBS. I WAS -- ACTUALLY QUITE A WIDE VARIETY. I WAS A LUMBERJACK. I WAITED TABLES.... I WORKED IN A LUMBER MILL. THAT WAS THE TOUGHEST JOB, I THINK." (042826TT.txt:4249)

> "WHEN I STARTED ZIP2, I HAD $100,000 IN STUDENT DEBT AND ONE COMPUTER." (042826TT.txt:4362)

> "I DO WORK A LOT OF HOURS. SO, I MEAN, I TYPICALLY WORK 80 TO 100 HOURS A WEEK. I DON'T TYPICALLY TAKE VACATIONS. I DON'T HAVE ANY VACATION HOMES, AND I DON'T HAVE YACHTS OR ANYTHING." (042826TT.txt:4666)

> "I GENERALLY THINK MY COMPANIES ARE GOING TO FAIL UNTIL PROVEN OTHERWISE." (042826TT.txt:4304)

### AI as existential concern — and the Larry Page anecdote

> "I HAVE EXTREME CONCERNS ABOUT AI. I'VE HAD CONCERNS ABOUT AI FOR A VERY LONG TIME. AND, IN FACT, THE -- THE REASON THAT OPENAI EXISTS IS BECAUSE I WAS VERY CLOSE FRIENDS WITH LARRY PAGE AT GOOGLE, AND I STAYED AT HIS HOUSE AND WE TALKED FOR MANY HOURS ABOUT AI SAFETY. AND AT SOME POINT, IT BECAME CLEAR TO ME THAT LARRY PAGE WAS NOT BEING SUFFICIENTLY CARING ABOUT THE SAFETY OF AI." (042826TT.txt:4820)

> "AND I SAID, 'WELL, WHAT IF AI WIPES OUT ALL THE HUMANS?' AND HE SAID, 'THAT WILL BE FINE AS LONG AS ARTIFICIAL INTELLIGENCE SURVIVES.' AND I'M, LIKE, WELL, THAT'S -- THAT'S INSANE." (042826TT.txt:4828)

> "MY PERSPECTIVE ON OPENAI, IN A NUTSHELL, IS IT REALLY -- THE REASON THAT OPENAI EXISTS IS BECAUSE LARRY PAGE CALLED ME A SPECIESIST, THAT'S FUNDAMENTALLY IT." (042826TT.txt:4995)

## 5\. Notable judicial / procedural moments

### The pre-trial Musk Twitter colloquy

The most extraordinary on-record moment of the day: after OpenAI's counsel raised Musk's "barrage of tweets" during jury selection, the judge called Musk forward to the lectern personally.

> THE COURT: "I'M JUST GOING TO ASK YOU DIRECTLY, SIR.... DO YOU WANT THIS TO BE AN ISSUE? I DON'T WANT TO HAVE -- I NEVER HAD TO HAVE A GAG ORDER. I DON'T USE -- I DON'T USE SOCIAL MEDIA. I NEVER HAVE. IT'S NOT APPROPRIATE FOR JUDGES TO SIT THERE AND TWEET THINGS." (042826TT.txt:697)

> THE COURT: "PERHAPS YOU HAVE NEVER DONE THAT BEFORE. PERHAPS LET'S SEE IF YOU COULD DO -- LIKE THIS WOULD BE A FIRST. WOULD YOU AGREE TO THAT?"
>
> MR. MUSK: "YES, ABSOLUTELY, OF COURSE." (042826TT.txt:738)

She then individually obtained the same agreement from Altman and Brockman ("I SEE A THUMBS UP" — line 750).

## 7\. Loose ends / cliffhangers

- **Musk direct stops mid-Exhibit 153**, in the middle of the 2017 equity-split / control negotiation narrative. Day 3 will continue direct, then OpenAI's cross (Savitt estimates "two to three hours"), then Microsoft's cross (likely brief — Cohen previewed limited involvement), then the second plaintiff witness, **Jared Birchall** (Musk's family-office manager who handled the OpenAI wire transfers).
- Musk has previewed but not yet detailed: (1) the "completely removed" profit cap and AGI-dissolution clause from the for-profit (he started to launch into it at line 5850 before being redirected); (2) the Nadella "below them, above them, and around them" episode (referenced extensively in openings but not yet in evidence); (3) the November 2023 Altman firing/reinstatement.
- **Stuart Russell** (UC Berkeley AI-safety professor) — both sides previewed his testimony. He will testify about four risk categories: individual, systemic, misalignment/loss-of-control, and concentration risk (042826TT.txt:1397–1430).
- **David Schizer** (former Columbia Law dean, nonprofit-governance expert) — plaintiffs' charity-law expert on how nonprofits should be run.
- **Helen Toner and Tasha McCauley** — former OpenAI board members involved in the November 2023 Altman firing — will testify by deposition video. Judge sustained most plaintiffs' designations on Toner as leading.
- **Jury question**: Judge Gonzalez Rogers indicated she has drafted her own jury instructions because "I didn't like either side's instructions" and wants short briefing on a set of legal issues before Friday's instruction conference (042826TT.txt:6340–6351).
- **Open trademark / "Open" naming question**: The judge's pointed reference to the OpenAI v. Open Space AI prosecution history may foreshadow an evidentiary ruling — or just a strong hint to the defense to drop the line of argument that "open" did not refer to open source.
- **Twitter "clean slate"**: Whether Musk actually keeps his agreement to refrain from tweeting about the case during trial is now a live oversight issue; the judge specifically framed it as something she would be watching ("LIKE THIS WOULD BE A FIRST").
- **Microsoft witness lineup** previewed: Satya Nadella (CEO), Kevin Scott (CTO), Amy Hood (CFO), Mike Wetter (corporate VP / due-diligence lead).
- **Statute-of-limitations defense**: Both defendants pinned heavily on the September 24, 2020 Musk tweet ("essentially captured by Microsoft") as evidence Musk knew enough to sue more than three years before filing.
