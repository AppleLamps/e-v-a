# Greg Brockman's Journal

A personal journal **[Greg Brockman](https://trial.mts.now/character/greg-brockman)** has kept on his laptop since **2010**. He used it for both his personal and professional life and, on his telling, **wrote it for himself** — "very chain of thought… stream of consciousness… a lot of things that are self contradictory." Entries about **[OpenAI](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/OpenAI)** run from **2015 to ~2023**. **460 pages** were produced in discovery; roughly **100 of those pages cover the August–September 2017 negotiation window** alone. Plaintiffs put four entries in evidence as **PX 151, 154, 161, 163**; defense added a fifth, **DX 1252**, on Day 7. The entries are now central to the case — Mr. Kry's Day 6 cross built around them, and Day 7 — Brockman's second day on the stand — was largely a fight over how to read them.

## Context

**What it is.** Brockman started the journal in **2010** thinking about what to study in school and kept it as a personal-and-professional notebook from then on. He wrote about OpenAI in it from **2015 through roughly 2023**. He described its style on the stand as " **very chain of thought**… stream of consciousness… a lot of things that are self contradictory… trying to test out — do I believe this? Something someone else said that I kinda write down to see how it feels." It also contains **embedded text and Signal messages from other people** alongside his own writing. **460 pages** were produced in discovery; ~100 of those cover just the August–September 2017 negotiation. Brockman: " **It's very painful. Very deeply personal writings that were never meant for the world to see, but there's nothing in there I'm ashamed of.**"

**How it came in.** Plaintiffs walked Brockman through four entries on Day 6 cross ( **[Mr. Kry](https://trial.mts.now/character/robert-kry)**) and again on Day 7 cross ( **[Mr. Molo](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gary_Molo)**), and re-read large parts of them on Day 7 redirect ( **Mr. Kry**) to put each line back in chronological context. The defense ( **Mr. Gerrada** for OpenAI) introduced the **Nov 5, 2017** entry ( **DX 1252**, "Real decision is fire Elon" / "Fire Elon route") on Day 7 over Molo's hearsay objection — admitted as a **prior consistent statement** to rebut Molo's accusation that Brockman's "morally bankrupt" gloss was a recent fabrication.

**The interpretive fight.** Brockman's defense reading is that " **morally bankrupt**" referred to _voting to remove Musk from the OpenAI board and then creating a B Corp without him_ — not to the for-profit conversion as such. He testified the " **only chance to get out from Elon**" line was about saying no to Musk's unilateral-control demand, after which Musk had said he would quit and they would build the for-profit alone; the "$1,000,000,000" line was a private mental ceiling above which the with-Musk-vs-without-Musk choice would be financially indifferent to him. Plaintiffs' reading is that the journal is a **contemporaneous record of intent** to push Musk out and convert OpenAI to a for-profit while publicly maintaining commitment to the nonprofit — Molo's recross frame: " **It's just an after-the-fact manufactured excuse for the very honest emotion that you expressed in the journal at the time.**"

**Why it matters.** The journal is the only contemporaneous, internal-to-OpenAI document in evidence that addresses honesty and motive in 2017–2018. Every other communication of that period is either an email between Musk and the founders (where the founders were addressing Musk directly) or a public-facing artifact (the Charter, blog posts, the IRS filings). The journal is Brockman writing to himself, in real time, while the negotiations were happening — which is why both sides spent two days of cross-examination on it and why Plaintiffs' equitable-remedies theory leans so heavily on it.
