# Case Overview

## Court filing

_Elon Musk, et al., v. Samuel Altman, et al._ United States District Court, Northern District of California, Oakland Division. Case No. **4:24-CV-04722 YGR**. Before the Hon. **Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers**, U.S. District Judge. Nine-person jury; trial expected to last roughly four weeks. Filed by Musk in 2024.

## What the trial is about

Musk sued OpenAI, its CEO Sam Altman, its president Greg Brockman, and Microsoft Corp., alleging that the defendants induced him to fund OpenAI as a 501(c)(3) charity for "the benefit of mankind as a whole" and then converted that charity — and its accumulated value — into a for-profit empire dominated by Microsoft.

Of the **26 claims Musk asserted in 2024, only two survive to trial**:

1. **Breach of charitable trust** under California law.
2. **Restitution under a theory of unjust enrichment.** (Note: the court ruled on Day 5 that under California Supreme Court precedent there is no standalone "unjust enrichment" cause of action — only restitution. See `(050126TT.txt:1245)`.)

Plaintiffs also pursue an alternative **Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17510.6 / .8 charitable-solicitation theory**, which the judge has indicated is on weak footing under _Cohen v. Kabbalah Centre_, 35 Cal.App.5th 13 (2019).

## What plaintiffs want

- A finding that the defendants breached a charitable trust.
- Restitution to Musk of the ~$38 million he donated.
- Plaintiffs floated on Day 5 the idea that Musk would make an "irrevocable instruction" to redirect any restitution recovery back to a nonprofit; the judge was skeptical: "I COULD ALSO JUST NOT GIVE AN ADVISORY OPINION ON THIS TOPIC." `(050126TT.txt:1521)`
- Equitable relief over OpenAI's restructuring (the contours of which are reserved for a post-jury phase).

## The defense theories

- **OpenAI / Altman / Brockman (Wachtell):** Musk wanted control, didn't get it, picked up his marbles, and is suing because his AI competitor (xAI) lost. "A pageant of hypocrisy." `(042826TT.txt:2177)`
- **OpenAI statute-of-limitations defense:** Musk publicly tweeted as early as Sept. 2020 that "OpenAI is essentially captured by Microsoft" — more than three years before suit. `(042826TT.txt:3855)`
- **Microsoft (Dechert):** Microsoft did its diligence, asked, was told everything was on mission, and Musk "never picked up the phone" to object. `(042826TT.txt:3841)`
- **No written promise:** "There isn't a single email or a single text message or any other document where Mr. Musk claims that OpenAI was obligated to remain a nonprofit." — Savitt opening `(042826TT.txt:2698)`
- **The $1B / $38M gap:** Musk publicly committed $1B and contributed only ~$38M. `(042826TT.txt:2736)`

## Stakes

- Press coverage frames the case as a possible $150B exposure tied to the OpenAI restructuring; the Day 7 Robert Wu deposition put the inside number at **~$250 billion in aggregate target redemption** that has to be paid out to limited partners (including Microsoft) before the nonprofit's residual interest pays a cent — and that ceiling **escalates 20% per year starting in 2025**.
- The **Watershed MOU**, surfaced through Wu, would further restructure the for-profit and let **Microsoft retain product-IP rights even after a third-party-panel determination that AGI has been achieved** — directly implicating the original 2018 Charter provision that AGI would be excluded from any commercial license.
- A win for Musk could unwind or constrain OpenAI's transition out of nonprofit governance and reshape how AI charities can convert to for-profits.
- A loss could entrench OpenAI's structure and Microsoft's commercial position.
- The judge has framed it narrowly: **"This is not a trial on the safety risks of artificial intelligence. ... The question here is whether there was a breach of charitable trust."**`(043026TT.txt:817–820)`

## What this trial is NOT about (per court rulings)

- AI extinction risk — categorically excluded from expert testimony. `(043026TT.txt:712)`
- xAI's safety practices in detail (Grok specifics off-limits). `(042926TT.txt:7517)`
- Trump-administration AI policy. `(042926TT.txt:7644)`
- OpenAI's current safety practices generally (FRE 403 limits).

## Key legal sub-issues being briefed

- **§ 17510.6 vs. § 17510.8** — does the membership-exception trump the solicitation theory? Judge has signaled yes, under _Cohen v. Kabbalah Centre_. `(050126TT.txt:604)`
- **Statute of limitations / discovery rule** under _Fox v. Ethicon Endo-Surgery_ — when did Musk know enough to sue? `(050126TT.txt:1671)`
- **Laches** — _BART v. Spencer_ model instruction. `(050126TT.txt:1900)`
- **Restatement (Third) of Trusts § 28** — does the OpenAI charter create a charitable trust, or just a generic "for the public good" purpose? `(050126TT.txt:1078)`
