## 1\. Summary

Day 6 was the first day of plaintiffs' AI‑safety case and the first day the trial was open to the public via the court's audio livestream. **Stuart Russell**, the UC Berkeley AI safety pioneer and co‑author of _Artificial Intelligence: A Modern Approach_, opened the morning as plaintiffs' first non‑party fact/expert witness; **Greg Brockman**, OpenAI's co‑founder and current president, took the stand immediately after and remained on the witness chair through lunch.

Russell's direct, run by **Mr. Kry** for plaintiffs, was tightly framed as "general background on the risks of AI" — discrimination, AI‑induced psychosis, biorisk, cyber misuse, disinformation, misalignment ("we might ask an AI system to cure cancer as quickly as possible. … the AI system could devise a plan … inducing tumors in everybody on earth"), and the winner‑take‑all dynamics of AGI. The judge held him to a tight leash: at least three sustained scope objections kept Russell from straying past his expert report into specific OpenAI products (Mythos, system cards) or current company practice. **Mr. Frentzen** for OpenAI cross‑examined; **Mr. Gerrado** for Microsoft followed with a brief targeted cross. Both got Russell to concede he had been retained to give _general_ AI‑safety background only — no analysis of OpenAI's safety practices, no comparison with peer labs, no opinion on the OpenAI/Microsoft partnership, no review of the complaint, no review of any case witness testimony — and that his $235,000 compensation represents roughly 20% of his annual income.

The day's centerpiece was **Brockman's cross by Mr. Kry**, which ran over two hours and was the most damaging witness exam plaintiffs have had since Musk's own direct on Day 2. Kry methodically built the picture of a co‑founder who (1) solicited founding donations to a charity by claiming a personal $100,000 gift he never paid, (2) accepted a hidden $10,000,000 side‑equity grant from Sam Altman in 2017 that he did not disclose to Musk, (3) was an investor in **Cerebras** while pushing OpenAI to merge with or acquire Cerebras, (4) wrote in his own contemporaneous laptop journal that converting the nonprofit to a B Corp without Musk would be "morally bankrupt" and that he could not say OpenAI was committed to the nonprofit because saying so would be "a lie," (5) wrote in that same journal six days after telling Musk he wanted to keep fundraising for the charity that "this is the only chance we have to get out from under Elon. … take me to $1,000,000,000," and (6) ended up holding ~$30 billion in OpenAI equity. The exam ended with Kry asking, point blank, why — given Brockman said in his own journal he would "be good with a billion" — he didn't simply donate the remaining $29 billion to the charity he owed a fiduciary duty to. Brockman's answer: "There's a deep mischaracterization of how this all works."

The afternoon redirect ( **Mr. Savitt**, Wachtell, for OpenAI) elicited a flat denial that Brockman ever committed — or heard anyone at OpenAI commit — to Musk that OpenAI would stay a nonprofit; testimony that Musk left in 2018 to start an AGI competitor inside Tesla because in Musk's own words there was "0% chance" of AGI at OpenAI and that the Tesla AGI work would be done "in secret" because "the shareholders wouldn't like it"; that Musk's last contribution was 2020; and that the OpenAI Foundation today holds ~$150–200B in PBC equity and funds AI‑resilience and Alzheimer's research initiatives.

Of note for the equitable‑remedies fight previewed on Day 5: the OpenAI for‑profit's most recent post‑money valuation is **$840 billion**, Microsoft's stake is worth **~$200 billion**, and OpenAI is exploring an IPO.

Plaintiffs' Exhibits 67, 90, 99 etc. established that, in OpenAI's first three years, Musk personally:

- Provided the substantial majority of OpenAI's funding (~$38M total).
- Helped recruit "all but one" of the founding team — Brockman: "Some people, certainly, were excited about the prospect of working with him." All but one ended up joining.
- Picked up the phone to **Satya Nadella** in June 2017 to secure ~10,000 NVIDIA GPUs from Microsoft for OpenAI's DOTA project, after which Brockman emailed back: "Awesome. Thanks." Q: "You didn't know Satya Nadella at that point in time?" — **"Did not know Satya personally at that time. No."** _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~10:01–10:04 PT)_
- Personally arranged for OpenAI to receive one of the first NVIDIA DGX‑1 supercomputers via Jensen Huang. Q: "Do you think Mr. Huang could have picked you out of a crowd of two at that time?" — **"I didn't know Jensen at the time. No."** _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~10:04–10:05 PT)_
- Wrote January 1, 2018 (PX 99): Brockman's own email back to Musk read **"in every meeting with you I continue to learn, grow, and see the world in a new way. … it was an honor to work alongside you."** _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~09:59–10:00 PT)_

### 4f. Other deals that were considered

Brockman conceded the 2017–2018 capitalization brainstorm covered:

- **Cerebras** — merger or acquisition, while Brockman held undisclosed Cerebras equity.
- **An Initial Coin Offering** (ICO). "A lot of those are dodgy enterprises. … Elon vetoed that idea." (Brockman initially disputed this, then conceded.) _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~10:20–10:21 PT)_
- **A Tesla merger.** "Elon definitely pushed that very hard. Yes." Did not consummate. _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~10:21–10:23 PT)_

Under Brockman's proposal: **Brockman 11%** (3.33% earned by writing a check, 7.68% sweat equity), **Musk 51.2%**. _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~10:25–10:26 PT)_

### 4g. Musk's September 22, 2017 ultimatum, and Brockman's response

PX 157: September 22, 2017 email from Musk:

> "Either go do something on your own or continue with OpenAI as a nonprofit. I will no longer fund OpenAI until you have made a firm commitment to stay. Or I'm just being a fool who is essentially providing free funding for you to create a startup."

Two months later — six days before he wrote "the only chance we have to get out from under Elon" — Brockman told Musk in a meeting "we wanted to get more results in the nonprofit and to fundraise there." He recorded that exchange in the same journal in which he had already written that they could not honestly tell Musk they were committed. _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~11:12–11:13 PT)_

### 4i. The November 2023 board firing

### 4j. The Charter 2.0 draft

PX 359, a December 28, 2023 email from Brockman to Sam Altman, Jason Kwon, and the OpenAI board (Larry Summers, Brett Taylor, Adam D'Angelo): Brockman attached a **"Charter 2.0"** draft for board feedback. The original 2018 Charter said OpenAI's " **primary fiduciary duty is to humanity**." The 2.0 draft removed that line and added:

This was the same year as the **$10 billion** Microsoft investment. The draft was never published. ## 5\. Brockman redirect (Savitt) — the defense narrative

Wachtell's William Savitt did a brief, leading‑question redirect on the topics Mr. Molo had covered, over Kry's objection. The court allowed it, ruling it permissible cross‑exam on direct‑exam topics for a short period. Highlights:

- "What commitments, if any, did you ever make to Elon Musk about OpenAI's corporate structure?" — **"None."**
- "What commitments, if any, did you ever hear anyone at OpenAI make to Mr. Musk about OpenAI's corporate structure?" — **"Never heard any."** _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~12:11 PT)_

- **Why Musk left in 2018.** "He said the most important thing was that there was going to be a counterweight to Google's DeepMind. … He said there was no hope. - **Last Musk contribution.** **2020.**

- **Equity granted by board, Brockman recused.** "I did not participate in the board vote that granted you your stake. … Essentially all" OpenAI employees have for‑profit equity. _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~12:19–12:20 PT)_

- **Foundation activities.** Two pillars today: (1) **AI resilience** ("society really needs to build around the shape of this technology in the same way that society is built around cars and engines — roads, seat belts, rules of the road"), (2) **public health** including a partnership with **ARC Institute** on Alzheimer's. Brockman currently serves the foundation as "an informal adviser." _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~12:21–12:22 PT)_

- **Counterfactual.** "How would the foundation's initiatives be funded if not for the OpenAI for‑profit?" — **"They would not."** _(5/4/2026 Testimony @ ~12:22 PT)_
