# Day 3 — Musk v. Altman / OpenAI — April 29, 2026

Volume 3, pages 386–644. Before Hon. Yvonne Gonzalez Rogers, Northern District of California, Oakland Division (4:24-CV-04722 YGR). Source: `042926TT.txt`.

Witness: ELON REEVE MUSK (continued). Direct examination resumed by Mr. Molo (MoloLamken). Cross-examination by Mr. Savitt (Wachtell, Lipton, Rosen & Katz, for OpenAI defendants).

* * *

## 1\. Summary

Day 3 was dominated by Elon Musk on the witness stand. The morning began with about an hour of housekeeping — stipulated exhibit admissions on both sides, a denial of OpenAI's request to defer plaintiffs' deposition video clips before Brockman's live testimony, and the Court chastising a spectator (Dr. Belitski) for taking photos in the courthouse, leading later in the day to an order that the Marshals confiscate any device used to record. Musk's resumed direct (Mr. Molo) walked through the 2017 negotiations over a possible OpenAI for‑profit, the September 2017 cap‑table proposals, his "guys, I've had enough — this is the final straw" email, his February 2018 board departure, the 2019 capped‑profit launch, and the "bait and switch" 2022 text exchange culminating in the Alex Spiro investigation. Musk repeatedly framed the case in three phases: full confidence, growing doubts, and "phase three — they stole the charity." After the morning recess, Mr. Savitt began a methodical cross that occupied the rest of the day, repeatedly impeaching Musk with his own deposition (taken Sept. 26, 2025), his contemporaneous emails and texts (DX 507, 559, 631, 642, 679, 686, 691, 749, 761, 773, 806, 819, 827, 844, 853, 869, 1025, 1444), and stipulated facts. Key cross themes: that Musk's $1B pledge produced only ~$38M in cash; that Musk himself first proposed a "C‑corp with parallel non‑profit," later wrote that nonprofit status "in hindsight, might have been the wrong move," demanded "unequivocal initial control," authorized Neuralink to poach OpenAI staff while still on OpenAI's board, decided to build the Google counterweight at Tesla after concluding OpenAI had "zero percent" probability, and that Tesla and xAI are themselves pursuing AGI. The Court repeatedly admonished both sides — sustaining Mr. Savitt's "leading" objections during direct, ordering Musk to actually answer yes/no questions on cross, cutting off discussion of grok / xAI safety practices and Trump administration AI policy under FRE 403, and warning OpenAI counsel about judicial estoppel concerning their position vis‑à‑vis the Ravine litigation. The day ended with the Court ordering 5‑page briefs by noon Thursday on whether Musk's contributions were a "solicitation" under Cal. Bus. & Prof. Code § 17510.6 — a charitable‑trust theory the plaintiffs are pursuing in the alternative.

## 2\. Musk direct examination (continued) highlights

The resumed direct ran from approximately line 924 through line 2556. Mr. Molo's themes: (a) the founding promise of a 501(c)(3); (b) Musk's good relationships with the engineers; (c) the 2017 cap-table dispute and Musk's "final straw" email; (d) his good-faith reliance on Altman's reassurances; (e) the 2019 capped-profit launch as acceptable; (f) the 2022 "bait and switch" realization.

- "If you go as a nonprofit, you have the moral high ground… But what you can't do is have your cake and eat it, too. You can't say it's going to be a nonprofit and have all of the, sort of clear associations of a nonprofit, but then turn it into a for-profit. That's what's messed up." (042926TT.txt:929–950)

- "I deliberately chose to create this as a nonprofit for the public good." (042926TT.txt:1054)

- "Everyone was in complete agreement that OpenAI would be an independent pure play 501(c)(3). It could have an adjunct for-profit where the profits would flow to the nonprofit. But fundamentally it would be a charity." (042926TT.txt:1024–1027)

- "All public and private communication was consistent with being a 501(c)(3) nonprofit… in podcast meeting interviews, press releases, internally and externally there was complete consistency on this matter." (042926TT.txt:1035–1040)

- On Sutskever's Jan. 1, 2018 email calling him "the most overwhelmingly competent person in the world": "I must say the feeling is reciprocal. So it was certainly heartwarming to receive this email… when you have someone like Ilya who is one of the greatest AI computer scientists in the world, he can work anywhere he wants, anywhere. So it's an honor that he would work at OpenAI." (042926TT.txt:1104–1110)

- Pushed on whether he ever called an OpenAI employee a "jackass": "It's possible I may have done that on one occasion, but it would not be in anger. It would be, you know, along the lines of, don't be a jackass… I don't lose my temper. I'm not like a, you know, yeller. I don't yell at people, basically." (042926TT.txt:1160–1164)

- On the for-profit subsidiary concept: "What you can't have is the for-profit becomes the main event, and that's the issue we've got really at stake here." (042926TT.txt:1191–1192)

- On his September 2017 email asking for "unequivocal" initial control: "I would have initially 51 percent of the company, but as soon as we raised additional funding, that would dilute my percentage below 51 percent and I would not have control of the company within a year or two." (042926TT.txt:1365–1368)

- Reading PX 158, his Sept. 20, 2017 reply ("Guys, I've had enough. This is the final straw"): "What I meant by that is, if they want to have even more stock in the company than what was initially proposed, basically, if they want to get rich, they should go do so as a for-profit. But what they should not do is have me continue to fund a nonprofit and then get rich off of that. They should not get rich off a nonprofit, that's not right." (042926TT.txt:1585–1591)

- "Well, I actually was a fool who provided free funding for them to create a startup. I literally was… I gave them, you know, $38 million of essentially free funding, which they then used to create an $800 billion for-profit company." (042926TT.txt:1599–1607)

- On Altman's "I remain enthusiastic about the nonprofit structure" email (PX 158): "I understood that what Sam Altman was saying is that OpenAI would remain a nonprofit. And I believed him… So I was foolish enough to believe him." (042926TT.txt:1636–1647)

- On leaving the OpenAI board in February 2018: "My obligations to SpaceX and Tesla were too great. I could not afford to even be on board meetings because I had to make sure especially that Tesla survived." (042926TT.txt:1741–1743)

- On Larry Page calling him "a speciesist": "I was worried about Google's approach to safety because of my conversations with Larry Page, where he called me a speciesist for being pro human. So I felt that they were not being sufficiently caring about the future of humanity." (042926TT.txt:1768–1771)

- On the three phases (a recurring frame Musk would return to repeatedly on cross): "I would just divide this into three phases. Essentially, there was one where I was enthusiastically supported, phase one; phase two where I was a little uncertain; and then phase three where I'm sure they're looting the nonprofit. We're currently in phase three." (042926TT.txt:2133–2142)

- On the September 2020 "OpenAI is essentially captured by Microsoft" tweet and the defense's portrayal of his reaction: "Well, like I said, I tried to be quite literal. It does seem like the opposite of open, and an exclusive license sounds — well, it sounds like an exclusive license. But Sam Altman immediately reached out to reassure me that OpenAI was on its — was staying on mission as a nonprofit." (042926TT.txt:2194–2198)

- On the defense having displayed yesterday his 2020 Altman text without his contemporaneous tweet: "No. They were trying to trick the jury." (042926TT.txt:2246)

- On the October 2022 "bait and switch" text (PX 296): "Like, a nonprofit doesn't have a valuation. So something is wrong here if you've got OpenAI as somehow a for-profit with a $20 billion valuation, that means the for-profit is now the tail wagging the dog… and that — this is when I thought there had been a breach of charitable trust." (042926TT.txt:2315–2321)

- On Altman's offer of equity in response: "Well, the reason I didn't accept the equity or the stock in OpenAI was, I don't understand how you can have stock in a nonprofit. So it just didn't seem to make sense to me. Yeah, so I mean, frankly, it felt like a bribe." (042926TT.txt:2381–2385)

- Closing the direct, on why suit was not brought earlier (the "stolen car" analogy): "In 2018, they had not, at that point, breached the charitable trust. I would say it is comparable to thinking that someone might steal your car is not the same as if someone has stolen your car… And I concluded that the car had indeed been stolen, meaning the nonprofit had been looted, they stole a charity, was my conclusion, in 2023." (042926TT.txt:2523–2547)

- Final clarification (added unsolicited): "I would have filed a lawsuit sooner if I thought they had stolen a charity sooner." (042926TT.txt:2552–2553)

## 3\. Musk cross-examination highlights

Cross began line 2689. Mr. Savitt's arc: (1) Tesla is in fact pursuing AGI; (2) the $1B promise produced only $38M; (3) Musk himself first proposed a C‑corp / parallel nonprofit, then thought nonprofit status "in hindsight" was the wrong move; (4) Musk demanded majority control of any for‑profit; (5) Musk cut off funding when his control demand failed; (6) Musk poached OpenAI staff for Tesla and authorized Neuralink to do the same while sitting on OpenAI's board; (7) Musk gave OpenAI "zero percent" odds and decided to build the Google counterweight at Tesla; (8) Musk was "OK by me" on the capped‑profit launch and never objected to the structure in writing; (9) Musk doesn't actually know OpenAI's safety practices; (10) Musk's 2017–2020 contemporaneous statements show he believed the deal was broken long before 2023.

### Tesla and AGI (the opening salvo, DX 1444)

- Q. "Tesla is also seeking to develop an enormous AI-enabled robot army, isn't it?"

A. "Not an army in, like, a military sense." Q. "Do you recall telling analysts that Tesla was going to be building an enormous AI‑enabled robot army?"

A. "Yeah. Not in the military." (042926TT.txt:4809–4816)

- Q. "And in this tweet \[March 4, 2026, DX 1444\] you wrote, 'Tesla will be one of the companies to make AGI and probably the first to make it in humanoid/atom-shaping form.'… So Tesla is one of the companies that's making AGI; correct?"

A. "No. I said 'will be' in the future, one of the companies." (042926TT.txt:2754–2763)

- On the Tesla compensation package and AI control: Q. (reading from analyst call) "'…my fundamental concern with regard to how much voting control I have in Tesla is, if I go ahead and build this enormous robot army, can I just be ousted at some point in the future?' Did you say that?"

A. "I think I did say something to that effect." (042926TT.txt:4961–4972)
