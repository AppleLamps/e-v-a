# Key Themes

The narratives both sides keep returning to. Each section links to the underlying quotes in the [daily digests](https://trial.mts.now/wiki/timeline).

## Plaintiffs' framing

### "Stole a charity"

Plaintiffs' single most-repeated line. Musk used it three times in cross alone. `(043026TT.txt:1270, 1290, 1736)`

- Molo opening: "We're here today because the defendants in this case **stole a charity**, and we're going to ask you to hold them accountable." `(042826TT.txt:1284)`
- Musk on direct: "If the verdict comes up that effectively makes it OK to loot a charity, the entire foundation of charitable giving in America will be destroyed. That's my concern." `(042826TT.txt:4204)`
- The **museum-store analogy**: "The museum store can't loot the museum, sell the Picassos, and pocket the profits. It's got to fund the nonprofit's issue." `(042826TT.txt:1594)`
- Musk in cross, after being asked why he hadn't started his own AI nonprofit in eight years: "Well, I thought I had started a nonprofit with OpenAI, but it turns out **they stole the charity** and turned it into a for-profit." `(043026TT.txt:2031)`
- Musk's "stolen car" timing analogy on direct: "Thinking that someone might steal your car is not the same as if someone has stolen your car... I concluded that the car had indeed been stolen, meaning the nonprofit had been looted, they stole a charity, was my conclusion, in 2023." `(042926TT.txt:2523)`

### Musk's "three phases"

Musk's organizing narrative for the timeline of his disillusionment — and his answer to the statute-of-limitations defense.

- "Phase one — enthusiastically supportive. Phase two — a little uncertain. Phase three — they're looting the nonprofit. We're currently in phase three." `(042926TT.txt:2133)`
- Cross: "When was phase two?" "I started feeling uncomfortable around 2017, 2018, and then that progressed to extreme discomfort in late '22 and '23." `(042926TT.txt:7148)`
- Recross attempt to pin a 2020 break-the-deal date: Musk: "No. That's because I became uncomfortable in 2020." `(043026TT.txt:3594)`

### "I was a fool"

Musk's repeated framing of his own role.

- "Well, I actually was a **fool** who provided free funding for them to create a startup. I literally was... I gave them, you know, $38 million of essentially free funding, which they then used to create an $800 billion for-profit company." `(042926TT.txt:1599)`
- "I understood that what Sam Altman was saying is that OpenAI would remain a nonprofit. And I believed him... So I was **foolish enough** to believe him." `(042926TT.txt:1636)`

### Greg Brockman's contributions ("zero")

A shorthand plaintiffs use — and Birchall confirmed on direct — to frame the equity dispute as a money question.

- Birchall: "Q. How much did Mr. Brockman contribute?

A. Zero." `(043026TT.txt:4416)`
- Plaintiffs' theory: those who contributed nothing now own equity in an $800B+ for-profit. Musk, who contributed everything, owns nothing.

## Defense's framing

### "Pageant of hypocrisy" / Tale of two Elons

Defense's framing.

- Savitt opening: "Mr. Musk's lawsuit is a **pageant of hypocrisy**." `(042826TT.txt:2177)`
- "What he cares about is Elon Musk being on top, and that's why we're here, members of the jury." `(042826TT.txt:2268)`
- "When OpenAI refused to be absorbed into Musk's empire, he just **picked up his marbles and went home** declaring that OpenAI's chances of success were exactly zero." `(042826TT.txt:2222)`
- "The evidence... could be summarized as a **tale of two Elons**." `(042826TT.txt:3101)`
- Musk dismissed OpenAI safety staff as " **jackasses**" at an all-hands. `(042826TT.txt:2674)`

### The $1B / $38M gap

The single most-repeated factual attack of the cross.

- Savitt opening: "As to his billion-dollar pledge, **Mr. Musk fell $960 million short**. He actually contributed less than 4 percent of what he promised." `(042826TT.txt:2736)`
- Musk in cross: "In strict monetary terms, I contributed 38 million." `(042926TT.txt:2898)`
- DX 1025 — Musk's March 15, 2023 tweet "$100 million to OpenAI"; under deposition: "I was mistaken." `(042926TT.txt:3030)`
- Birchall confirms the precise number on direct: **$38,191,066** in ~60 contributions (PX 112A). `(043026TT.txt:4291)`
- Birchall: For 2016–2017, Musk supplied "approximately 60 percent" of OpenAI's contributions; Altman "less than 10 percent"; Brockman "zero." `(043026TT.txt:4408–4416)`

### "Unequivocal control" (the 2017 negotiation breakdown)

Defense's pivot from "Musk-was-betrayed" to "Musk-wanted-control."

- Musk's own September 2017 emails (PX 156, DX 686): "I would have initially 51 percent of the company..." `(042926TT.txt:1365)`
- Cross: "So the idea is, you would have **unequivocal control** of this for-profit when it starts; right?" "Yes." `(042926TT.txt:3763)`
- The **"final straw" email** (PX 157, Sept. 20, 2017) — the climactic moment of plaintiffs' direct AND of defense's cross:

> Musk: "Guys, I've had enough. This is the final straw."

- Brockman/Altman reply (per Savitt opening): "you've shown to us that absolute control is extremely important to you." `(042826TT.txt slide deck reference)`
- Altman's "I remain enthusiastic about the nonprofit structure" (PX 158, Sept. 21, 2017) — Musk: "I was foolish enough to believe him." `(042926TT.txt:1647)`

### Tesla / xAI as Musk's own AGI play (the parallel)

The single most damaging line of cross.

- Musk's " **OpenAI to attach to Tesla as its cash cow**" (DX 749, Karpathy reply Musk endorsed). `(042926TT.txt:4762)`
- Musk to Gabe Newell, Nov. 2018 (DX 844): "I lost confidence that OpenAI could muster the resources to serve as an effective counterweight to Google/DeepMind and decided to **attempt that through Tesla instead**." `(042926TT.txt:5170)`
- Musk to Zilis, Feb. 2018 (DX 761): "Close and friendly, but we are going to **actively try to move three or four people from OpenAI to Tesla**." `(042926TT.txt:5232)`
- Musk to Altman/Brockman/Sutskever/Zilis, Dec. 31, 2018 (DX 853): "My probability assessment of OpenAI being relevant to DeepMind/Google without a dramatic change in execution and resources is **zero percent: not 1 percent**." `(042926TT.txt:5371)`
- Musk's March 2026 tweet (DX 1444): " **Tesla will be one of the companies to make AGI** and probably the first to make it in humanoid/atom-shaping form." `(042926TT.txt:2754)`
- Cross on xAI:

> Q. "If xAI achieves its goal of reaching AGI, you will control that AGI; right?"
>
> A. "Well, I think we're not tracking currently to reach AGI before many other companies." `(042926TT.txt:5111)`
>
> Q. "When xAI achieves AGI, you will control it, yes or no?"
>
> A. "I don't know."
>
> Q. "You control it now; right?"
>
> A. "Yes." `(042926TT.txt:5121)`

- Cross's punchline: "But you haven't created an AI nonprofit in those eight years, have you, Mr. Musk?" — A. "No." `(043026TT.txt:2023)`
- Musk concedes xAI "doesn't have any profit caps for its investors." `(043026TT.txt:1997)`
- Musk concedes xAI "started off as a B-corp and then became a C-corp" — and admits "B-corp and a C-corp are pretty much the same thing." `(043026TT.txt:1838)`

### The 2018 "purple box" term sheet

Defense's cleanest "Musk knew, Musk consented" exhibit.

- DX 827 — the OpenAI LP Summary Term Sheet (Aug. 31, 2018): includes a header in a **purple box** warning investors to view the investment "in the spirit of a donation" because there are **no profits expected**.
- Defense theory: this is exactly the structure Musk now claims violated his trust. He didn't sue.
- Musk's response on cross: "I did not look closely at the term sheet... If you see something that says 'in the spirit of a donation,' what do you expect? You expect that the fine print would contradict that?" `(042926TT.txt:6019)`
- Birchall to Zilis on the same term sheet (DX 828): "Pretty plain vanilla for-profit structure. So kind of hard to push a narrative that doesn't involve investors being very focused on ROI." `(043026TT.txt:6682)`
- Birchall's deposition reading: "There was a lot of **posturing and virtue signalling** about it not being an actual for-profit structure." `(043026TT.txt:6777)`

Savitt to Birchall: "You're referring to the **infamous purple box**; is that right, sir?" — Birchall: "I don't know what an 'infamous' purple box is." `(043026TT.txt:6700)`

### The "donor-advised funds" / tax-deduction wrinkle

A defense soft spot: Musk took a tax deduction every step of the way.

- Birchall on cross (Wilson): "For every single one of those donations, it's somewhere in the chain, Mr. Musk was entitled to take a tax deduction?" — A. "Yes, that's the nature of philanthropic giving." `(043026TT.txt:5413)`
- Birchall conceded he is CFO of the Musk Foundation but said, "I'm not familiar with all of the **legalities of donor advised funds**." `(043026TT.txt:5007)` — Wilson: "So you're the CFO of the Musk Foundation, and you can't answer that question?" — A. "I just answered it." `(043026TT.txt:5039)`
- The legal point: once Musk transferred to a DAF, **he could not take the money back** — he could only direct it. This implicates whether he has standing to seek "restitution" for it personally.

## Mission and meaning

### AGI safety as the original purpose

The reason Musk says he funded OpenAI in the first place.

- The **Larry Page "speciesist"** anecdote — Musk's most-repeated story:

> "I said, 'Well, what if AI wipes out all the humans?' And he said, 'That will be fine as long as artificial intelligence survives.' And I'm, like, well, that's — that's insane." `(042826TT.txt:4828)`
>
> "When he called me a speciesist in front of a dozen people for being pro-human, I was like, this is nuts. We better have some kind of counterpoint to Google." `(042826TT.txt:4844)`

- "We don't want to have a Terminator outcome. I think we want to be in like a Gene Roddenberry movie like 'Star Trek.'" `(042826TT.txt:4870)`
- "My guess is that AI will be probably smarter than any human as soon as next year." `(042826TT.txt:4764)`
- "The biggest risk would be that **AI kills us all**." `(042926TT.txt:6936)`

But the court has bracketed safety:

- " **This is not a trial on the safety risks of artificial intelligence.** ... The question here is whether there was a breach of charitable trust." `(043026TT.txt:817)`
- All **extinction** testimony excluded from plaintiffs' AI expert. `(043026TT.txt:712)`
- "I suspect that there are plenty of people who **don't want to put the future of humanity in Mr. Musk's hands**, but it doesn't matter. We aren't going to get into those issues." `(043026TT.txt:803)`

### The "OPEN" in OpenAI

A live trademark / mission-statement fight. Plaintiffs say "open" meant "open source"; defense said in opening that it didn't.

- Musk on direct: " **The 'OPEN' in OpenAI represents open source.**" `(042826TT.txt:5163)`
- Judge's pointed admonition to OpenAI counsel after openings (jury out): "You seem to suggest that the name OpenAI did not refer to open source, and I would suggest that perhaps you talk to Quinn Emanuel, and I suggest that perhaps you look at the **prosecution history in front of the PTO**... and you do not take inconsistent positions in front of them." `(042826TT.txt:6296)`
- Musk to Savitt on cross: "Without me, OpenAI wouldn't exist. I came up with the name, the idea for the company. I came up with the name, which means open source, **which you, I think, falsely said yesterday it didn't mean open source**. Ironically in this very courtroom, OpenAI argued that OpenAI meant open source." `(042926TT.txt:2873)`

## The Microsoft thread

### "Captured by Microsoft" (statute-of-limitations fight)

Defense's core SOL evidence.

- Musk's **Sept. 24, 2020 tweet** (PX 251 / DX evidence): "This does seem like the opposite of open. **OpenAI is essentially captured by Microsoft.**" `(042826TT.txt slide / 042926TT.txt:2154)`
- Cohen opening: "Mr. Musk... by September of 2020, he is tweeting that OpenAI is controlled by Microsoft." `(042826TT.txt:3855)`
- Musk's answer: "Sam Altman immediately reached out to reassure me that OpenAI was on its — was staying on mission as a nonprofit." `(042926TT.txt:2194)`
- Microsoft cross of Musk: "And you didn't come to court in 2020, did you?" — A. "No, because he assured me that they were staying on mission..." `(043026TT.txt:2438)`
- Musk on Nadella: "The only reason he's in this thing is because of me. Those are his words." `(042826TT.txt:5544)`

### The November 2023 Altman firing & Microsoft's reaction

A motif Musk returns to as evidence Microsoft "captured" OpenAI.

- "Microsoft's reaction to Altman being fired was to **attempt to higher \[hire\] everyone at OpenAI**, including Altman." `(042926TT.txt:2515)`
- Helen Toner and Tasha McCauley — former OpenAI directors who voted to fire Altman — will testify by video deposition.

## Evidence battles

### The February 2025 $97.4B xAI bid for OpenAI's assets

The fight that consumed the end of Day 4 and all of Day 5.

- DX 1156 — Musk to Zuckerberg, Feb. 3, 2025: "Are you open to the idea of bidding on the OpenAI IP with me and some others?" `(043026TT.txt:1645)`
- DX 1157 — Toberoff letter to OpenAI board, Feb. 10, 2025 — the $97B bid signed by Musk. `(043026TT.txt:1677)`
- Musk's framing on cross: "Essentially, they were trying to steal a charity and we were trying to stop them." `(043026TT.txt:1745)`

> "It would have been an offer to pay the charity a large amount of money for the assets." `(043026TT.txt:1749)`

- But also: "And you and your fellow investors weren't proposing to spend $97 billion to buy the assets and to **make them a nonprofit and open sourced**, were you?" — A. "No." `(043026TT.txt:1768)`
- Birchall on direct testified the bid was made because "Sam Altman was on both sides of that negotiation." `(043026TT.txt:4718)`
- Defense established Birchall had no first-hand knowledge for that claim.
- The court told Birchall: "I'm still struggling with how you can have conversations with these individuals to raise $97.5 billion, and you have no recollection of the content of those conversations, even in a general sense." `(043026TT.txt:7741)`
- And: "So it was **purely a business deal?**" — Witness: "Yes." `(043026TT.txt:7882)` — collapsing the privilege foundation.
- **Day 5 result: testimony struck.** "Given that this gentleman had no personal knowledge, I'm striking the testimony." `(050126TT.txt:339)`

### The Pioneer Building

The San Francisco office Musk leased for OpenAI — and the central piece of evidence for plaintiffs' alternative § 17510.6 charitable-solicitation theory.

- Lessee under the master lease: **Musk Industries LLC** (a passthrough vehicle), not OpenAI. `(043026TT.txt:4078)`
- DX 539, June 2016 Musk to Birchall: "I don't want Sam on the lease." `(043026TT.txt:5787)`
- DX 600, Birchall to Sam Teller, Jan. 2017: "Elon holds **100 percent of the power and authority** regarding the building." `(043026TT.txt:5829)`
- Neuralink shared the building rent-free until OpenAI's auditors raised an issue (DX 849, "to pass nonprofit audits"). `(043026TT.txt:6381)`
- DX 857, Jan. 2019: OpenAI initially asked $3.7M reimbursement for 2017–2018; Musk countered $1.25M ("approximately two-thirds cut"). `(043026TT.txt:6452)`
- PX 103, July 2020 Clark email: OpenAI for-profit "happy to cover 100 percent of the rent." This is the email plaintiffs use as the alleged 2020 "solicitation" that would defeat the statute of limitations.

## From the bench

### The judge's framing — what this case actually is

- "This is not a trial on the safety risks of artificial intelligence... The question here is whether there was a breach of charitable trust." `(043026TT.txt:817)`
- "There is no separate cause of action for unjust enrichment. The only thing you find in California law is **a claim of restitution under a theory of unjust enrichment**." `(050126TT.txt:1473)`
- "Mr. Musk's notion that somehow he wants something different, I don't know that he can do that because it's a claim for restitution. And the elements of restitution say that **it goes back to the plaintiff**." `(050126TT.txt:1446)`
- The unspoken implication: even if plaintiffs win on liability, the $38M may go back to Musk personally — not to a "restored" charity.
