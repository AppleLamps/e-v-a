 1          Pursuant to Section 2(a)(i) of the Court’s Standing Order re: Pretrial Instructions in Civil

 2 Cases, the OpenAI Defendants submit the following statement of the elements of proof and

 3 summary of anticipated evidence supporting their defenses to Plaintiff’s claims.

 4 I.       FIRST AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE: STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS

 5          A.      Elements of Proof

 6          Breach of Charitable Trust

 7          To establish this affirmative defense, Altman, Brockman, and OpenAI, Inc. must prove by a

 8 preponderance of the evidence that Plaintiff’s claimed harm occurred before August 5, 2021. If

 9 they meet that burden, the claim is timely only if Plaintiff proves by a preponderance of the evidence

10 that before that date he did not discover, and did not know of facts that would have caused a

11 reasonable person to suspect, that he had suffered the claimed harm, and with reasonable diligence

12 could not have discovered those facts.

13          Unjust Enrichment

14          To establish this affirmative defense, the OpenAI Defendants must prove by a preponderance

15 of the evidence that Plaintiff’s claimed harm occurred before August 5, 2022. If the OpenAI

16 Defendants meet that burden, the claim is timely only if Plaintiff proves by a preponderance of the

17 evidence that before that date he did not discover, and did not know of facts that would have caused

18 a reasonable person to suspect, the claimed harm, and with reasonable diligence could not have

19 discovered those facts.

20          Fraud

21          To establish this affirmative defense, Altman, Brockman, and OpenAI, Inc. must prove by a

22 preponderance of the evidence that Plaintiff’s claimed harm occurred before August 5, 2021. If

23 they meet that burden, the claim is timely only if Plaintiff proves by a preponderance of the evidence

24 that before that date he did not discover facts that would have caused a reasonable person to suspect

25 the claimed fraud, and with reasonable diligence could not have discovered those facts.

26          Constructive Fraud

27          To establish this affirmative defense, Altman, Brockman, and OpenAI, Inc. must prove by a

28 preponderance of the evidence that Plaintiff’s claimed harm occurred before August 5, 2021. If

 4          B.     Summary of Anticipated Evidence

 5          The OpenAI Defendants intend to prove, first, that the contributions grounding Plaintiff’s

 6 claims were received and expended well before August 5, 2021. The evidence supporting this

 7 showing will include tax and donation records; contemporaneous communications about the

 8 contributions; testimony from Plaintiff and from his agent and representative, Jared Birchall; and

 9 testimony from expert witness Louis G. Dudney, who will explain that, except for rent-related

10 contributions that continued through September 2020, the subject contributions were used to fund

11 OpenAI, Inc.’s operating expenses and were no longer available to fund those expenses after late

12 November 2017. The OpenAI Defendants’ evidentiary showing on this point may also include

13 testimony from OpenAI witnesses familiar with the contributions, including Altman and Chris

14 Clark.

15          The OpenAI Defendants further intend to prove that, before August 5, 2021, Plaintiff was

16 aware of, or reasonably should have suspected, the conduct he now complains constituted misuse

17 of his contributions—including the creation of an OpenAI for-profit enterprise, related asset

18 contributions, commercial partnership arrangements, and publication practices. The documentary

19 evidence supporting this showing will include: (i) contemporaneous communications among

20 Plaintiff, his agents and representatives, and the OpenAI Defendants regarding the 2018 creation of

21 OpenAI’s for-profit structure, related fundraising, and OpenAI’s posture toward open-sourcing;

22 (ii) publicly available materials describing the creation of OpenAI L.P., Microsoft’s investments,

23 related governance and operational changes, and OpenAI’s open-sourcing practices; and

24 (iii) Plaintiff’s reactions to those materials on social media and otherwise. Testimony that

25 information about changes to OpenAI’s structure and Microsoft’s investments was shared with

26 Plaintiff in 2018 and thereafter will be offered from Altman, Brockman, Plaintiff, Birchall, Sam

27 Teller, and Shivon Zilis.

 1 II.      SECOND AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE: LACHES

 2          A.     Elements of Proof

 3          To establish this affirmative defense, the OpenAI Defendants must prove by a preponderance

 4 of the evidence (i) that Plaintiff failed to bring this lawsuit for a period of time that amounts to an

 5 unreasonable delay and (ii) that Plaintiff’s delay resulted in prejudice to the OpenAI Defendants.

 6          B.     Summary of Anticipated Evidence

 7          To prove that Plaintiff’s delay in bringing this action was unreasonable, the OpenAI

 8 Defendants intend to offer the same evidence summarized above with respect to the statute of

 9 limitations defense.

10          To prove substantial prejudice, the OpenAI Defendants intend to offer evidence that they

11 and third parties relied on the elements of OpenAI’s organizational structure and commercial

12 partnerships about which Plaintiff now complains in making substantial operational, investment,

13 and commercial decisions; that the OpenAI Defendants’ ability to defend against Plaintiff’s claims

14 has been impaired by the passage of time; and that the delay has increased the burdens associated

15 with the relief Plaintiff now seeks. The OpenAI Defendants intend to make this showing principally

16 through the testimony of Altman and OpenAI director Bret Taylor, as well as through testimony of

17 Microsoft CEO Satya Nadella.

18 III.     THIRD AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE: UNCLEAN HANDS

19          A.     Elements of Proof

20          To establish this affirmative defense, the OpenAI Defendants must prove by a preponderance

21 of the evidence (1) that Plaintiff engaged in conduct that was not in good faith; (2) that the conduct

22 was directly related to his claims; and (3) that the conduct resulted in prejudice to the OpenAI

23 Defendants.

24          B.     Summary of Anticipated Evidence

25          To prove that Plaintiff engaged in inequitable conduct directly related to the claims and

26 equitable relief he seeks in this action, the OpenAI Defendants will offer contemporaneous

27 communications and other documents showing that, while still affiliated with and owing fiduciary

28 duties to OpenAI, Inc., and during the period of his claimed contributions to OpenAI, Inc., Plaintiff

24 Defendants’ proposed jury instructions for the governing elements and standards applicable to each

25 claim.

 5          B.      Unjust Enrichment

 6          Plaintiff cannot carry his burden of proof with respect to his unjust enrichment claim: No

 7 OpenAI Defendant other than OpenAI, Inc. received a benefit from the contributions that ground

 8 Plaintiff’s claim, and no OpenAI Defendant unjustly retained any benefit at Plaintiff’s expense. As

 9 to unjust retention, Plaintiff cannot establish the quasi-contract predicate for this element; he cannot

10 show any implied agreement with the OpenAI Defendants, any noncompliance with any such

11 agreement, or any OpenAI Defendant’s knowledge of such an agreement or noncompliance with it.

12          To show that the implied agreement Plaintiff alleges never existed, the OpenAI Defendants

13 will offer communications and other documents from the period when Plaintiff claims the agreement

14 was formed, as well as later communications and documents involving Plaintiff and his agents and

15 representatives that demonstrate the absence of the alleged agreement. The OpenAI Defendants

16 will also offer witness testimony on this point, principally from Altman, Brockman, Sutskever,

17 Plaintiff, Birchall, Teller, and Zilis.

18          To demonstrate that there was no breach of any agreement with Plaintiff, the OpenAI

19 Defendants intend to offer the same evidence, summarized above, that demonstrates there was no

20 breach of charitable trust.

21          C.      Fraud

22          Plaintiff cannot carry his burden of proof with respect to his fraud claim: No statement on

23 which his claim relies constituted a promise (as opposed to a non-actionable expression of opinion,

24 aspiration, or feeling); none of Altman, Brockman, or OpenAI, Inc. lacked intent to perform at the

25 time of any alleged promise or intended for Plaintiff to rely on any alleged promise; Plaintiff neither

26 actually relied nor reasonably relied on any alleged promise; no defendant named in this claim failed

27 to perform any act allegedly promised; Plaintiff suffered no harm; and Plaintiff cannot establish that

28 his alleged reliance on any alleged promise was a substantial factor in causing his alleged harm.
