19                                                ANALYSIS
20           Under the Federal Rules of Evidence, a district court may take judicial notice of matters
21   that are either (1) “generally known within the trial court’s territorial jurisdiction” or (2) capable of
22   accurate and ready determination by resort to “sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be
23   questioned.” Fed. R. Evid. 201(b)(1)-(2).
24           The Court takes judicial notice of the complaint, motion to strike, and opposition to
25   demurrer filed in Musk v. Altman (Exhibits A, B, and C), as they are publicly available court filings
26   in a related legal proceeding. It is well established that courts “may take judicial notice of court
27   filings” in other actions. See, e.g., Reyn’s Pasta Bella, LLC v. Visa USA, Inc., 442 F.3d 741, 746
28   n.6 (9th Cir. 2006) (taking judicial notice of briefs, a hearing transcript, and “several other

 6          The Court also takes judicial notice of the webpage titled OpenAI and Elon Musk (Ex. D)

 8   sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned.” Fed. R. Evid. 201(b)(2). Publicly

 9   accessible websites are “[p]roper subjects of judicial notice when ruling on a motion to dismiss.”

10   Perkins v. LinkedIn Corp., 53 F. Supp. 3d 1190, 1204 (N.D. Cal. 2014). The existence and

11   authenticity of this publicly accessible webpage cannot reasonably be disputed, and the Court thus

12   takes judicial notice of it in ruling on the OpenAI Defendants’ Motion to Dismiss.
