 1   I.     Introduction
 2          1.      Plaintiff Elon Musk asserts claims against defendants Sam Altman, Greg Brockman,
 3   and OpenAI, Inc. (the OpenAI nonprofit) for breach of charitable trust. 1 Musk also asserts claims
 4   against those defendants and the OpenAI for-profit enterprise for unjust enrichment.
 5          2.      Musk’s claims are all predicated on claimed charitable contributions to the OpenAI
 6   nonprofit from 2016 to 2020 and alleged breaches of restrictions he claims to have imposed on
 7   those donations. After a [*]-day trial in which the Court and an advisory jury heard from [*]
 8   witnesses, the Court finds the following facts, which do not support Musk’s claims and show that
 9   those claims are in any event barred by statutes of limitations.
10   II.    Breach of Charitable Trust
11          3.      Musk alleges that his approximately $37.8 million in donations to OpenAI, Inc.
12   formed a charitable trust requiring OpenAI, Inc. to remain a nonprofit and open-source organization
13   forever. The evidence adduced at trial does not establish either that Musk manifested an intent to
14   impose those restrictions, or that OpenAI, Inc. or Musk’s co-founders accepted them. The evidence
15   instead shows that Musk was at best indifferent to the nonprofit structure of OpenAI and that
16   OpenAI’s founders, including Musk, never intended to open-source all of OpenAI’s technology,
17   let alone in perpetuity. Section II.A, infra. Nor does the evidence establish that any of the OpenAI
18   Defendants breached any of Musk’s alleged restrictions, even if they existed. Section II.B, infra.

 1          B.      Even If a Trust Were Created, There Was No Breach
 2                  i.      Musk’s contributions were spent consistent with his intentions.
 3          28.     The record shows that all of Musk’s contributions were spent on expenses consistent

 4   with Musk’s intentions, with Musk’s quarterly general-purpose donations having been fully spent

 5   by November 2017 and Musk’s monthly rent-related donations having been fully spent by

 6   September 2020.

 7          29.     OpenAI’s expert, Louis Dudney, performed a financial tracing exercise to assess

 8   when Musk’s financial contributions were fully spent.

 9          30.     Starting with the $25 million in quarterly $5 million donations, Dudney used what

10   is commonly referred to as the First In, First Out (“FIFO”) tracing method. The FIFO method is a

11   systematic approach to attribute outflows of funds assuming that funds deposited into the account

12   are spent in the order they were received. Dudney testified that the FIFO method is appropriate in

13   this case given the availability of detailed, chronological cash records and the character of OpenAI,

14   Inc.’s cash flows in the relevant time periods.

15          31.     Dudney determined that, applying the FIFO method, Musk’s quarterly $5 million

16   donations were fully spent as of November 2017. He also determined that the funds were spent in

17   support of the functional expenses of OpenAI, Inc., including to purchase compute and for

18   employee wages and benefits—the intended uses for Musk’s general-support donations.

19          32.     Neither Musk nor anyone on his team complained about how the funds were being

20   spent. The funds were spent before OpenAI created a for-profit entity and before any of the conduct

21   that Musk now alleges violated a charitable trust.

22          33.     Dudney separately analyzed the $12.5 million in rent contributions. He explained

23   that this approach is appropriate because he was able to separately identify the rent-related

24   donations and specifically tie them to rent-related expenditures. Dudney testified that Musk’s rent

25   contributions were spent as of September 2020, and that they were spent on rent and related costs.

26                  ii.     OpenAI, Inc. remains a nonprofit devoted to its original mission.
