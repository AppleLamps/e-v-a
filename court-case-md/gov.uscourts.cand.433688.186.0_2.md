10                                               ARGUMENT

11     I.   OPENAI’S AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSES FAIL THE TWOMBLY/IQBAL STANDARD.

12          “An affirmative defense is an assertion raising new facts and arguments that, if true, will

13 defeat plaintiff’s claim, even if all allegations in [the] complaint are true.” Winns v. Exela Enter.
14 Sols., Inc., No. 4:20-CV-06762-YGR, 2021 WL 5632587, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Dec. 1, 2021) (quoting
15 Bay Area Roofers Health & Welfare Tr. v. Sun Life Assurance Co. of Can., No. 13-CV-04192-WHO,
16 2013 WL 6700017 at *2 (N.D. Cal. Dec. 19, 2013)). “In pleading affirmative defenses, a defendant
17 must allege sufficient facts to ‘nudge[ ] their [legal] claims across the line from conceivable to
18 plausible.’” Id. (citing and quoting Twombly, 550 U.S. at 570; Barnes v. AT & T Pension Ben. Plan-
19 Nonbargained Program, 718 F. Supp. 2d 1167, 1172 (N.D. Cal. 2010)). “While this standard does
20 not require extensive factual allegations, mere labels and conclusions do not suffice . . . .” Id. (citing
21 Iqbal, 556 U.S. at 678).

20       II.   OPENAI’S NEGATIVE DEFENSES ARE IMPERMISSIBLY DUPLICATIVE.

21             In the Barnes decision, which the Court cited favorably in Winns, 2021 WL 5632587 at *2,

22 the court observed that “‘[a] defense which demonstrates that plaintiff has not met its burden of

25 defense is merely rebuttal against the evidence presented by the plaintiff.” Id. “Thus, it is curious
26 that defendant asserts as affirmative defenses that which it need not prove.” Id. at 1174.
