 1                                            INTRODUCTION

 2          This case presents a disturbing transformation. What began as a non-profit co-founded by

 3 Elon Musk to develop AI for humanity’s benefit has, through coordinated deception, become an

 4 enormous revenue engine for Microsoft and its co-Defendants. The First Amended Complaint
 5 (“FAC”) details how Microsoft knowingly participated in this betrayal of the public’s trust—not as
 6 a passive investor but as an active architect and participant in the looting of OpenAI.
 7          When Musk contributed over $44 million to establish a charitable foundation committed to

 8 developing safe and open AI, and named it “OpenAI,” he did so based on explicit promises that it
 9 would remain a charity “unconstrained by a need to generate financial return.” Microsoft
10 systematically helped dismantle these commitments. The exclusive agreements Microsoft extracted,
11 the pressure it applied when OpenAI’s independent board members sought to preserve its charitable
12 mission by firing Sam Altman, and its ongoing efforts to entrench a “de facto merger” all reflect a
13 deliberate strategy to exploit charitable assets.
14          The Motion to Dismiss of Microsoft, Reid Hoffman, and Deannah Templeton (collectively,

15 “Microsoft”) recasts this narrative, portraying each as an innocent bystander who merely partnered
16 with an already-commercialized OpenAI. This revisionist account, designed to exculpate them from
17 aiding and abetting liability, does not withstand scrutiny. Microsoft’s CEO boasted about its total
18 control of OpenAI, and a sophisticated corporation like Microsoft, investing billions, necessarily
19 understood OpenAI’s foundational commitments that it helped violate.
20          The antitrust claims rest on similarly solid foundations. Microsoft and OpenAI together

21 command approximately 69% of the generative AI market, and their joint conduct—from exclusive
22 licensing and preferential pricing arrangements, to predatory hiring practices, to investor exclusivity
23 requirements targeting competitors—reflects a classic pattern of anticompetitive market
24 strangulation, not healthy competition.
25          At its core, this case asks whether a corporation may knowingly participate in the conversion

26 of charitable assets for its private gain without legal consequence. The question answers itself. The
27 Court should reject Microsoft’s effort to escape accountability.

 1                                          STATEMENT OF FACTS

 2             The FAC recounts the brazen scheme to defraud Musk and convert a charitable non-profit

 3 into a for-profit juggernaut benefiting Microsoft and its co-Defendants. We begin with OpenAI’s

 4 founding, when Sam Altman approached Musk with a proposal to co-found a non-profit, research
 5 foundation dedicated to developing AI for the benefit of humanity. FAC ¶¶ 82-90.1 Musk agreed to
 6 back this venture, contributing over $44 million, his name, expertise, time, and connections to
 7 recruit top scientific talent, based on express promises that OpenAI would remain a true non-profit
 8 committed to open sourcing its technological advancements for the public’s benefit. ¶¶ 91-97.
 9             Microsoft entered the picture in September 2016, and began selling compute to OpenAI at

10 below-market rates and proposed using OpenAI as a marketing tool to which Musk objected. ¶ 99.
11             By 2017-2018, Altman and Brockman began pressing Musk to convert OpenAI to a for-

12 profit. ¶¶ 102-03. Musk ultimately rejected this, stating unequivocally: “Either go do something on
13 your own or continue with OpenAI as a non-profit. I will no longer fund OpenAI until you have
14 made a firm commitment to stay or I’m just being a fool who is essentially providing free funding
15 to a start-up.” ¶ 103. In response, Altman reassured Musk that he remained “enthusiastic about the
16 non-profit structure!” as did Brockman. ¶ 104.
17             This assurance was a lie. Shortly thereafter, Altman and Brockman began secretly working

18 with Microsoft to form a labyrinthine for-profit structure (the “For-Profit Entities”) designed to loot
19 OpenAI, Inc. of its intellectual property and employees for private gain, with almost no revenue

 5          When considering a motion to dismiss under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(b)(6), the
