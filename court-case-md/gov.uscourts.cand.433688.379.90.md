 6          Microsoft and OpenAI have converted a nonprofit organization founded to “benefit

 7 humanity as a whole, unconstrained by a need to generate financial return” — and funded by

 8 Plaintiff Elon Musk for that exclusive purpose — into a private company valued at as much as $1

 9 trillion. Microsoft — with a stake in OpenAI it says was recently valued at $135 billion — disavows

10 its role in OpenAI’s conversion into a commercial enterprise and disclaims knowledge of OpenAI’s

11 duties to its early charitable donors. But there is ample evidence from which a reasonable jury could

12 infer that Microsoft was well aware of OpenAI’s breach of its duty to its donors, including Musk,

13 to use their funds for charitable purposes and that it aided and abetted that breach and unjustly

14 enriched itself.

15          Musk’s claims are not duplicative. He has alleged distinct theories of liability: (i) that

16 Microsoft knowingly aided and abetted OpenAI’s breach of its fiduciary duty to its donors; and

17 (ii) that Microsoft has benefited from Musk’s donations to OpenAI under circumstances that would

18 make its retention of those benefits unjust. Both claims should go to trial.

19                                             BACKGROUND

20          A.        Microsoft Knew About OpenAI’s Nonprofit Commitments from the Beginning
21          In December 2015, Elon Musk, Sam Altman, Greg Brockman, and Ilya Sutskever co-
22 founded OpenAI as a nonprofit artificial intelligence research lab with a mission to benefit

23 humanity. Ex. 2; Ex. 3 (Sutskever Tr.) at 345:9-18; Ex. 4 (Musk Tr.) at 22:5-23:3. Musk started

24 OpenAI as a nonprofit because of his longstanding concern that concentrated control of artificial

25 intelligence by closed, for-profit companies posed an existential threat to humanity. Ex. 4 (Musk

26 Tr.) at 22:5-23:3, 28:23-30:7. As its primary funder, he also insisted OpenAI remain a nonprofit to

27 ensure it did not become corrupted by profit motives. Ex. 4 (Musk Tr.) at 23:9-26:16, 50:23-52:23;

28 Ex. 5; Ex. 6 at -5439. His co-founders agreed, and OpenAI announced those commitments in its

13          B.      Microsoft Invests in a For-Profit OpenAI

14          The landscape shifted in 2018, when OpenAI created its first Generative Pre-trained

15 Transformer (“GPT”), a large language model (“LLM”) and the predecessor to its AI chatbot,

16 ChatGPT. Ex. 3 (Sutskever Tr.) at 60:1-7. Around the same time, Musk resigned from OpenAI’s

17 board after the OpenAI co-founders failed to reach agreement about its corporate governance

18 structure moving forward. Ex. 14; Ex. 15.

19          Within weeks of Musk’s resignation, Altman shared with Microsoft his plans to “launch a

20 new commercial venture,” i.e., a for-profit arm of OpenAI in which Microsoft could invest. Ex. 1
