 1          Pursuant to the Court’s October 9 Order, Dkt. 314, Plaintiffs respectfully provide notice that

 2 Civil Local Rule 5-1(d)(5) does not apply to the technological difficulties that impacted their filing

 3 of docket entries 300 (Plaintiffs’ Administrative Motion to Consider Whether Another Party’s

 4 Material Should Be Sealed (“Administrative Motion”)) and 301 (Plaintiffs’ Discovery Letter Brief)

 5 (together, the “Motions”). Civil Local Rule 5-1(d)(5) applies only when “the ECF system [has been]

 6 subject to a technical failure” and requires that, in such cases, the filer must have “attempt[ed] to file

 7 electronically at least two times after 12:00 noon separated by at least one hour on each day of delay

 8 due to such technical failure.” Civ. L.R. 5-1(d)(5). Plaintiffs did not attempt to file at least two times

 9 separated by at least one hour, and that standard is inapplicable because the issues that impeded

10 Plaintiffs’ filing were not the result of any ECF system failure. Rather, the filing attorney’s file-

11 system window froze when attempting to upload the exhibits to Plaintiffs’ motions (Dkt. 300). See

12 Ex. 1 ¶ 3. Counsel attempted several times to upload the exhibits and encountered the same problem,

13 preventing counsel from effecting the filing by midnight. Id.

14          Because Civil Local Rule 5-1(d)(5) does not apply, this case is governed by the general

15 standards of Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 6(b). That rule states: “When an act may or must be

16 done within a specified time, the court may, for good cause, extend the time . . . on motion made after

17 the time has expired if the party failed to act because of excusable neglect.” The excusable neglect

18 standard requires a court to “tak[e] account of all relevant circumstances surrounding the party’s

19 omission.” Pioneer Inv. Servs. Co. v. Brunswick Assocs. Ltd. P’ship, 507 U.S. 380, 395 (1993). Those

20 circumstances include “the danger of prejudice to the [other party], the length of the delay and its

21 potential impact on judicial proceedings, the reason for the delay . . . , and whether the movant acted

22 in good faith.” Id. The standard is “not limited to situations where the failure to timely file is due to

23 circumstances beyond the control of the filer.” Id. at 391. The Court’s determination “is at bottom

24 an equitable one.” Id. at 395.

13          A partner and two associates, both former federal judicial clerks highly experienced in ECF

14 filings, participated in the filing. Ex. 2 ¶ 3 ; Ex. 1 ¶ 2 . They followed the firm’s standard practice

15 for ECF filings, which requires that two associates file together. Ex. 1 ¶ 2 . One conducts the filing,

16 and a second monitors the filing via screenshare on Microsoft Teams (“Teams”). Ex. 2 ¶ 3; Ex. 1

17 ¶ 2 . At 11:45 p.m. PT, one associate logged into ECF, and shared her screen with another associate

18 via Teams. Ex. 1 ¶ 3. When counsel attempted to upload the first exhibit to Plaintiffs’ Administrative

19 Motion, her file-system window froze. Id. She attempted to upload the exhibit at least two more

20 times, but the problem persisted. Id. She was unable to resolve the technological issue on that

21 computer. Id. ¶¶ 3-4. Therefore, as quickly as possible, she arranged for the other associate to log

22 into ECF using a different computer while she monitored via Teams. Id. ¶ 4 . That associate

23 completed the filing of the Administrative Motion at 12:08 a.m. PT. Id.; Dkt. 300. At 12:19 a.m.

24 PT, the partner emailed both this Court’s courtroom deputy and Mr. Sutskever’s counsel to advise

25 them of the technological difficulties. Dkt. 311-1. The filing of the public redacted version of the

26 Discovery Letter Brief was completed at 12:20 a.m. Dkt. 301.

 1          First, the length of filing delay was de minimis. The filing occurred at 12:08 a.m. It was eight

 2 minutes late and had no impact on these judicial proceedings.

 3          Second, as detailed above, the reason for the delay was a technological failure that counsel

 4 took precautions to avoid and then resolved as swiftly as possible. Ex. 1 ¶¶ 2 -4. The filing occurred

 5 pursuant to a practice designed to avoid problems in the filing process. Two lawyers, highly

 6 experienced in ECF filings pursuant to that practice, conducted the filing. Further, the filing process

 7 began late in the evening because the meet and confer was scheduled for late that day to accommodate

 8 Mr. Sutskever’s counsel’s scheduling conflicts. See Dkt. 300-2, 300-3, 300-4.

 9          Third, Plaintiffs were clearly aware of, and endeavored in good faith to comply with, the filing

10 deadline. See Dkt. 312 (Court order citing Dkt. 302-2 stating knowledge of and intention to comply

11 with deadline); Dkts. 302-1, 302-2. Evidencing their diligence, Plaintiffs’ counsel sent a draft of their

12 portion of the Discovery Letter Brief to Mr. Sutskever’s counsel well in advance of the deadline,

13 stated their intention to comply with the deadline, and finalized and attempted to file their motions

14 prior to the deadline. Dkts. 302-1, 302-2. Counsel used a filing practice designed to safeguard against

15 filing problems and when confronted with technological failure, they addressed the issue as swiftly

16 as they could. Ex. 1 ¶¶ 2-4. Plaintiffs’ counsel then immediately advised the Court and Mr.

17 Sutskever’s counsel of the problem. Dkt. 311-1.

18          Fourth, the 8-minute filing delay caused no prejudice to the witness, Mr. Sutskever, or to any

19 party. Plaintiffs provided Mr. Sutskever’s counsel with Plaintiffs’ draft portion of the Discovery

20 Letter Brief stating their position on the issues to be addressed at the meet and confer. Dkt. 302-1.

21 Plaintiffs informed Mr. Sutskever’s counsel of their plans throughout the evening, including by

22 summarizing the changes that had been made to the draft. Dkt. 302-2. And Plaintiffs’ counsel advised

23 Mr. Sutskever’s counsel promptly about the technical difficulties with the filing. Dkt. 311-1.

28 Cal. Jan. 4, 2019), Dkt. 124 at 4 (declining to strike untimely opposition brief filed at 12:10 a.m.
