 1                             STATEMENT OF ISSUES TO BE DECIDED

 2           1.      Whether summary judgment should be granted in favor of the OpenAI Defendants

 3    on Musk’s breach of charitable trust claim (Count XVIII) because there is no genuine dispute of

 4    material fact that Musk lacks standing to pursue the claim, and there is otherwise no charitable trust

 5    for Musk to enforce.

 6           2.      Whether summary judgment should be granted in favor of the OpenAI Defendants

 7    on Musk’s constructive fraud claim (Count VI) because there is no genuine dispute of material fact

 8    that: (a) there is no fiduciary relationship between Musk and the OpenAI Defendants; (b) Musk

 9    lacks standing to pursue the claim; and (c) the claim fails for the same reason as Musk’s fraud claim

10    and is time-barred under the applicable three-year statute of limitations.

11           3.      Whether summary judgment should be granted in favor of the OpenAI Defendants

12    on Musk’s fraud claim (Count VII) because there is no genuine dispute of material fact that: (a)

13    there was no actionable misrepresentation; (b) Musk did not justifiably rely on the alleged

14    misrepresentations; and (c) the claim is time-barred under the applicable three-year statute of

15    limitations.

16           4.      Whether summary judgment should be granted in favor of the OpenAI Defendants

17    on Musk’s unjust enrichment claim (Count IV) because there is no genuine dispute of material fact

18    that: (a) California law bars the claim because Musk has chosen to pursue tort claims based on the

19    same alleged conduct; (b) any request-based quasi-contract theory fails because there was no

20    expectation that Musk would be compensated for his alleged contributions to OpenAI; (c) any

21    fraud-based theory of unjust enrichment fails because (i) the alleged misrepresentations are not

22    actionable, and (ii) Musk also did not justifiably rely on them; and (d) the claim is time-barred

23    under both the two-year statute of limitations applicable to quasi-contract claims and the three-year

24    statute of limitations applicable to unjust enrichment claims grounded in fraud.

 3           Unjust enrichment “is not a standalone cause of action” under California law. Silver v.

 4    Stripe Inc., 2021 WL 3191752, at *8 (N.D. Cal. July 28, 2021). In the Ninth Circuit, “courts have

 5    construed purported claims for unjust enrichment as quasi-contract claims seeking restitution.”

 6    Brodsky v. Apple Inc., 2019 WL 4141936, at *10 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 30, 2019); Dkt. 298 at 2. The

 7    claim requires proof of the defendant’s “receipt of a benefit and unjust retention of the benefit at

 8    the expense of another.” Dkt. 163 at 3. As Witkin explains, “[i]t must ordinarily appear that the

 9    benefits were conferred by mistake, fraud, coercion, or request”; otherwise, the enrichment “is not

10    unjust.” Witkin, Contracts § 1057; see McBride v. Boughton, 123 Cal. App. 4th 379, 388 (2004)

11    (unjust enrichment permits restitution where “the defendant obtained [the] benefit … by fraud,

12    duress, conversion, or similar conduct”); Best Carpet Values, Inc. v. Google, LLC, 90 F.4th 962,

13    974 (9th Cir. 2024) (an unjust enrichment claim based on the defendant’s alleged “wrongful act”

14    requires a showing that a “cognizable legal right of [the plaintiff] has been violated”). And a

15    request-based quasi-contract claim can stand only if the plaintiff rendered the requested services in

16    circumstances where “it was the expectation of both parties during the time that the services were

17    rendered that compensation should be made.” Witkin, Contracts §§ 1072-73; accord Taylor v.

18    Google, LLC, 2024 WL 837044, at *2 (9th Cir. Feb. 28, 2024).

19           Musk’s unjust enrichment claim fails on several grounds: (1) California law bars the claim
