 1                                         DECLARATION

 2          I, Elon Musk, declare as follows:

 3          1.      I am the Founder and Chief Executive Officer of X.AI Corp. (“xAI”), as well as

 4 of Tesla and SpaceX. I submit this declaration in support of Plaintiffs’ Motion for a Preliminary
 5 Injunction. I have personal knowledge of the facts stated in this declaration, except as otherwise
 6 noted, and if called as a witness, I could and would competently testify to them.
 7          2.      xAI is engaged in the development and implementation of generative artificial

 8 intelligence and, as such, competes directly with OpenAI and Microsoft.
 9          3.      I have been informed that, during OpenAI’s October 2024 funding round, Sam

10 Altman stated that participants’ ability to invest in this round was contingent on their agreement
11 to refrain from funding OpenAI’s competitors and specifically named xAI.
12          4.      After speaking to other market participants, I believe that other investors, not

13 just those who participated in the October 2024 funding round, believe OpenAI may deny
14 allocations to those who invest in xAI and other competitors of OpenAI.
15          5.      OpenAI’s efforts to create a syndicate of high-value investors exclusively

16 committed to OpenAI damages competition in the generative AI market.
17          6.      The infrastructure necessary to develop advanced AI platforms alone is

18 staggeringly expensive and represents critical and prohibitively steep barriers to generative AI
19 development. Unlike OpenAI, which benefits from its close partnership with Microsoft that
20 provides it free or heavily discounted computing power, xAI must build its own. This means
21 that xAI must undertake the extraordinarily costly and complex challenge of constructing its
22 own technological infrastructure from the ground up. To do so, xAI needs significant capital

23 investment, far more than OpenAI does with Microsoft as its de facto parent.
24          7.      Additional necessary expenses to effectively compete in the generative AI

25 marketplace include costs associated with training the AI models—a process that requires
26 massive amounts of curated datasets, which are expensive to collect and process—and the
27 recruitment of specialized engineering talent, the costs of which results in very significant
28 additional annual expenses.

 1          8.     Simply put, more capital investment from high-value investors results in a

 2 stronger, more competitive generative AI marketplace. Without substantial and continuous
 3 access to capital, the growth and ability of OpenAI’s rivals to compete will be artificially

 4 stymied.
 5          9.     I am also deeply concerned that OpenAI and Microsoft’s obvious sharing of

 6 competitively sensitive information will further disadvantage competitors in the generative AI
 7 market, including xAI. The sharing of trade secrets between these companies concerning costs,
 8 pricing strategies, technological capabilities, product roadmaps, marketing targets, and business
 9 applications effectively transforms what should be a competitive relationship into a subsidiary
10 one. This coordinated effort by OpenAI and Microsoft does not remotely resemble true
11 competition.
12          10.    In contrast, xAI must rely on its own technical infrastructure and research and

13 develop its own training data sets, as well as conduct its own pre- and post-training programs
14 on models. By sharing all of this and more, Microsoft and OpenAI save themselves considerable
15 time and expense, two of the most important factors in determining success in the generative

16 AI marketplace today.
17          11.    I have been informed that OpenAI promised investors it would transition from

18 its non-profit structure to become a fully for-profit company within two years or would repay
19 all investment from the October 2024 funding round.
20          12.    Sam Altman and Greg Brockman suggested to me in summer of 2017 that

21 OpenAI should convert to a for-profit company. It was clear to me that they had discussed the
22 matter with each other prior to raising it with me. I made it known to Altman and Brockman

23 that, if OpenAI were to convert to a for-profit company, I expected my fair share of equity in
24 proportion to the substantial financial contributions I had made to OpenAI since its founding.
25          13.    Once it became clear Altman and Brockman would not agree, I made absolutely

26 clear by an email on September 20, 2017, that further assistance and donations from me were
27 contingent on OpenAI remaining a non-profit. Altman and Brockman agreed, and based thereon,
28 I continued to provide both money and services to OpenAI.
