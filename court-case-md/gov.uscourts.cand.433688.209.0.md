 1                                       ISSUES TO BE DECIDED

 5 disposition of Plaintiffs’ Motion.

 1                                            INTRODUCTION

 2          OpenAI’s Opposition tacitly concedes the fundamental deficiencies Plaintiffs identified in

 3 their Motion to Strike.1 The proposed amended answer removes all thirty-seven negative defenses

 4 and attempts to add factual detail to the remaining affirmative defenses. This complete overhaul—
 5 eliminating 67% of the defenses and only now attempting to substantiate the remaining ones—
 6 effectively confirms Plaintiffs’ argument: the original answer failed to provide fair notice, fell far
 7 short of the Twombly/Iqbal standard, and improperly included duplicative and confusing negative
 8 defenses.
 9          While OpenAI’s vastly narrowed, yet only somewhat improved, proposed amended pleading

10 remains deficient, Plaintiffs recognize that motions to strike are a disfavored means of addressing
11 such deficiencies. Given the expedited nature of these proceedings and in the interest of judicial
12 efficiency, Plaintiffs would consent to the Court treating their Motion to Strike as withdrawn if the
13 proposed amended pleading is ordered filed. Plaintiffs make this concession solely to advance the
14 proceedings and expressly reserve all objections, defenses, and arguments concerning the Proposed
15 Amended Answer, Counterclaims, and Defenses.
16                                              ARGUMENT

17          The Court need not reach these issues given the above, but if it does, OpenAI’s thin attempts

18 to justify its original pleading fail. First, OpenAI claims negative defenses are permissible, citing
19 out-of-District cases while utterly failing to engage with the directly applicable Barnes v. AT&T
20 Pension Ben. Plan-Nonbargained Program, 718 F. Supp. 2d 1167 (N.D. Cal. 2010), from this
21 District. Regardless, even if negative defenses are sometimes permissible, Plaintiffs showed why
22 OpenAI’s particular form of pleading them is not; they are not merely duplicative but also confusing
23 in such a way that prejudices Plaintiffs and deprives them of fair notice. Second, OpenAI
24 strategically relegates to Part III its attempts to circumvent the Twombly/Iqbal standard that controls
25 affirmative defense pleading, even though this was the Motion’s main point. OpenAI’s approach

 6        I.   A MOTION TO STRIKE SHOULD BE GRANTED WHEN DEFENDANTS PLEAD
               DUPLICATIVE AND CONFUSING NEGATIVE DEFENSES.
 7
 8             “To the extent that the [answer] restates negative defenses that exist in other parts of the

 9 complaint, those defenses are redundant pursuant to Rule 12(f) and should be struck so as to
10 simplify and streamline the litigation.” Barnes v. AT & T Pension Ben. Plan-Nonbargained
11 Program, 718 F. Supp. 2d 1167, 1174 (N.D. Cal. 2010). OpenAI cites this case but ignores its on-
12 point discussion, immediately pivoting to out-of-District cases to claim the rule is otherwise. Dkt.
13 205 at 7-8. It is not.
14             Regardless, the problem with OpenAI’s negative defenses extends beyond mere duplication,

15 even if such redundancy were tolerable. Contrary to OpenAI’s assertion that Plaintiffs show no
16 prejudice, the Motion provided specific examples showing how the overlapping defenses rendered
17 OpenAI’s answer incomprehensible to Plaintiffs. Dkt. 186 at 5-6 & n.1. The Opposition fails to
18 disentangle these logic puzzles, effectively conceding the pleading is unintelligible.
19       II.   MERE LABELS FAIL EVEN THE PRE-TWOMBLY/IQBAL STANDARD.

22 squarely fails that standard, it resorts to arguing that facts scatted elsewhere—in Plaintiffs’ Second
23 Amended Complaint, OpenAI’s Counterclaims, etc.—can salvage these defenses. But this Court has
24 rejected such reasoning: “In pleading affirmative defenses, a defendant must allege sufficient facts

10                                             CONCLUSION

11          While the Federal Rules of Civil Procedure provide OpenAI no right to amend its plainly

12 deficient Answer, Counterclaims, and Defenses, Dkt. 174, Plaintiffs will not stand on procedural

14 confident that substance, not pleading deficiencies, will determine the outcome.
15          Accordingly, without waiving any substantive response or argument regarding OpenAI’s

16 proposed amended pleading, Plaintiffs would consent to the Court treating their Motion as
17 withdrawn and ordering the Proposed Amended Answer, Counterclaims, and Defenses be filed so as
