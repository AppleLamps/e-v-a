                                  13          Movant Leonard Horowitz seeks to intervene in this action. The parties to the action jointly
                                  14   object. Having carefully considered the papers submitted and the pleadings in this action, and for
                                  15   the reasons set forth below, the Court hereby DENIES the Motion.1
                                  16          Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 24 provides for intervention both permissively and as of
                                  17   right.2 A movant may intervene as of right where they “claim[] an interest relating to the property
                                  18   or transaction that is the subject of the action, and is so situated that disposing of the action may as
                                  19   a practical matter impair or impede the movant’s ability to protect its interest, unless existing
                                  20   parties adequately represent that interest.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 24(a)(2). In order to intervene as of right,
                                  21   the movant must show:
                                  22                (1) the intervention application is timely; (2) the applicant has a significant
                                                    protectable interest relating to the property or transaction that is the subject of the
                                  23                action; (3) the disposition of the action may, as a practical matter, impair or impede
                                                    the applicant's ability to protect its interest; and (4) the existing parties may not
                                  24                adequately represent the applicant's interest.
                                  25

                                       finds this motion appropriate for decision without oral argument.

                                       intervention. As the Court explains below, Horowitz is entitled to neither.

                                   1   Citizens for Balanced Use v. Montana Wilderness Ass’n., 647 F.3d 893, 897 (9th Cir. 2011)
                                   2   (internal quotations omitted). Permissive intervention, by contrast, stems from a showing that a
                                   3   movant “has a claim or defense that shares with the main action a common question of law or fact.”
                                   4   Fed. R. Civ. P. 24(b)(1)(B).
                                                      “An applicant who seeks permissive intervention must prove that it meets three
                                   5
                                                      threshold requirements: (1) it shares a common question of law or fact with the main
                                   6                  action; (2) its motion is timely; and (3) the court has an independent basis for
                                                      jurisdiction over the applicant's claims. . . . Even if an applicant satisfies those
                                   7                  threshold requirements, the district court has discretion to deny permissive
                                                      intervention.”
                                   8
                                       Donnelly v. Glickman, 159 F.3d 405, 412 (9th Cir. 1998) (internal cites omitted).
                                   9
                                              Here, movant fails to meet the criteria for either form of intervention. Horowitz’s alleged
                                  10
                                       claims have nothing to do with those brought by Musk. Horowitz alleges harm stemming from
                                  11
                                       search results after entering his own name in ChatGPT. Though he uses terminology in his
                                  12
Northern District of California




                                       complaint similar to Musk’s complaint, the use of the same legal jargon does not create
 United States District Court




                                  13
                                       commonality amongst factual claims which are, at bottom, distinct.
                                  14
                                              Thus, Horowitz has shown neither that “the disposition of th[is] action may, as a practical
                                  15
                                       matter, impair or impede [his] ability to protect [his] interest,” nor that his claims “share[] a
                                  16
                                       common question of law or fact with the main action.” Fed. R. Civ. P. 24.
                                  17
                                              As such, the motion to intervene is DENIED.
