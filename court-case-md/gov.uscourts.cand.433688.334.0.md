                    1   I.     INTRODUCTION
                    2          Pursuant to Civil Local Rule 79-5(f)(3), Non-Party Ilya Sutskever (“Mr. Sutskever”) hereby

                    3   responds to Plaintiffs’ Administrative Motion To Consider Whether Other Parties’ Material Should

                    4   Be Sealed, ECF No. 300. Mr. Sutskever respectfully requests limited redactions to Plaintiffs’

                    5   “Joint” Letter Brief and Exhibit 4 which contain Mr. Sutskever’s confidential, personal

                    6   information.

                    7          As set forth below and in the accompanying Declaration of Ilya Sutskever (“Sutskever

                    8   Decl.”), Mr. Sutskever confirms the confidentiality of these portions pursuant to the terms of the

                    9   operative Amended Protective Order in this case, which the Court entered on September 18, 2025

                   10   (ECF No. 270). The majority of the testimony Mr. Sutskever requests to seal relates to a Highly

                   11   Confidential document he produced pursuant to the parties’ Agreement Regarding Production And

                   12   Use Of Highly Sensitive Document. Sutskever Decl. ¶ 4. That Agreement required, among other

                   13   things, that a Receiving Party “use its best efforts to limit such filing to only those minimal

                   14   portions” of the document or deposition testimony about the document that are “strictly

                   15   necessary.” Id. Notwithstanding this agreement, Plaintiffs unnecessarily filed more than 16 pages

                   16   of deposition testimony about the document. As explained in detail below, good cause exists to

                   17   seal his testimony relating to this document and specific harm will result if it is not sealed.

                   18          The remaining portions of testimony Mr. Sutskever seeks to seal contain highly confidential

                   19   information pertaining to his business venture, financial information, retention of counsel, and

                   20   personal reflections and communications. Accordingly, Mr. Sutskever hereby requests that the

                   21   Court enter an order permanently sealing the information identified in Paragraph 3 to Mr.

                   22   Sutskever’s declaration, filed concurrently herewith.

                   23   II.    LEGAL STANDARD
                   24          The presumption of public access to judicial proceedings and records “is not

                   25   absolute.” Nixon v. Warner Commc’ns, Inc.¸ 435 U.S. 589, 598 (1978). While dispositive motions

                   26   are subject to a “compelling reasons” standard to determine whether a party is entitled to file under

                   27   seal, to seal documents “not related, or only tangentially related, to the merits of a case,” “a party

                   28   need only satisfy the less exacting ‘good cause’ standard.” Ctr. for Auto Safety v. Chrysler Grp.,

                    1   LLC, 809 F.3d 1092, 1096–97, 1099 (9th Cir. 2016) (noting that the good cause standard applies
                    2   to “sealed materials attached to a discovery motion unrelated to the merits of a case.”). Good cause
                    3   is derived from Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26(c)(1). See id. at 1097 (“The court may, for good
                    4   cause, issue an order to protect a party or person from annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or
                    5   undue burden or expense….”) (citing Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(c)). The litigant moving to seal or redact
                    6   must show that “specific prejudice or harm will result” from disclosure. Heath v. Google LLC, No.
                    7   15-CV-01824-BLF, 2018 WL 4561773, at *1 (N.D. Cal. Sept. 20, 2018) (quoting Phillips ex rel.
                    8   Estates of Byrd v. Gen. Motors Corp., 307 F.3d 1206, 1210–11 (9th Cir. 2002)).
                    9   III.   ARGUMENT
                   10          Mr. Sutskever seeks to redact limited portions of Plaintiffs’ Letter Brief and Exhibit 4,
                   11   which concern (1) nonresponsive, sensitive personal and financial information, (2) information
                   12   related to confidential business decisions, and (3) information pertaining to Mr. Sutskever’s
                   13   personal and private reflections. Because these requests are narrowly tailored and supported by
                   14   good cause, Mr. Sutskever respectfully requests this Court grant his request to redact and seal these
                   15   portions of Plaintiffs’ Letter Brief and Exhibit 4.
                   16          A.      Good Cause Warrants Sealing The Information Mr. Sutskever Seeks To
                                       Protect From Public Disclosure
                   17

                   18          Here, the good cause standard is easily met. First, courts have consistently held that
                   19   redaction of nonresponsive personal content, including financial information, is proper. See, e.g.,
                   20   Richter v. Oracle Am., Inc., No. 22-CV-04795-BLF, 2023 WL 5663217, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 30,
                   21   2023) (permitting redaction of personal financial information); See, e.g., Freitas v. Cricket
                   22   Wireless, LLC, No. C 19-7270 WHA, 2022 WL 971578, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 31, 2022) (allowing
                   23   redactions of exhibits containing private information that could be used for improper
                   24   purposes). Additionally, the California Supreme Court has recognized that the right to privacy
                   25   enshrined in the California Constitution “extends to one’s confidential financial affairs as well as
                   26   to the details of one’s personal life.” Valley Bank of Nevada v. Superior Ct. of San Joaquin Cnty.,
                   27   15 Cal.3d 652, 656 (1975). The public filing of information related to non-party Mr. Sutskever’s
                   28   finances, personal communications, and retention of legal counsel, particularly in the context of a

                    5          Additionally, courts in this Circuit have held that a former employee can properly seal
                    6   documents the former employee possesses by virtue of his former employment. Sywula v. Teleport
                    7   Mobility, Inc., 652 F. Supp. 3d 1195, 1210-13 (S.D. Cal. 2023) (granting a request to seal internal

                   10   as a former employee. Id.
                   11          Courts have similarly consistently found that confidential “business information that might
                   12   harm the litigants’ competitive standing” satisfies the good cause standard. See Big Run Studios
                   13   Inc. v. AviaGames Inc., No. 21-CV-04656-AMO, 2024 WL 1364734, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 28,

                   15   result in competitive harm by revealing the strategy related to the inception of a new, highly-valued
                   16   company that is entirely unrelated to this litigation. See Blockchain Innovation, LLC v. Franklin
                   17   Res., Inc., No. 21-CV-08787-TSH, 2024 WL 4394758, at *3 (N.D. Cal. Oct. 3, 2024) (“[D]isclosing
                   18   information related to a company’s business strategy and finances can impose harm by allowing
                   19   competitors to capitalize on a business’s internal business decisions.”).
                   20          Finally, Mr. Sutskever seeks to seal two instances of testimony containing his private,
                   21   irrelevant lay opinion that could lead to undue embarrassment or harassment. This Court has held
                   22   that “[u]nder the Rule 26(c) ‘good cause’ standard, the court may seal documents ‘to protect a party
                   23   or person from annoyance, embarrassment, oppression, or undue burden or expense.’” Diablo
                   24   Techs., Inc. v. Netlist, Inc., No. 13-CV-3901-YGR, 2014 WL 31467, at *3 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 3,

                    1          B.      Mr. Sutskever’s Sealing Request Is Narrowly Tailored.
                    2          Mr. Sutskever seeks a limited order only redacting certain portions of Plaintiffs’ Letter Brief

                    3   and Exhibit 4. The proposed redactions are narrowly tailored to encompass only the specific

                    4   portions that qualify as confidential information and therefore do not “unduly limit[] public access.”

                    5   ASUS Comput. Int’l v. Round Rock Research, LLC, No. 12-CV-02099 JST (NC), 2014 WL

                    6   2810193, at *2 (N.D. Cal. June 20, 2014). Accordingly, Mr. Sutskever’s requests are the least

                    7   restrictive method of ensuring the protection of confidential information while permitting public

                    8   access to the maximum amount of information.

                    9          C.      Specific Prejudice And Harm Will Result If This Information Is Disclosed.
                   10          Given the extensive media coverage and public discourse surrounding the events underlying

                   11   the material Mr. Sutskever seeks to seal, including the decision of OpenAI’s Board to terminate

                   12   Sam Altman in November 2023, there is no doubt that publication of this material could promote

                   13   public scandal. The Ninth Circuit has held that the public’s interest in disclosure is outweighed

                   14   when “‘court files might have become a vehicle for improper purposes,’ such as the use of records

                   15   to gratify private spite, promote public scandal, circulate libelous statements, or release trade

                   16   secrets.” Kamakana v. City & Cnty. of Honolulu, 447 F.3d 1172, 1179 (9th Cir. 2006). Here,

                   17   making public testimony related to the events of November 2023 would only serve to reignite a

                   18   media storm, and threaten to promote not greater public understanding of the judicial process, but

                   19   rather eye-catching headlines premised on public scandal. Sealing limited portions of Plaintiffs’

                   20   Letter Brief and Exhibit 4 protects Mr. Sutskever, a non-party, from the undue burden of being

                   21   forced back into the limelight of an event that occurred nearly two years ago. See Pryor v. City of

                   22   Clearlake, No. C 11-0954 CW, 2012 WL 2711032, at *2 (N.D. Cal. July 6, 2012) (approving

                   23   redactions necessary to protect individuals’ privacy and to “avoid disclosures immaterial to the

                   24   Court’s ruling and solely likely to promote scandal and further private spite.”).

                   25   IV.    CONCLUSION
                   26          For these reasons and those set out in the accompanying Sutskever Decl., Mr. Sutskever

                   27   respectfully requests that the Court allow the proposed redactions to Plaintiffs’ Letter Brief and

                   28   Exhibit 4.
