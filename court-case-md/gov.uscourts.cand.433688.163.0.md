                                  13          Contract (Musk Against Altman and OpenAI)

                                  14          “To state a breach of contract claim, a plaintiff must assert: (1) the existence of a contract;

                                  15   (2) plaintiff’s performance or excuse for non-performance; (3) breach of the terms of the contract;

                                  16   and (4) that the breach caused harm to the plaintiff.” Smith v. Nationstar Mortg. LLC, 2019 WL

                                  17   1745121, at *6 (N.D. Cal. Apr. 18, 2019). Unlike an implied contract, an express contract is “stated

                                  18   in words.” Cal. Civ. Code, §1620. Here, the parties agree that there is no one single document that

                                  19   consists of a contract. At best, Musk argues that emails exchanged with various defendants between

                                  20   2015 and 2019 constitute an express contract. (FAC ¶ 251). The Court disagrees. Therefore, the

                                  21   motion to dismiss Count I is GRANTED.

                                  22          Although there is no express contract, Musk adequately pleads in the alternative that there is

                                  23   an implied-in-fact contract manifested by the alleged conduct of the OpenAI defendants. (Id. ¶ 260-

                                  24   262, 264). The FAC also adequately alleges Musk’s performance, defendants’ breach, and the

                                  25   resulting harm to Musk. The motion to dismiss Count II is DENIED.

                                  26          Under California law, a defendant is liable for false advertising if, “with intent directly or
