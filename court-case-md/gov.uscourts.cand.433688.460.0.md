 1          Plaintiff Elon Musk this week filed an “Amended Notice of Remedies.” In it, Musk claims
 2   to “clarify” the remedies he has been pursuing in this action. In fact, however, the Amended Notice
 3   does not “clarify” Musk’s position but changes it entirely. The Amended Notice of Remedies
 4   directly contradicts statements Musk has been making to the Court for months. It identifies
 5   remedies Musk has nowhere before sought and which he has previously repudiated. It anticipates
 6   a trial on issues never before identified and as to which no evidentiary record has been compiled.
 7   It would render many of the Court’s pretrial rulings moot and require a new round of pretrial motion
 8   practice. In short, the Amended Notice of Remedies seeks to upend the expectations of the parties
 9   and the Court, no doubt in recognition that Musk’s claims are neither plausible nor appealing—and
10   with the apparent objectives of sandbagging the defendants and injecting chaos into the
11   proceedings, while trying to recast his public narrative about his lawsuit. Musk realized far too late
12   that the case he has chosen to pursue for over a year does not hold up to scrutiny. Musk has
13   abandoned the only remedy he has pursued. He cannot now replace it on the eve of trial with
14   extravagant new remedies that are legally improper and factually unsupported. Without a remedy,
15   there remains no case left to try.
16          On January 16, Musk filed a Notice of Remedies at the Court’s direction. There, Musk
17   identified a single remedy—disgorgement of $100-plus billion, to be paid by the OpenAI nonprofit
18   to Musk himself. Musk’s lone damages expert, Dr. Paul Wazzan, opined only as to this proposed
19   disgorgement remedy. The parties prepared extensive pretrial briefing—including disputed legal
20   issues, motions in limine, and a Daubert motion directed at Wazzan—all addressed to Musk’s lone
21   proposed remedy. The parties likewise spent weeks preparing jury instructions and witness lists
22   predicated on that lone remedy and appeared for a pretrial conference where Musk’s counsel
23   reaffirmed his lone theory of recovery again and again. The Court then made Daubert and
24   evidentiary rulings against the backdrop of Musk’s proposed remedy.
25          Musk now proposes to expunge all of that. In the Amended Notice, Musk proposes to pursue
26   monetary remedies—the disgorgement of unspecified “ill-gotten gains”—not for himself but on
27   behalf of the OpenAI nonprofit. Such remedies are of course derivative, and Musk has no standing
28   to pursue derivative claims (as this Court has already held). Musk also seeks to pursue broad

26   OpenAI from further violating its obligations and abandoning its mission as a safety-first charitable
27   organization.” Id. at 20. The Court denied Musk’s motion. Dkt 121.

21                                              ARGUMENT
22          I.      Musk cannot pursue his new monetary remedy.
23          The Amended Notice seeks a completely different monetary remedy than the one Musk has
24   previously pursued. In January, Musk said his remedy was the Wazzan disgorgement theory and
25   that Musk would receive any award. As Musk’s original January notice explained, Wazzan
26   purported to calculate the OpenAI nonprofit’s alleged wrongful gains by multiplying the current
27   value of the for-profit enterprise by the nonprofit’s share of that entity, and then multiplying that
28   output by the portion of the nonprofit’s value supposedly attributable to Musk’s contributions. Dkt.

 2          Whether plausible or not, Wazzan’s analysis purported to identify what gains were at issue,
 3   from whom, and in what amount. As Wazzan recognized both in his report and at deposition, his
 4   analysis was premised on the understanding that Musk was seeking to recover alleged wrongful
 5   gains from the OpenAI nonprofit (a defendant on each of Musk’s remaining claims) and to recover
 6   those gains for himself (the plaintiff on each of those claims). See, e.g., Dkt. 393-2 (Wazzan Report)
 7   ¶ 29 (“Mr. Musk is entitled to recover the wrongful gains that OpenAI and Microsoft obtained by
 8   misusing the charitable contributions he made to OpenAI Nonprofit.”); Dkt. 393-5 (Wazzan Dep.)
 9   at 68:19-24 (“Q. [I]f Mr. Musk prevails and the Court adopts your analysis, OpenAI nonprofit
10   should be paying back some of the wrongful gains that you analyze; is that correct? A. Some portion
11   of their economic interests would transfer to Musk, yes.”).
12          The Amended Notice abandons that framework. Musk now says he is not seeking relief for
13   himself and instead seeks to “return all ill-gotten gains, including Microsoft’s, to the OpenAI
14   charity.” Dkt. 459 at 2. That request bears little resemblance to the remedy Wazzan’s report
15   sponsored or Musk disclosed in January. Musk has provided no notice or information about his
16   new remedy theory—asserting only that he will seek disgorgement of unspecified “assets” from
17   unidentified “transactions” to be returned to the nonprofit. Id. at 4. What transactions? What assets?
18   What amounts? From whom, precisely? Musk never says.
19          Nor has Musk put forward any evidence of any kind in support of his new theory. At
20   deposition, Wazzan disclaimed any analysis of wrongful gains by any person or any OpenAI entity
21   other than the nonprofit. When asked “what portion” of the wrongful gains he had calculated was
22   “a gain by the OpenAI nonprofit,” Wazzan’s response was unequivocal: “All of it.” Dkt. 393-5
23   (Wazzan Dep.) at 70. Wazzan also admitted that there was no “other OpenAI defendant” for which
24   he had “undertaken an analysis of wrongful profits.” Id. at 182-83. Not Altman. Not Brockman.
25   Not the OpenAI employee vehicle. Not any “other” entity. It is for this reason that the OpenAI
26   Defendants objected to Musk’s proposal to include Altman and Brockman on the verdict form:
27   Neither Musk nor his expert had “articulated any framework or methodology for calculating the
28   amount of [their] purported wrongful gains.” Dkt. 427 at 37 (Disputed Legal Issue No. 4).

13           Musk’s change of position also deprives him of standing. Musk pretends to seek to recover

18           Musk should not be permitted at this late stage to pursue a newly disclosed remedy that
19   Defendants had no opportunity to test through motion practice, or expert discovery, or as

21           II.     Musk should not be permitted to seek a permanent supervisory injunction over
22                   OpenAI’s future conduct or the removal of its directors and officers.
23           The Amended Notice seeks an injunction that would require “both the OpenAI nonprofit
24   and any for-profit subsidiary or successor” to honor Musk’s view of OpenAI’s “original charter
25   commitments,” with “[a]ny future product release, capital raise, or corporate transaction”
26   supposedly subject to this Court’s “continuing jurisdiction and contempt authority.” Dkt. 459 at 3.
27   That sweeping remedial relief has never been pleaded, never previously been identified, and should
28   not now be permitted.

 1           This proposed remedy is exorbitant. Musk effectively asks a federal court sitting in diversity
 2   in California to manage—indefinitely—the future conduct of a Delaware nonprofit and its for-
 3   profit affiliate, including all future financings and transactions. That relief is not remotely
 4   administrable, as it would require the Court to evaluate and police future business decisions as they
 5   arise, without endpoint or any workable limiting principle. It also would displace the ongoing
 6   supervisory role of the California and Delaware Attorneys General, who spent over a year
 7   reviewing (and then approving) the very structures and transactions that Musk would have the Court
 8   overturn, and under whose jurisdiction the OpenAI nonprofit remains subject.
 9           The Amended Notice also improperly seeks removal of Altman as a director of the nonprofit
10   board and removal of Altman and Brockman as officers. Dkt. 459 at 3-4. That relief was never
11   pleaded and never identified in any way before the Amended Notice. There exists no evidence at
12   all to support it, and Defendants have had no opportunity to prepare the record opposing it. This
13   remedy is also improperly derivative in character. It cannot be introduced into the case now.
14           Moreover, California law does not even authorize Musk to seek this kind of relief.
15   California Corporations Code section 5223 authorizes only a director, a threshold number of
16   members, or the Attorney General to seek removal of a director. Musk—a former donor and a
17   former director who left the board years ago—is none of those. The two authorities Musk cites
18   confirm his lack of standing. Dkt. 459 at 3-4 (citing People v. Wounded Warriors Support Grp.,
19   2018 WL 6581211, at *1-2 (Cal. Super. Ct. Nov. 6, 2018) (action brought by California Attorney
20   General pursuant to section 5223); People v. Help Hospitalized Veterans, 2013 WL 8213349 (Cal.
21   Super. Ct. Aug. 28, 2013) (action brought by California Attorney General)).
