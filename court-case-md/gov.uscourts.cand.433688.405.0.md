16          The Motion and attached exhibits should be sealed. Sealing is justified where “compelling
17 reasons sufficient to outweigh the public’s interest in disclosure” exist, including when “court files

18 might have become a vehicle for improper purposes, such as the use of records to gratify private

19 spite, promote public scandal, [or] circulate libelous statements[.]” Kamakana v. City & County of

20 Honolulu, 447 F.3d 1172, 1179 (9th Cir. 2006) (internal quotation marks omitted). In determining

21 whether compelling reasons exist to overcome “the public interest in understanding the judicial

22 process,” courts should consider “all relevant factors, including . . . whether disclosure of the

23 material could result in improper use of the material[.]” Foltz v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co.,

24 331 F.3d 1122, 1135 (9th Cir. 2003) (internal quotation marks and citation omitted).

25          The Motion seeks to exclude documents and testimony that Defendants have indicated they
26 intend to introduce at trial.    As described in the Motion, those materials include inherently
27 prejudicial and personal information about Plaintiff that has no relevance to the issues and risks

28 poisoning the jury’s impartial view of the evidence at trial. The Motion therefore refers to and

 1 attaches documents and testimony tending to “gratify private spite” and “promote public scandal”

 2 about Plaintiff and members of his family. Kamakana, 447 F.3d at 1179. And because these

 3 materials are irrelevant to the merits, as Plaintiff contends, they are unnecessary to aid the public’s

 4 “interest in understanding the judicial process” in this case. Foltz, 331 F.3d at 1135. That lopsided

 5 balance favors sealing.

 6          The risk that the materials in the Motion will become a “vehicle for improper purposes” is

 7 further heightened by significant pretrial publicity. Kamakana, 447 F.3d at 1179; cf. Doe v. Rose,

 8 No. CV-15-07503-MWF-JCX, 2016 WL 9107137, at *3-4 (C.D. Cal. Sept. 30, 2016) (granting gag

 9 order under the more demanding “substantial likelihood of material prejudice” standard where the

10 “nearness of trial” and “extent of pretrial publicity” risked tainting the jury pool). This is a high-

11 profile case. The national media attention ensures that disclosure of inherently inflammatory

12 materials will create the same prejudice among the public that the Motion seeks to avoid at trial.

13 See Does I thru XXIII v. Advanced Textile Corp., 214 F.3d 1058, 1073 (9th Cir. 2000) (“The public

14 also has an interest in seeing this case decided on the merits.”). Given the proximity of trial, sealing

15 is necessary to avoid the risk that prospective jurors will prejudge the evidence based on provocative

16 and irrelevant information that would otherwise be widely disseminated in the media.

17          Finally, Plaintiff’s sealing request is narrowly tailored and avoids sealing entire documents

18 where possible, consistent with Civil Local Rule 79-5(a). In Exhibits 1 and 4 to the Motion, Plaintiff

19 seeks to redact limited portions this Court has already ordered sealed, plus a few additional passages

20 not previously excerpted. Dkts. 375, 391-38, 391-39, 391-58; Declaration of Jennifer M. Schubert

21 (“Schubert Decl.”) ¶¶ 3, 7. Exhibits 2 and 3 – single-page documents designated as “Highly

22 Confidential” in discovery – are private exchanges irrelevant to this Phase I trial. See Dkt. 144 (Apr.

23 4, 2025 Hrg. Tr.) at 8:6-9; Dkt. 228 at 9; Schubert Decl. ¶¶ 4-6. The OpenAI Defendants do not

24 oppose Plaintiff’s sealing request. Schubert Decl. ¶ 8 .

25          For these reasons, Plaintiff respectfully requests that the Court seal the Motion and its
