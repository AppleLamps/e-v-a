 2              Defendants’ demurrer is based on the erroneous premise that Plaintiff’s Complaint has not

 3 alleged sufficient details concerning the parties’ Founding Agreement or Defendants’ breaches of

 4 it. Defendants are wrong. The Complaint sufficiently pleads the details of the Founding Agreement

 5 (Complaint [“Compl.”] ¶ 24) and also attaches as Exhibits contemporaneous documents that spell

 6 out those details. The first of those exhibits, Defendant OpenAI, Inc.’s Certificate of Incorporation

 7 (“COI”), states on its first page: “This Corporation shall be a nonprofit corporation organized

 8 exclusively for charitable and/or educational purposes,” that “[t]he specific purpose of this

 9 corporation is to provide funding for research, development and distribution of technology related

10 to artificial intelligence” that “will benefit the public,” and that “[t]he corporation is not organized

11 for the private gain of any person.” (Id. Ex. 1 at 1.1) The Complaint further alleges that Plaintiff

12 contributed tens of millions of dollars to OpenAI over a period of multiple years in consideration

13 for, and in reliance on, the Parties’ Founding Agreement (Id. ¶¶ 26, 61), only to have the Founding

14 Agreement flipped on its head in 2023, when Defendant Samuel Altman forced the resignation of a

15 majority of OpenAI’s non-profit Board and replaced them with new members hand-picked by

16 himself and blessed by Microsoft Corporation (Id. ¶ 31). OpenAI has become a de facto subsidiary

17 of the world’s largest for-profit company, Microsoft, developing artificial general intelligence

18 (“AGI”) technology for Microsoft’s private benefit, not for the public benefit, contrary to its own

19 COI and the Founding Agreement it memorializes. (Id. ¶¶ 31-32.)

20              The detailed factual allegations in Plaintiff’s Complaint are more than adequate to state

21 claims for breach of a written contract, promissory estoppel, breach of fiduciary duty, and unfair

22 competition. In their attempt to attack Plaintiff’s well-pleaded Complaint, Defendants resort to

23 mischaracterizing Plaintiff’s allegations and attacking straw men. Defendants also advance

24 arguments that are based on disputed facts outside the Complaint, which are plainly unsuitable for

25 resolution at the pleadings stage. For example, they contend they can show that Plaintiff supposedly

26 “abandoned” OpenAI, and thus is somehow precluded from bringing this suit. (Demurrer [“Dem.”]

 3              Additionally, Defendants’ erroneous “mutual assent” argument also improperly ignores

 4 Plaintiff’s allegations that the parties acted in accordance with the Founding Agreement for many

13 repeatedly misrepresent Plaintiff’s allegations and the terms of the Founding Agreement. (E.g.,

14 Dem. at 10-11, 13, 18). Defendants cannot prevail by knocking down their own straw men.

15              C.     The Complaint Alleges Bargained-For Consideration

16              Defendants next challenge the Complaint’s allegations of consideration. (Dem. at 12-13.)

17 Adequate consideration is presumed for written contracts, such as at issue here, and need not be pled
