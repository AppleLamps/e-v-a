 1                                           INTRODUCTION

 2          Defendant Microsoft Corp. seeks to preclude Plaintiff ’s expert on nonprofit custom and

 3 practice, Columbia Law School Dean Emeritus David Schizer, from offering any testimony that

 4 may reflect negatively on Microsoft’s conduct, even when the testimony is plainly relevant to

 5 whether OpenAI departed from nonprofit custom and practice. The Court should deny that motion.

 6 Plaintiff has repeatedly made clear that Dean Schizer will testify only about whether OpenAI

 7 departed from nonprofit custom and practice, not about whether Microsoft departed from custom

 8 and practice for for-profit entities. The mere fact that Dean Schizer’s opinions about OpenAI’s

 9 conduct may incidentally cast Microsoft in a negative light is not a proper basis to exclude his

10 otherwise relevant and appropriate testimony.

11                                              ARGUMENT

12          Dean Schizer is an expert on nonprofit custom and practice. From 2004 to 2014, he led

13 Columbia Law School, a nonprofit educational institution. Mot. Ex. A ¶¶ 4-5. From 2017 through

14 2019, Dean Schizer was the CEO of the American Jewish Joint Distribution Committee, an

15 international nonprofit humanitarian organization serving vulnerable populations across the globe.

16 Id. ¶ 6. He has studied nonprofits in his academic research. Id. ¶ 8. And he is the author of How to

17 Save the World in Six (Not So Easy) Steps: Bringing Out the Best in Nonprofits, a book that provides

18 guidance on effective nonprofit management and governance. Id. ¶ 7. Dean Schizer’s opinions in

19 this case describe nonprofit custom and practice, particularly with respect to for-profit affiliates or

20 for-profit partners, and address whether certain actions that OpenAI took depart from that nonprofit

21 custom and practice. See, e.g., id. ¶ 3. Plaintiff has repeatedly confirmed to Microsoft that “Dean

22 Schizer will opine on whether OpenAI departed from custom and practice for nonprofits, not on

23 whether Microsoft departed from custom and practice for for-profits.” Mot. Ex. C.

24          Microsoft acknowledges that Dean Schizer’s expert report reflects “a nonprofit-focused

25 methodology and provide[s] no guidelines for for-profit conduct.” Mot. at 3. Microsoft’s motion

26 does not object to a single line of that report. Instead, in an effort to gin up a nonexistent

27 controversy, Microsoft points to three instances in Dean Schizer’s deposition where Microsoft’s

28 counsel asked Dean Schizer plainly improper questions that sought to elicit opinions beyond the

 6          That questioning is not a proper basis for a motion in limine. The opinions that Dean Schizer

 7 plans to offer at trial are the opinions in his expert report, not responses to deposition questions that

 8 improperly sought his views on different topics. Even in those responses, Dean Schizer emphasized

 9 that his focus was on OpenAI’s conduct. For example, when asked whether “Microsoft [did]

10 anything wrong in negotiating for the IP rights it negotiated for,” Dean Schizer replied: “[M]y

11 impression is that [Microsoft] got a really, really, really good deal. And I have questions about

12 why OpenAI agreed to it, but my focus is on OpenAI.” Mot. Ex. B at 299:12-20 (emphasis added).

13 That response confirms that Dean Schizer’s focus is on whether OpenAI departed from nonprofit

14 custom and practice by failing to protect the nonprofit’s economic interests, not on whether

15 Microsoft acted wrongfully by extracting an overly generous deal.

16          To the extent Microsoft is seeking to preclude any testimony on these topics even to the

17 extent they relate to OpenAI’s conduct, the Court should reject its argument. OpenAI’s interactions

18 with its for-profit partner are highly relevant, and there is no basis for excluding the testimony

19 merely because it might incidentally cast a negative light on Microsoft’s actions. For example, the

20 fact that OpenAI gave Microsoft a “really, really, really good deal” in its IP licensing agreements is

21 highly relevant to whether OpenAI departed from nonprofit custom and practice by failing to protect

22 the nonprofit’s economic interests in transactions with its for-profit partner. Mot. Ex. B at 299:12-

23 20. The fact that OpenAI allowed Microsoft to participate in deliberations over who to appoint to

24 its nonprofit board by proposing, vetoing, and vetting particular candidates was also a stark

25 departure from nonprofit practice. Id. at 306:21-307:18. And the fact that OpenAI actively

26 encouraged Microsoft’s offer to hire away OpenAI’s entire workforce in order to exert pressure on

27 the nonprofit board to reinstate Sam Altman as CEO was an egregious departure from nonprofit

28 governance norms. Id. at 304:14-24.
