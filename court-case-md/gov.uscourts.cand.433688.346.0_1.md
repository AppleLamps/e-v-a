 8                                                 ARGUMENT

 9          A stay is “not a matter of right” but is instead “an exercise of judicial discretion.” Nken v. Holder,

10 556 U.S. 418, 433 (2009). See In re Soc. Media Adolescent Addiction/ Pers. Inj. Prods. Liab. Litig., No.

11 22-MD-03047 (YGR) 2024 WL 4495810, at *1 (N.D. Cal. Oct. 15, 2024) (citing Maness v. Meyers, 419

12 U.S. 449, 458 (1975) (“We begin with the basic proposition that all orders and judgments of courts must

13 be complied with promptly. If a person to whom a court directs an order believes that order is incorrect

14 the remedy is to appeal, but, absent a stay, he must comply promptly with the order pending appeal.”)). A

15 stay is an “intrusion into the ordinary processes of administration and judicial review” and accordingly, “is

16 not a matter of right, even if irreparable injury might otherwise result to the appellant.” Nken, 556 U.S. at

17 427.

18          The party requesting a stay bears the burden of showing that the circumstances justify an exercise

19 of judicial discretion. Nken, 556 U.S. at 433-34. In the context of a motion to stay a magistrate judge’s

20 non-dispositive discovery order, this burden “is a steep hill to climb.” In re Soc. Media, 2024 WL 4495810,

21 at * 2 (quoting PlayUp, Inc. v. Mintas, 635 F. Supp. 3d 1087, 1095 (D. Nev. 2022) (“ ‘A stay of a magistrate

22 judge’s discovery order should be granted sparingly.’”)). “Hence, the presumption is that discovery ordered

23 by a magistrate judge will proceed regardless of the filed objection.” PlayUp, Inc., 635 F. Supp. 3d at 1095.

24          In determining whether a stay applicant has met his heavy burden, a court must consider: “(1)

25 whether the stay applicant has made a strong showing that he is likely to succeed on the merits; (2) whether

26 the applicant will be irreparably injured absent a stay; (3) whether issuance of the stay will substantially

27 injure the other parties interested in the proceeding; and (4) where the public interest lies.” Nken, 556 U.S.

 5          A. Mr. Sutskever is not likely to succeed on the merits of his appeal.

 6          Magistrate Judge Hixson’s well-reasoned Order is entitled to “significant deference,” as magistrate

 7 judges have “broad discretion in resolving discovery matters.” In re Soc. Media, 2024 WL 4495810, at *2

 8 (citing United States v. Abonce-Barrera, 257 F.3d 959, 969 (9th Cir. 2001). “A non-dispositive order

 9 entered by a magistrate must be deferred to unless it is ‘clearly erroneous or contrary to law.’ ” Grimes v.

10 City & Cnty. of S.F., 951 F.2d 236, 241 (9th Cir. 1991) (citing Fed. R. Civ. P. 72(a); 28 U.S.C. §

11 636(b)(1)(A)). This is a “formidable standard of review.” In re Soc. Media, 2024 WL 4495810, at *2

12 (citing PlayUp, 635 F. Supp. 3d at 1096-97).
