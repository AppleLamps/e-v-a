 1          Pursuant to Section 2(a)(i) of the Court’s Standing Order re: Pretrial Instructions in Civil

 2   Cases (last updated March 17, 2025), Defendant Microsoft Corporation (“Microsoft”) submits the

 3   following statement of elements of defenses and summary of evidence supporting those defenses.
 4   I.     PLAINTIFF CANNOT ESTABLISH ESSENTIAL ELEMENTS (to Plaintiff’s claim

 5          of Aiding and Abetting Breach of Charitable Trust against Microsoft)

 6          A.      Elements of Claim

 7          Plaintiff Elon Musk bears the burden on all elements of his aiding and abetting claim
 8   against Microsoft. Before turning to the aiding and abetting claim against Microsoft, Musk must

 9   first establish that he was harmed by OpenAI, Altman, or Brockman’s breach of a specific

10   charitable trust created by Musk. Assuming Musk can establish that OpenAI, Altman, or
11   Brockman committed a breach of charitable trust that harmed Musk, Microsoft contends that

12   Musk will be unable to meet his burden of proof to establish that (1) Microsoft knew that Musk

13   made his donations for specific charitable purposes, and that OpenAI, Altman, or Brockman

14   committed a breach of charitable trust by using the donations for other inconsistent purposes; (2)
15   Microsoft intended to assist the breach of charitable trust; (3) Microsoft gave substantial

16   assistance or encouragement to OpenAI, Altman, or Brockman in breaching the charitable trust

17   created by Musk; and (4) Microsoft’s conduct was a substantial factor in causing harm to Musk.
18   Microsoft also disputes the amount of claimed damages.

19          B.      Summary of Anticipated Evidence

20          Microsoft intends to negate Musk’s proof of the knowledge element primarily through

21   testimony from Satya Nadella, Kevin Scott, Amy Hood, Michael Wetter, Musk, Shivon Zilis,
22   Jared Birchall, Sam Teller, Mira Murati, Ilya Sutskever, and Tasha McCauley; documents

23   reflecting OpenAI’s contractual representations to Microsoft; documents reflecting Microsoft’s

24   due diligence; and other contemporaneous documents.

25          Microsoft intends to negate Musk’s proof of the intent element primarily through the same
26   evidence it will use relating to the knowledge element, as well as documents reflecting the

27   rationale for the partnership and OpenAI’s board decisions.

 1           Microsoft intends to negate Musk’s proof of the substantial assistance primarily through

 2    testimony from Nadella, Scott, Hood, Wetter, Musk, Zilis, Birchall, Teller, Murati, Sutskever,

 3    McCauley, and its expert, Edward Buthusiem; documents reflecting OpenAI’s board decisions;
 4    documents describing the benefits of Microsoft’s collaboration with OpenAI in pursuing its

 5    nonprofit mission; and other contemporaneous documents.

 6           Microsoft intends to negate Musk’s proof that Microsoft caused any of his harm

 7    primarily through the same evidence it will use relating to the substantial assistance element, as
 8    well as documents reflecting OpenAI’s independent motivations and decision-making.

 9           Microsoft intends to dispute Musk’s claimed damages primarily through the same

10    evidence it will use relating to the substantial assistance and causation elements, as well as Dr.
11    Jonathan Arnold’s expert testimony to establish that Musk’s calculation of Microsoft’s wrongful

12    gains is unreliable and inaccurate.

13    II.   FIRST AFFIRMATIVE DEFENSE: STATUTE OF LIMITATIONS (to Plaintiff’s

14          Claim of Aiding and Abetting Breach of Charitable Trust against Microsoft)
15          A.      Elements of Affirmative Defense

16           Microsoft must prove that any of the claimed harm to Musk as a result of Microsoft’s

17    conduct occurred before November 14, 2021. If Microsoft proves that any claimed harm from
18    Microsoft’s conduct occurred before November 14, 2021, Musk’s claim was still filed on time if

19    Musk proves that before that date, he did not discover facts that would have caused a reasonable

20    person to suspect that Microsoft participated in causing his harm, and he could not have

21    discovered those facts with reasonable diligence.
22          B.      Summary of Anticipated Evidence

23           Microsoft joins, and incorporates by reference, OpenAI’s summary of evidence for its

24    affirmative defense of statute of limitations.

25           In addition, Microsoft intends to prove that Musk’s claimed harm occurred before
26    November 14, 2021 primarily through testimony from Musk, Nadella, Scott, Hood, Altman, and

27    Brockman; Microsoft’s investment and commercial agreements with OpenAI; and other

28    contemporaneous documents.
