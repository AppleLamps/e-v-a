 1                                              INTRODUCTION

 2           OpenAI proposes to call three expert witnesses to address a single topic at trial: custom and

 3 practice in the nonprofit sector. Each expert will opine that OpenAI’s governance structure and

 4 relationship with its for-profit affiliates and partners is consistent with custom and practice for

 5 nonprofits. That testimony is needlessly cumulative, prejudicial to Musk, and a waste of the Court’s

 6 time. The Court should preclude that duplicative testimony and require OpenAI to select a single

 7 nonprofit expert to call at trial.

 8                                               BACKGROUND

 9           OpenAI proposes to call Peter Frumkin, Daniel J. Hemel, and John C. Coates IV as expert

10 witnesses. Ex. 1. According to OpenAI’s expert witness list, each of those experts will focus on

11 the same topic: nonprofit governance and nonprofits’ use of for-profit affiliates and partners.

12 Frumkin will testify that “hybrid nonprofit/for-profit structures, including the use of for-profit

13 affiliates and/or a public benefit corporation controlled by a nonprofit, and related recapitalization

14 steps . . . are consistent with industry-accepted nonprofit-sector organizational models.” Id. at 1.

15 Hemel will testify that it is “common practice” for nonprofits “to engage with the for-profit sector

16 to advance charitable purposes” and will respond to the opposing opinions of Musk’s expert, Dean

17 David M. Schizer. Id. at 2.           Coates will likewise respond to Dean Schizer’s opinions about

18 “customary nonprofit/for-profit partnership and restructuring practices.” Id. at 1.

19
                                                   ARGUMENT
20
     I.      THE COURT SHOULD EXCLUDE OPENAI’S PROPOSED EXPERT
21           TESTIMONY AS UNDULY CUMULATIVE

22           OpenAI should not be permitted to pad the trial record and attempt to sway the jury with

23 cumulative expert testimony on a single topic. Under Federal Rule of Evidence 403, the Court has

24 “broad discretion” to exclude needlessly cumulative evidence. Davis v. Mason Cty., 927 F.2d 1473,

25 1484 (9th Cir. 1991); see also Fed. R. Civ. P. 16(c)(2)(D) (authorizing courts to “avoid[ ]

26 unnecessary proof and cumulative evidence,” including by “limiting the use of [expert] testimony”).

27 Courts in this district have thus noted that “it is the usual practice . . . , in the absence of extraordinary

28 circumstances, to limit the parties to one expert for each distinct [topic].” Riley v. Dow Chem. Co.,

 1 123 F.R.D. 639, 640 (N.D. Cal. 1989). Other courts agree, routinely excluding expert testimony as

 2 needlessly cumulative where it would cover the same topics as other testimony. See Davis, 927

 3 F.2d at 1484 (upholding exclusion of expert from testifying on the “same topic” as other experts);

 4 Rogers v. Raymark Indus., Inc., 922 F.2d 1426, 1430-31 (9th Cir. 1991) (upholding exclusion of

 5 expert testimony as unduly cumulative where “other witnesses already had presented most of the

 6 relevant testimony”); Barabin v. Scapa Dryer Felts, Inc., No. C07-1454, 2018 WL 1570781, at *3

 7 (W.D. Wash. Mar. 30, 2018) (excluding one of two experts where both offered “substantially similar

 8 testimonies”); MF Global Holdings Ltd. v. PricewaterhouseCoopers LLP, 232 F. Supp. 3d 558, 577

 9 (S.D.N.Y. 2017) (excluding duplicative expert testimony); Highland Cap. Mgmt., L.P. v. Schneider,

10 551 F. Supp. 2d 173, 183-84 (S.D.N.Y. 2008) (excluding expert testimony where it would be

11 “unnecessary and . . . a waste of time for all of [the party’s] experts to opine on the same subjects”);

12 Qualey v. Pierce Cty., No. 3:23-cv-05679, 2025 WL 254810, at *5 (W.D. Wash. Jan. 21, 2025)

13 (similar).

14          OpenAI’s three nonprofit experts offer testimony on the same specific topic that is plainly

15 unnecessarily cumulative. OpenAI’s expert witness list confirms that each of those three experts

16 will be called to discuss how OpenAI’s governance structures, use of for-profit affiliates, and

17 partnerships with for-profit companies compare to custom and practice in the nonprofit sector.

18 Ex. 1. That is quintessentially cumulative testimony that courts routinely exclude. Plaintiff and

19 Microsoft are each offering only one nonprofit expert apiece. OpenAI does not need three experts

20 to provide its own perspective on that topic.

21                                             CONCLUSION

22          The Court should exclude OpenAI’s proposed cumulative expert testimony and require

23 OpenAI to select a single nonprofit expert to call as a witness at trial.
