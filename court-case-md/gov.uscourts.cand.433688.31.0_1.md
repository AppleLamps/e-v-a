12       I.   INTRODUCTION
13            Non-party and putative intervenor Leonard Horowitz’s (“Horowitz”) Motion to Intervene
14 (Dkt. No. 24) (“Motion”) should be denied. The Motion fails to demonstrate any legitimate basis to
15 intervene under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 24 (“Rule 24”).1 As the putative intervenor,
16 Horowitz bears the burden of proving each of the requisite elements for intervention, yet his Motion
17 falls well short of doing so.
18            On October 8, 2024, Horowitz filed his Motion and a Proposed Complaint appended thereto
19 which advances a host of sensational allegations ranging from the OpenAI Defendants’ “alliance
20 with Google and the CIA” (Mot. at 12), to claims about “anthrax mailings” (id. at 21-23) and “the
21 natural healing arts and sciences,” including the “528Hz frequency” (id. at 24-26). The Proposed
22 Complaint asserts that the OpenAI Defendants violated federal and state law when their ChatGPT
23 interface provided Horowitz with false information in response to his queries about himself and his
24 work—and charged him a fee for doing so. Id. at 17-21, 35-37, 46-47, 48-50, 52-54. Horowitz’s
25 claims have nothing whatsoever to do with those in the underlying action and serve only to distract.
