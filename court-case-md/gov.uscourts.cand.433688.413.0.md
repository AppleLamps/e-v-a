     II.          LEGAL STANDARD
17
                  A party seeking to file documents under seal must establish “good cause” under Fed.
18
     R. Civ. P. 26(c). See Foltz v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 331 F.3d 1122, 1135 (9th Cir.
19

20   2003). “District courts within the Ninth Circuit have found that a party’s legitimate interest in

21   ensuring the privacy of personal information outweighs the public’s interest in access to court
22   filings.” Richter v. Oracle Am., Inc., No. 22-CV-04795-BLF, 2023 WL 5663217, at *2 (N.D.
23
     Cal. Aug. 30, 2023).

 1          Here, the “good cause” standard is met as to the materials sought to be sealed by

 2   Microsoft, as the deposition transcript discloses sensitive and irrelevant personal information

 3   concerning a third party who is not involved in this case. As courts have recognized, personal

 4   information involving nonparties can outweigh the presumption of public access, especially

 5   when the information concerns a sensitive private matter and has no bearing on the issues in

 6   dispute. See, e.g., Moussouris v. Microsoft Corp., No. 15-CV-1483-JLR, 2018 WL 1159251, at

 7   *6 (W.D. Wash. Feb. 16, 2018) (noting that the privacy interests of nonparties may outweigh

 8   the public's interest in knowing certain information when the information is private to the

 9   individuals involved and the nonparties have not sought to place that information in the public

10   sphere), report and recommendation adopted, 2018 WL 1157997 (W.D. Wash. Mar. 1, 2018);

11   In re BofI Holding, Inc. Securities Litigation, No. 15-cv-2324, 2021 WL 3700749, at *8 (S.D.

12   Cal. 2021) (“Requests to seal personal information are often granted to protect an individual's

13   privacy and prevent exposure to harm, particularly where the information relates to

14   nonparties.”); In re MyFord Touch Consumer Litig,, No. 13-cv-3072, 2018 WL 1027391, at *2

15   (N.D. Cal. 2018) (“minimal” public interest in information that “does not relate directly to the

16   issues in dispute”).

17          Microsoft seeks to seal only this limited testimony regarding personal information

18   concerning a third party that is not relevant to the issues in the present case. Thus, Microsoft

19   references the information only to seek to exclude it in Microsoft’s Motion in Limine. As

20   explained in the Declaration of Nisha Patel in Support of Defendant Microsoft’s Administrative

21   Motion to Seal, making this information public could compromise the third party’s privacy.

22   IV.    CONCLUSION

23          For the foregoing reasons, Microsoft respectfully moves this Court pursuant to Civil

24   L.R. 79-5 to keep the above-listed material under seal.
