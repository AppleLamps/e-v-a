                                  10          In ECF No. 296, Plaintiff Elon Musk moves for an order authorizing alternative service of

                                  11   a deposition subpoena on non-party Ermira Murati, finding that Murati has been adequately

                                  12   served, and compelling Murati’s compliance with the subpoena. OpenAI opposes the motion,
Northern District of California
 United States District Court




                                  13   ECF No. 303, and Microsoft joins that opposition. ECF No. 304.

                                  14          OpenAI does not address the merits of the motion but argues that it is untimely. OpenAI is

                                  15   mistaken. Civil Local Rule 37-3 states that “[d]iscovery requests that call for responses or

                                  16   depositions after the applicable discovery cut-off are not enforceable, except by order of the Court

                                  17   for good cause shown.” OpenAI is correct that October 1, 2025 was the fact discovery cutoff, but

                                  18   Musk’s deposition subpoenas satisfy Rule 37-3 because they did not call for depositions after

                                  19   October 1, 2025. Musk noticed Murati’s deposition for September 18, 2025 (ECF No. 296-6),

                                  20   September 30, 2025 (ECF No. 296-8), and October 1, 2025 (ECF No. 296-10) – all timely dates.

                                  21   Nor is the motion to compel untimely, as it was filed on October 7, 2025, which is timely under

                                  22   Rule 37-3 given the October 1, 2025 fact discovery cutoff.

                                  23          Separately, the Court thinks Musk’s motion has merit and will enter Plaintiff’s proposed

                                  24   order granting the motion.

                                  25          IT IS SO ORDERED.
