 3   III.     ARGUMENT

 4            Here, the “compelling reasons” standard is met as to the materials sought to be sealed

 5   by Microsoft, as the deposition transcript discloses sensitive commercial contractual terms that

 6   could impose harm by providing competitors with “insight into the parties’ business model and

 7   strategy.” Exeltis USA Inc. v. First Databank, Inc., No. 17-CV-04810-HSG, 2020 WL 2838812,

 8   at *1 (N.D. Cal. June 1, 2020) (citation omitted). In fact, there is a compelling reason to seal

 9   agreements that “detail Defendant’s commercial practices, including product rates, rate

10   structures, customers’ purchase requirements, and the conditions for advance payments.”

11   Nicolosi Distributing, Inc. v. Finishmaster, Inc., No. 18–cv–03587–BLF, 2018 WL 10758114,

12   at *2 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 28, 2018). Even years-old financial data remain sensitive when they

13   reveal a company’s ongoing pricing strategies. In re MyFord Touch Consumer Litig., No. 13-

14   CV-03072-EMC, 2018 WL 1027391, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Feb. 2, 2018). As this Court has

15   recognized, these types of competitive concerns are a particularly “legitimate basis for sealing

16   information which otherwise has no bearing on the litigation before the court.” Murphy v. Kavo

17   Am. Corp., No. 11-CV-0410-YGR, 2012 WL 1497489, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Apr. 27, 2012). See

18   also In re MyFord, 2018 WL 1027391 at *2 (“minimal” public interest in information that

19   “does not relate directly to the issues in dispute”).

20            Microsoft seeks to seal only limited testimony regarding the terms of agreements that

21   are competitively sensitive and which are not relevant to the Motion to Exclude. This

22   confidential business information relates to Microsoft’s Azure pricing and OpenAI’s spending

23   commitment to Microsoft. This is exactly the type of competitively sensitive information that

24   courts in the Ninth Circuit consistently protect. See supra, pp. 3-4. As explained in the

25   Declaration of Michael Wetter in Support of Defendant Microsoft’s Undisputed Requests to

26   Seal Summary Judgment Exhibits [Dkt. 368-106], making this information public could

27   compromise Microsoft’s competitive position in ongoing and future negotiations with Azure
