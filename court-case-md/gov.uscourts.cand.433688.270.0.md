 1   I.        PURPOSES AND LIMITATIONS

 2             1.1   Disclosures and discovery in this action are likely to involve production of

 3   confidential, proprietary, or private information for which special protection from public disclosure

 4   and from use for any purpose other than prosecuting this litigation may be warranted. Accordingly,

 5   the Parties hereby stipulate to and respectfully request the Court to enter this Stipulated Protective

 6   Order. The Parties acknowledge that this Stipulated Protective Order does not confer blanket

 7   protections on all disclosures or responses to discovery and that the protection it affords from public

 8   disclosure and use extends only to the limited information or items that are entitled to confidential

 9   treatment under the applicable legal principles. The Parties further acknowledge, as set forth in

10   Paragraph 12.3, below, that this Stipulated Protective Order does not entitle them to file confidential

11   information under seal; Civil Local Rule 79-5 sets forth the procedures that must be followed and

12   the standards that will be applied when a party seeks permission from the court to file material under

13   seal.

16   generated, stored, or maintained) that a Producing Party in good faith and reasonably believes

17   contains nonpublic, commercially sensitive information concerning a party’s negotiating position in

18   a live potential transaction that is protected from disclosure to negotiating counterparties by the

19   Business Strategy doctrine, which the Court has found to apply to the ongoing negotiations between

20   OpenAI and Microsoft relating to OpenAI’s potential recapitalization. Discovery Order, Dkt. 251,

21   at 5-9.

23   Protected Material under this Stipulated Protective Order.

25   stored or maintained) that a Producing Party in good faith and reasonably believes contains

 8   V.     DESIGNATING PROTECTED MATERIAL

 9          5.1        Restraint and Care in Designating Protected Material. Each Party or Non-Party

10   that designates information or items under this Stipulated Protective Order must take care to limit

11   any such designation to specific material that qualifies under the appropriate standards. If practical

12   to do so, the Designating Party must designate for protection only those parts of Discovery Material

13   that qualify – so that other portions of the material, documents, items, or communications for which

14   protection is not warranted are not swept unjustifiably within the ambit of this Order. Mass,

15   indiscriminate, or routinized designations are prohibited. Designations that are clearly shown to be

16   unjustified or made for an improper purpose (e.g., to cause unnecessary delay, expense, or burdens)

17   may expose the Designating Party to sanctions.

19   information or items that it designated for protection do not qualify for protection at all or do not

20   qualify for the level of protection initially asserted, that Designating Party must promptly notify all

21   other Receiving Parties that it is withdrawing the mistaken designation.

 5   VI.    CHALLENGING CONFIDENTIALITY DESIGNATIONS

 6          6.1        Timing of Challenges. Any Receiving Party may challenge a designation of

 7   Protected Material pursuant to paragraphs 6.2 and 6.3 below. Unless a prompt challenge is

 8   necessary to avoid foreseeable, substantial unfairness, unnecessary economic burdens, or a

 9   significant disruption or delay of the litigation, a Receiving Party does not waive its right to

10   challenge a designation by not challenging it promptly after the original designation is disclosed.

11          6.2        Meet and Confer. The Challenging Party shall initiate the dispute resolution

12   process by providing written notice to the Designating Party that (a) identifies each designation it is

13   challenging, (b) describes the basis for each challenge, and (c) recites that the challenge is being

14   made in accordance with this paragraph. The parties shall attempt to resolve each challenge in good

15   faith and must begin the process by conferring directly (in voice-to-voice dialogue; other forms of

16   communication are not sufficient) within 7 days of service of the written notice. In conferring, the

17   Challenging Party must explain the basis for its challenge and must give the Designating Party an

18   opportunity to review the designated material, to reconsider the circumstances, and, if no change in

19   designation is offered, to explain the basis for the chosen designation. A Challenging Party may

20   proceed to the next stage of the challenge process only if it has engaged in this meet and confer

21   process first or establishes that the Designating Party is unwilling to participate in the meet and

22   confer process in a timely manner or, following the meet and confer, the parties cannot resolve a

23   challenge without court intervention.

25   intervention, the parties shall follow the Court’s Standing Order in Civil Cases regarding Discovery

26   and Discovery Motions. The parties may file a joint letter brief regarding retaining confidentiality

27   within 21 days of the initial notice of challenge or within 14 days of the parties agreeing that the

28   meet and confer process will not resolve their dispute, whichever is earlier. Failure by a Designating
