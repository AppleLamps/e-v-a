 1          Plaintiff Elon Musk, Defendants Samuel Altman, et al. (the “OpenAI Defendants”), and
 2 Defendant Microsoft Corporation respectfully submit this joint response to the Court’s April 13,

 3 2026 Civil Minutes, which directed the parties to file (1) their positions on the Court’s proposal to

 4 bifurcate the liability phase of the trial from the remedies phase; and (2) a proposed agenda for the

 5 April 17 conference on topics on which the parties seek the Court’s guidance. Dkt. 465.

 6

 7 I.       POSITIONS ON BIFURCATION
 8          The parties met and conferred and have no objection to the Court’s proposal to bifurcate the
 9 liability phase of the trial from the remedies phase. The parties further agree that the jury empaneled

10 for the liability phase should be empaneled on an advisory basis for all counts.

11          The parties understand that, if the trial is bifurcated, the trial time allocated to the parties for
12 the liability phase of the case will remain as stated in the Court’s Pretrial Order No. 1 (Dkt. 446):

13 Plaintiff – 24 hours; OpenAI Defendants – 24 hours; Microsoft – 8 hours.

14          The parties propose to discuss at the April 17 hearing any scheduling issues that may arise
15 out of the bifurcation, including the timing of any remedy proceedings, the timetable for submitting

16 updated jury instructions and verdict forms to reflect the bifurcation, and the timetable for

17 submitting proposed findings of fact and conclusions of law on liability.

18          Defendants submit that because the advisory jury will not be deciding remedies, and because
19 the Court has yet to resolve which, if any, of Plaintiff ’s remedies are legally available under the

20 circumstances, neither the Court nor the parties should inform the jury what remedies are requested

21 or how or by whom they may be determined. Nor should Plaintiff be permitted to represent to the

22 jury that any remedy he seeks would benefit the OpenAI nonprofit or its public beneficiaries.

23 Defendants further submit that while the parties should not elicit testimony or make arguments

24 regarding specific remedies, the defense should be permitted to present evidence of and argue bias,

25 subject to normal evidentiary limits that will be enforced by the Court at trial.

26          Plaintiff submits that he should be permitted to inform the jury that he is bringing this suit
27 to ensure that OpenAI adheres to its public mission and to remedy its failure to do so. Plaintiff

28 submits that the Court should instruct the jury that the Court will determine appropriate remedies

 6 II.       AGENDA OF ITEMS FOR DISCUSSION

 7          The parties met and conferred on the following issues that one or more of the parties plans

 8 to raise at the April 17 hearing. On some issues, the parties have reached agreement and merely

 9 seek the Court’s approval. On other issues, the parties request further guidance from the Court.

10

11          A.      Witnesses

12          1.      Satya Nadella. The parties have agreed that Microsoft witness Satya Nadella will

13 appear only in Microsoft’s case, that Plaintiff will examine Mr. Nadella only when he appears in

14 Microsoft’s case rather than calling him in Plaintiff ’s case, and that Microsoft will defer any Rule

15 50(a) motion until after Mr. Nadella has testified. The parties seek the Court’s approval.

16          2.      Mira Murati. The parties have agreed to call non-party witness Mira Murati by
17 deposition video only, although she is a resident of the District and under subpoena for trial. The

18 parties seek the Court’s approval.

19          3.      Tasha McCauley. Plaintiff has subpoenaed non-party witness Tasha McCauley for
20 testimony at trial, and may seek the Court’s permission to allow the witness to testify by Zoom

21 rather than live due to the witness’s anticipated travel plans. Plaintiff raised this matter with

22 Defendants on April 15, and Defendants are considering their position.

23

24          B.      Exhibits
25          4.      Impeachment with Documents. At the pretrial conference, the Court provided
26 guidance about its required procedures for impeaching witnesses with prior deposition testimony.

27 The parties seek confirmation that the same procedures do not apply to impeachment with

28 documents.

 1          5.      Exhibits for Video Witnesses. For exhibits used in deposition videos to which no

 2 party objects, the parties propose to move the exhibits into evidence immediately prior to the video

 3 being played. The parties wanted to confirm whether this approach is consistent with the Court’s

 4 preferences. The parties also seek the Court’s guidance on when unresolved objections to exhibits

 5 to be used in deposition videos should be raised.

 6          6.      Opening Demonstratives. Recognizing the Court’s rule against showing exhibits to

 7 the jury during opening statements without prior approval, the parties seek the Court’s guidance on

 8 the procedures for using stipulated and non-stipulated trial exhibits in their opening statements.

 9

10          C.      Experts

11          7.      Expert Examination. Plaintiff respectfully seeks this Court’s further guidance on the

12 manner of examining expert witnesses. Relatedly, Plaintiff seeks the Court’s guidance on whether

13 stipulated exhibits may be used with experts without first being introduced through a fact witness.

14          8.      Timing of Experts. Plaintiff seeks this Court’s guidance on any restrictions on the
15 sequence in its case in which it may call its expert witnesses, particularly with respect to Plaintiff ’s

16 AI safety expert Stuart Russell, whom Plaintiff plans to call earlier in the case to educate the jury

17 generally about AI safety rather than to offer any opinions specific to the facts of this dispute.

18 Defendants had understood the Court’s prior guidance to be that all expert witnesses should testify

19 at the end of the relevant side’s case. Defendants do not object to requiring all experts to testify at

20 the end of the relevant side’s case, but if that approach is not followed here, Defendants propose

21 that there be no restrictions on when the parties may call their expert witnesses.

22          9.      Expert CVs, charts. Plaintiff seeks this Court’s guidance on whether the parties may
23 introduce into evidence (1) copies of the experts’ CVs; and (2) copies of the tables in Dr. Wazzan’s

24 report in which he sets forth his numerical analysis. Defendants object to the admission of these

25 exhibits on hearsay and prejudice grounds. Defendants also note that Dr. Wazzan’s opinions and

26 the tables in his report are not relevant to the liability phase.

 7          E.     Pretrial Deadlines

 8          12.    Updated Interrogatory Responses. The OpenAI Defendants have agreed to serve

17          14.    Summary Witnesses. The parties propose that Plaintiff disclose the identity of his
18 summary witness on April 26 and that the OpenAI Defendants disclose the identity of their summary

19 witness on May 3. The parties seek the Court’s approval.
