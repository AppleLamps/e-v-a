 4       III.      Argument

 5              “[A]ll orders and judgments of courts must be complied with promptly. If a person to whom

 6 a court directs an order believes that order is incorrect the remedy is to appeal, but, absent a stay, he

 7 must comply promptly with the order pending appeal.” Maness v. Meyers, 419 U.S. 449, 458 (1975).

 8 Mr. Sutskever has not only failed to comply promptly with the Court’s Order as required by law, but

 9 has repeatedly refused to do so or to seek the requisite stay. There is no good faith basis for that
10 noncompliance. Plaintiffs therefore seek an order that Mr. Sutskever show cause why a contempt

11 citation should not be issued.

12              Under Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 45(g), “[t]he court for the district where compliance

13 is required . . . may hold in contempt a person who, having been served, fails without adequate

14 excuse to obey the subpoena or an order related to it.” Where the recipient of such an order “fails

15 to comply without adequate excuse, the recipient is in contempt of court and the proponent must file

16 an application for an order to show cause why a contempt citation should not issue.” GREE, Inc. v.

17 Supercell Oy, No. 21-mc-80069, 2021 WL 1222487, at *2 (N.D. Cal. April 1, 2021) (Hixson, J.).

18 Civil contempt is intended “to coerce [a non-party] into compliance with the court’s order.” Shell

19 Offshore Inc. v. Greenpeace, Inc., 815 F.3d 623, 629 (9th Cir. 2016) (quoting United States v. United

20 Mine Workers of Am., 330 U.S. 258, 303-04 (1947)). “The district court has wide latitude in

21 deciding whether there has been a contemptuous defiance of one of its orders.” Stone v. City and

22 Cty. of San Francisco, 968 F.2d 850, 856 (9th Cir. 1992).

23              To establish civil contempt, the moving party must show by clear and convincing evidence

24 that the alleged contemnor violated a specific order of the court. Erickson v. Builder Advisor Grp.

25 LLC, No. 22-MC-80094, 2022 WL 3109587, at *2 (N.D. Cal. July 6, 2022) (Hixson, J.) (citing FTC

26 v. Affordable Media, 179 F.3d 1228, 1239 (9th Cir. 1999)). The burden then shifts to the alleged

27 contemnor to show why they were unable to comply. Erickson, 2022 WL 3109587, at *2 (citing

28 Reno Air Racing Ass’n v. Mccord, 452 F.3d 1126, 1130 (9th Cir. 2006)). In making its assessment,

 1 the Court must look to whether the alleged contemnor performed “all reasonable steps within

 2 [his/her] power to ensure compliance” with the court’s orders. Stone, 968 F.2d at 856. “Intent is

 3 irrelevant to a finding of civil contempt, and therefore, good faith is not a defense.” Erickson, 2022

 4 WL 3109587, at *2 (quoting Stone, 968 F.2d at 856).

 5          The requirements for contempt are met. This Court’s order is crystal clear: “[T]he Court

 6 GRANTS Plaintiffs’ motion to compel and ORDERS (1) Sutskever to produce the Brockman memo

 7 to Plaintiffs and (2) Plaintiffs may depose Sutskever in a second deposition concerning the value of

 8 his financial interest in OpenAI and concerning the Brockman memo.” Dkt. 324 at 2. That Order

 9 unambiguously required Mr. Sutskever (1) to produce the Brockman Memo (not merely make it
10 available for review under convoluted procedures of his own devising); and (2) to make himself

11 available for a deposition on both his equity stake and the Brockman memo.

12          Mr. Sutskever did neither of those things, despite having had ample time to comply with the

13 order, which was entered eleven days ago. “The lack of an express deadline for compliance does

14 not relieve a party of its duty to comply with a court order.” Advanced Microtherm, Inc. v. Norman

15 Wright Mech. Equip. Corp., No. C 04-2266 JW (PVT), 2010 WL 10133699, at *1 (N.D. Cal. Sept.

16 22, 2010). Courts in this District have held that the time for compliance with a straightforward

17 discovery order, like the one at issue here, is “within a week or so.” Id. at *2. The Order is hardly

18 burdensome—Mr. Sutskever was ordered to produce one document that he already possesses.

19 Plaintiffs also offered to ease the burden of a follow-up deposition, by agreeing to take it by video

20 on an agreed-upon date. And indeed, Mr. Sutskever has never suggested that he is unable to comply

21 with the Court’s order, just that he was unwilling to do so unless Plaintiffs agree to burdensome

22 concessions and partial compliance.

23          Mr. Sutskever’s claimed plan to appeal the Order is not grounds for refusing to comply with

24 it. No stay issued automatically with the Court’s Order on October 17. “It is well-established law

25 that the filing of an objection to a magistrate judge’s order on a non-dispositive motion does not

26 automatically stay that order’s operation.” In re Soc. Media Adolescent Addiction/Pers. Inj. Prods.

27 Liab. Litig., No. 22-MD-03047-YGR (PHK), 2024 WL 4495810, at *1 (N.D. Cal. Oct. 15, 2024);

28 see also Advanced Microtherm, 2010 WL 10133699, at *1 (similar); Cuviello v. Feld Ent. Inc., No.

16 person on November 3, 4 or 5, 2025. To the extent allowed by law, Plaintiffs also request that the

17 Court award coercive contempt sanctions and Plaintiffs’ attorneys’ fees incurred in connection with

18 this motion.
