12          2.       The Court held an initial Case Management Conference (the “CMC”) on April 4,

13 2025. Dkt. 149. Following the CMC, on April 9, 2025, the Court entered a Case Management and

14 Pretrial Order (the “First Scheduling Order”) setting a pretrial schedule in this action. Dkt. 146.

15          3.       At the joint request of Plaintiff Elon Musk, the OpenAI Defendants, and Defendant

16 Microsoft Corporation, Dkt. 214, the Court entered on July 25, 2025 an order modifying the First

17 Scheduling Order by extending certain document and expert discovery deadlines (the “Second

18 Scheduling Order”), Dkt. 215.

19          4.       At Plaintiff Musk’s request, Dkt. 267, on September 22, 2025 the Court entered an

20 order modifying the Second Scheduling Order by granting a two-week extension of the expert

21 discovery window (the “Current Pretrial Schedule”), Dkt. 278. The Current Pretrial Schedule set

22 December 19, 2025 as the new expert discovery cutoff date. By agreement of the parties, expert

23 discovery concluded on December 23, 2025.

24          5.       None of the Court’s scheduling orders, including the Current Pretrial Schedule, set

25 briefing deadlines or a hearing date for Daubert motions.

26          6.       Consistent with Civil Local Rule 7, the OpenAI Defendants request that the Court

27 set the below briefing and hearing schedule for any Daubert motions submitted in this action (which,

11          8.       To further facilitate the orderly briefing of the parties’ Daubert motions, and

12 consistent with Civil Local Rule 7, the OpenAI Defendants also request that the Court set the

13 following page limits for the parties’ Daubert briefing:

14                a. Opening briefs shall not exceed 25 pages of text;

15                b. Opposition briefs shall not exceed 25 pages of text; and

16                c. Reply briefs shall not exceed 10 pages of text.

17          9.       Defendant Microsoft Corporation has advised that it concurs in the filing of this

18 motion.

19          10.      Plaintiff Musk has advised that he does not on the basis that it is too burdensome to

20 brief any Daubert motions before trial. Wiener Decl. ¶ 9. Musk would instead have the Court address

21 all objections to expert testimony through an unspecified mechanism “at trial.” Id. In the event the

22 Court chooses to address these matters before trial, as the Standing Order contemplates, Musk asks

23 that in lieu of separate motions challenging proffered experts, the Court limit the OpenAI

24 Defendants and Microsoft to a single combined submission capped at 25 pages. Id.

25          11.      The OpenAI Defendants submit that Plaintiff Musk’s proposed approach is the

26 opposite of efficient, imposing burdens on the Court and parties at trial that could be alleviated if

27 Daubert motions were briefed and considered in an orderly and complete pretrial process. The Court

28 should have the opportunity to evaluate fully and in advance of trial the admissibility of challenged

 2 remaining in the pretrial schedule to allow for that. Proceeding instead in the manner Plaintiff Musk

 3 suggests, with objections to experts handled on the fly during trial, would inject chaos into the trial
