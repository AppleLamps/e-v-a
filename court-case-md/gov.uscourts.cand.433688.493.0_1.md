 6   DEFENDANTS’ OBJECTIONS
 7          Defendants object to the proposed continuing violation instruction on multiple grounds.
 8   First, Plaintiff has waived the right to request any such instruction. After failing to propose a
 9   continuing violation instruction in the parties’ joint proposed jury instructions submitted in
10   accordance with the Court’s standing order regarding pretrial submissions, Dkt. 433, Plaintiff
11   disclaimed reliance on the doctrine at the March 13, 2026 pretrial conference. In response to the
12   Court’s direct question on the matter—“You’re not arguing continuing violation, correct?”—
13   Plaintiff declared: “We aren’t advancing the continuing violation doctrine.” Dkt. 445 at 33 (Mar.
14   13, 2026 Tr.). When the Court asked Plaintiff to confirm—“You are not?”—Plaintiff assured the
15   Court that it was “[c]orrect.” Id.; see also Dkt. 456 at 5 n.4 (Pretrial Order No. 3) (“At the pretrial
16   conference, Musk stated that he is not seeking a ‘continuous violation’ instruction.”). The Court is
17   entitled to enforce its pretrial procedures regarding the submission of proposed jury instructions
18   and to accept a party’s waiver. See Fed. R. Civ. P. 51(a); Kendrick v. Baca, 261 F. App’x 32, 33
19   (9th Cir. 2007) (“Rule 51(a) authorizes a district court to require parties who wish to submit
20   proposed jury instructions to abide by the court’s scheduling order.”). To permit Plaintiff on the
21   eve of trial to avoid the consequences of his waiver would only embolden his constantly shifting
22   positions that have frustrated the Court and Defendants.
23          Plaintiff contends that his statements to the Court at the pretrial conference were “not
24   intended to be a waiver.” Dkt. 486 at 2. But Plaintiff’s reliance on the continuous accrual doctrine
25   instead of the continuing violation doctrine was plainly a tactical decision based on his explicit
26   view that “the continuous accrual doctrine is the better fit for the facts of this case.” Dkt. 427 at 46
27   n.19. Starting with his complaint, Plaintiff advanced the theory that the OpenAI Defendants
28   repeatedly breached their duties, dating back to the formation of the for-profit subsidiary and initial

16   Qualcomm Antitrust Litig., 2023 WL 6301063, at *9 (N.D. Cal. Sept. 26, 2023) (relieving plaintiffs
17   of their “strategic choices” would “open the flood gates to prolonged do-over litigation”). 2

21   own.” Aryeh, 55 Cal. 4th at 1197-98. But as noted above, Plaintiff told this Court at the pretrial
22   conference—consistent with his litigating position from the outset of the case—that an actionable

 1   “breach [of OpenAI’s charitable obligations] occurred in 2019,” when OpenAI, Inc. “formed th[e]
 2   for-profit entity.” Dkt. 445 at 26 (Mar. 13, 2026 Tr.). He also confirmed to this Court that his claim
 3   is based on a theory that “[e]very one of the[] deals with Microsoft was a new breach. Yes, there
 4   was a breach in 2019, but there was a bigger breach in 2021, and there was an even bigger one in
 5   2023….” Id. at 35. Thus, Plaintiff is claiming “discrete, independently actionable alleged wrongs,”
 6   and the continuing violation doctrine accordingly does not apply. Aryeh, 55 Cal. 4th at 1198; see
 7   Brodsky v. Apple Inc., 445 F. Supp. 3d 110, 137 (N.D. Cal. 2020) (under Aryeh, continuing
 8   violation doctrine is “not [applicable] when a plaintiff experiences a series of discrete,
 9   independently actionable alleged wrongs”).
10           At bottom, Plaintiff is arguing for a continuing violation instruction based on his theory that
11   the OpenAI Defendants were required to maintain OpenAI as an “open source” “nonprofit”
12   “forever,” Musk Tr. 54:15-23, 285:19-286:24, just as he previously argued for a continuous accrual
13   instruction on the same theory. See Dkt. 456 at 7 (Pretrial Order No. 3) (observing that “Musk
14   argues that the jury should receive a continuous accrual instruction merely because OpenAI’s
15   obligation to Musk to stay open source and nonprofit remained ongoing”). The Court rejected that
16   argument, reasoning that “continuous accrual does not apply just because the alleged wrong has
17   continuing impact.” Id. The same result is warranted here, as the continuing violation doctrine
18   likewise does not apply to “continual ill effects from an original violation.” Deirmenjian v. Deutsch
19   Bank, A.G., 2006 WL 4749756, at *40 (C.D. Cal. Sept. 25, 2006). “If it were otherwise, claims
20   such as those [Plaintiff] assert[s] would never be time-barred.” Id. at *41; cf. Dkt. 456 at 7 (Pretrial
21   Order No. 3) (“Instructing otherwise would vitiate the statute [of limitations] and allow parties to
22   file suit at any time, as long as their injuries persisted.”).
23           Defendants reserve the right, if the Court finds that a continuing violation instruction is not
24   waived and should be given, to propose a competing instruction.
