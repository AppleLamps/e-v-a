 1          The OpenAI Defendants respectfully file this memorandum of law under the Federal

 2   Rules of Civil Procedure to preclude Plaintiff from eliciting improper testimony from Dr. Stuart

 3   Russell regarding “climate risk” and “concentration risk” that was never disclosed in his report.

 4   In their opening statement, Plaintiff’s counsel told the jury that Plaintiff’s AI safety expert would

 5   testify regarding four risks, including “risks for things like AI being able to manipulate the

 6   climate and affect the atmosphere, which could have very serious consequences.” Trial Tr. Vol.

 7   2, 235:6-9. Plaintiff’s counsel also told the jury: “The last risk he’s going to talk about is

 8   concentration risk. And concentration risk is the risk that this power of AI, or AGI specifically, is

 9   held by one powerful corporation or maybe a small group, and that the rest of us become

10   subservient to that corporation or group.” Trial Tr. Vol. 2, 235:10-14.

11          Such testimony exceeds the scope of his disclosed opinions and should be excluded under

12   Rule 26. Fed. R. Civ. P. 26(a)(2)(B). The federal rules generally “forbid[ ] the use of any

13   information not properly disclosed.” Key v. Qualcomm Inc., 129 F.4th 1129, 1143 (9th Cir.

14   2025). Thus, “when a party fails to provide information required by Rule 26, such party is not

15   allowed to use that information or witness to supply evidence on a motion, at a hearing, or at a

16   trial, unless the failure was substantially justified or is harmless.” Engilis v. Monsanto Co., 151

17   F.4th 1040, 1051–52 (9th Cir. 2025).

18          Neither of the two risks identified in Plaintiff’s opening statement match any of Dr.

19   Russell’s disclosed opinions. Dr. Russell’s report does not reference any ability of AI systems to

20   manipulate the climate and affect the atmosphere other than one line that AI systems “might

21   gradually change our view of climate change so that we pay less attention to it.” Russell Rpt.

22   ⁋ 77. (Defendants assume Plaintiff is not trying to violate the Court’s Order on Dr. Russell by

23   introducing one of his “[e]xtinction scenarios” in para. 104 of the report, which posits a scenario

24   in which the planet becomes uninhabitable.)

25          Dr. Russell also never offered an opinion on “concentration risk” as framed by Plaintiff.

26   The entirety of his writing on this issue is one sentence: “The winner-take-all nature of AGI could

27   also result in the benefits of AI being controlled or concentrated in one company or a small

28   handful of companies.” Russell Rpt. ⁋ 58. Plaintiff’s demonstratives for Dr. Russell now

 4            The prejudice to the OpenAI Defendants would be substantial. These are not minor

 5   elaborations on disclosed opinions; they are new and inflammatory theories presented for the first

 6   time at trial, after expert discovery has closed and after the OpenAI Defendants prepared their

 7   examination strategy and response based on the opinions Dr. Russell actually disclosed.

 8   Permitting Plaintiff to elicit these undisclosed opinions would deprive the OpenAI Defendants of

 9   a fair opportunity to test the basis for the opinions in deposition or otherwise move against them

10   in the ordinary course before trial.

11            Because these opinions were never disclosed and that failure would cause the OpenAI

12   Defendants significant harm, Plaintiff should be barred from eliciting them from Dr. Russell at
