                                  13          We are here with a couple of disputes about what is sometimes called the “business

                                  14   strategy doctrine.” First let’s discuss the doctrine, and then let’s turn to the disputes.

                                  15   A.     Business Strategy Doctrine
                                  16          Let’s start with BNS Inc. v. Koppers Co., Inc., 683 F. Supp. 454 (D. Del. 1988), a leading

                                  17   case in this area. In that case, Plaintiff BNS sought to acquire Defendant Koppers through an all

                                  18   cash hostile tender offer. The Koppers board had formally rejected as inadequate BNS’s initial

                                  19   offer of $45 per share and a subsequent offer of $56 per share. At the time the discovery dispute

                                  20   arose, the Koppers board was considering BNS’s latest offer of $60 per share. During the course

                                  21   of the tender offer, the Koppers board had announced that it was also exploring alternatives to the

                                  22   tender offer. See id. at 455. In discovery, BNS sought two categories of documents: all

                                  23   documents relating to Kopper’s ongoing analysis of the $60 per share offer to which Koppers had

                                  24   not yet formally responded, as well as all documents relating to alternative defense measures

                                  25   which the Koppers board was considering. See id. at 455-56.

                                  26          The court explained that “[w]hile not framed as such by the pleadings, the Koppers

                                  27   position is one seeking a protective order under Rule 26(c).” Id. at 457. The court noted that Rule

                                  28   26(c) “requires striking a balance between the countervailing legitimate interests and

                                  26          Courts applying this doctrine have explained that “[t]he business strategy doctrine applies

                                  27   when documents reveal strategies for retaining or obtaining corporate control during a live

                                  28   corporate takeover situation.” Allergan, Inc. v. Valeant Pharms. Int’l, Inc., 2014 WL 12573358, at
                                                                                         2
                                       Case 4:24-cv-04722-YGR             Document 251        Filed 09/09/25       Page 3 of 9




                                   1   *1 (C.D. Cal. Sept. 22, 2014). “Because of the dynamic context, deciding whether the business

                                   2   strategy doctrine applies is a fact-specific and time-sensitive inquiry. In determining whether

                                   3   certain documents should be protected, courts must balance the interests of the litigant who needs

                                   4   the documents to prepare for trial and the interests of the litigant who seeks to protect its offensive

                                   5   or defensive corporate control strategy. Courts operate on the assumption that the documents must

                                   6   be produced sooner or later and only protect the documents until a particular strategy is no longer

                                   7   being considered.” Id. “[A] target company’s strategies, alternatives, or proposals, while under

                                   8   consideration are protected from disclosure. The Court also agrees that the protection from

                                   9   disclosure is for a limited time.” Stena Finance B.V. v. Sea Containers Ltd., 131 F.R.D. 361, 362

                                  10   (D.D.C. 1989).

                                  11          “[T]his court and others have recognized that in a contest for corporate control, the board

                                  12   of directors of a target company continues to have an ongoing responsibility to manage the
Northern District of California
 United States District Court




                                  13   corporation and, in the face of such a contest, that responsibility may entail the exploration of

                                  14   alternative transactions that would better promote corporate and shareholder welfare. The

                                  15   discovery process may legitimately give some consideration to such efforts. We have repeatedly

                                  16   recognized that disclosure of such efforts, while they are ongoing, may be detrimental to

                                  17   shareholder interests.” Parsons v. Jefferson-Pilot Corp., 141 F.R.D. 408, 418-19 (M.D.N.C.

                                  18   1992); accord, Grand Metropolitan PLC v. Pillsbury Co., 1988 WL 130637, at *3 (Del. Ch. Nov.

                                  19   22, 1988).

                                  20          “Most importantly, defendants must establish the time period for the documents to be

                                  21   protected. . . . Only strategies or plans still being pursued in good faith should enjoy protection. . .

                                  22   . Even under a business strategy protection, there is no doubt [Plaintiff] will obtain otherwise

                                  23   unprotected relevant documents; the only question is when.” IPALCO Enterprises, Inc. v. PSI

                                  24   Resources, Inc., 148 F.R.D. 604, 607 (S.D. Ind. 1993); accord Parsons, 141 F.R.D. at 419 (“The

                                  25   cases engaging in this analysis, however, have developed a few helpful principles. Primary among

                                  26   these is the notion that, once a decision has been made, the shareholders are entitled to test the

                                  27   validity of that decision and, for that purpose, to inquire into its underlying basis.”) (cleaned up);

                                  28   Hexion Specialty Chemicals Inc. v. Huntsman Corp., 959 A.2d 47, 53 (Del. Ch. 2008) (rejecting
                                                                                          3
                                       Case 4:24-cv-04722-YGR            Document 251         Filed 09/09/25      Page 4 of 9




                                   1   the application of the business strategy doctrine to the situation of a deal that had already been

                                   2   negotiated and entered into but had not yet closed: “almost any piece of relevant discovery could

                                   3   be used to renegotiate the deal and that fact, standing alone, will not prevent discovery.”).

                                   4          Some courts have also applied the business strategy doctrine (which has also been called

                                   5   “business strategy immunity” or “white knight immunity”) outside of the context of corporate

                                   6   takeovers. For example, in Matter of Heizer Corp., 1987 WL 19560 (Del. Ch. Nov. 9, 1987), the

                                   7   court applied the doctrine in the context of ongoing negotiations with a third party:

                                   8                  I agree with the Heizer Group that the analogy to corporate takeovers
                                                      is imperfect. However, one of the considerations that has caused this
                                   9                  Court to limit the scope of “white knight” discovery applies equally
                                                      to the present controversy. In the corporate context, this Court has
                                  10                  consistently recognized the threat that disclosure of ongoing
                                                      negotiations with third parties will undermine the corporation's ability
                                  11                  to successfully conclude those negotiations. The prospect that
                                                      disclosure would thus harm the corporation and its stockholders has
                                  12                  been a factor in limiting “white knight” discovery. The same concern

                                  15   (“The pending motion to compel the production of documents raises the question whether a

                                  16   corporate defendant may, in the circumstances presented, legitimately withhold from discovery

                                  17   documents that disclose its corporate plans to deal with a possible future strike of its work force,”

                                  18   and answering the question in the affirmative).

                                  19   B.     Application to This Case
                                  20          1.        OpenAI
                                  21          Plaintiffs say that OpenAI has withheld or redacted 159 documents based on the “business

                                  22   strategy privilege,” 98 of them solely on that ground. The parties agree the documents span from

                                  23   May 2024 to June 2025. OpenAI makes two arguments in support of the business strategy

                                  24   doctrine. First, it says that its board is considering a potential recapitalization of OpenAI’s for-

                                  25   profit arm into a public benefit corporation still under the non-profit’s control. Defendant

                                  26   Microsoft is a major investor in the for-profit arm, and the OpenAI board has been in intensive

                                  27   negotiations with Microsoft as to the relative economic interests in any eventual public benefit

                                  28   corporation as between the non-profit and OpenAI’s investors. Second, in February 2025, Musk
                                                                                          4
                                       Case 4:24-cv-04722-YGR              Document 251       Filed 09/09/25      Page 5 of 9




                                   1   and xAI made an unsolicited $97 billion bid for the assets of the nonprofit. Although OpenAI

                                   2   rejected the bid, no one representing Musk has retracted their declaration of a future intention to

                                   3   big, and Musk and xAI must therefore be regarded as active bidders for OpenAI’s assets.

                                   4          Let’s consider these arguments in turn.

                                   5                  a.      Microsoft
                                   6          The Court finds that the business strategy doctrine applies as to OpenAI’s ongoing

                                   7   negotiations with Microsoft. This is not a ruling on the application of the doctrine to any

                                   8   particular document that OpenAI has redacted or withheld, none of which the Court has seen.

                                   9   Rather, the Court makes a more general holding that OpenAI’s ongoing negotiation with

                                  10   Microsoft is the sort of live transaction that triggers the policy concerns that animate the business

                                  11   strategy doctrine. In so doing, the Court follows Heizer and Gioia in applying the doctrine outside

                                  12   of just the narrow confines of corporate takeovers.
Northern District of California
 United States District Court




                                  13          The ongoing negotiation between OpenAI and Microsoft is a significant negotiation. The

                                  14   parties (meaning OpenAI and Microsoft, not Plaintiffs) represent that it is currently in progress.

                                  15   They cannot say at this time that it will conclude in a deal, or if so, when that deal will be struck,

                                  16   but they both maintain that unfettered discovery into the negotiations as they are underway could

                                  17   harm either or both sides to the negotiations. The problem, as the Court sees it, is not with

                                  18   providing the documents and information to the Plaintiffs, who maintain the contemplated

                                  19   transaction is flatly illegal and should be enjoined, but that Microsoft and OpenAI are co-

                                  20   Defendants and any discovery that gets produced to the Plaintiffs will end up in the other

                                  21   Defendant’s possession as well.

                                  22          This is a real, legitimate problem, and the business strategy doctrine was meant to help in

                                  23   this situation. Accordingly, the Court finds that the business strategy doctrine applies here. There

                                  24   is then a second question about what to do about that, and the Court discusses that issue below.

                                  25                  b.      Plaintiffs
                                  26          By contrast, the business strategy doctrine does not apply as to Plaintiffs. As noted above,

                                  27   in the context of a corporate takeover, for the target company the business strategy doctrine is

                                  28   about protecting the target from discovery into the board’s consideration of pending bids for
                                                                                          5
                                       Case 4:24-cv-04722-YGR            Document 251         Filed 09/09/25      Page 6 of 9




                                   1   control, as well as pending decisions on defensive strategies. Once those decisions are made, the

                                   2   business strategy doctrine no longer applies.

                                   3           Here, OpenAI rejected Musk’s bid four days after it was made. Any application of the

                                   4   business strategy doctrine ended right then and there. In addition, following rejection of that bid,

                                   5   OpenAI has repeatedly said that Musk’s bid was a sham. ECF No. 299 ¶¶ 83-90; ECF No. 210 at

                                   6   3; ECF No. 199 at 3. It is not true that OpenAI’s board is currently considering a bid it long ago

                                   7   rejected and that it regards as a sham. Musk does not have a current bid for OpenAI pending.

                                   8   Under the case law, a past bid that was rejected does not trigger the business strategy doctrine.

                                   9   That doctrine applied here from February 10 (the date of Musk’s bid) until February 14 (the date

                                  10   OpenAI’s board rejected it). During those four days, Musk was not entitled to discovery into

                                  11   OpenAI’s consideration of his bid, as well as any defensive measures it was considering in

                                  12   response to that bid. But once OpenAI rejected the bid, those materials became discoverable.
Northern District of California
 United States District Court




                                  13   There is certainly no authority to support OpenAI’s position that the potential for another bid in

                                  14   the future gives rise to an indefinite block on discovery into the board’s actions. OpenAI tries to

                                  15   flip the doctrine on its head by arguing that Musk has not disclaimed an intent to make another bid

                                  16   in the future, as if the burden is on the plaintiff to negate the possibility of a future bid. However,

                                  17   the doctrine is about things happening now – live negotiations, live planning, and so on – not

                                  18   things that might or might not happen in the future. Besides, “almost any piece of relevant

                                  19   discovery could be used to [make a future bid] and that fact, standing alone, will not prevent

                                  20   discovery.” Hexion, 959 A.2d at 53. Accordingly, the business strategy doctrine does not apply

                                  21   as to Plaintiffs.

                                  22           2.      Microsoft
                                  23           Microsoft “seeks a narrow protective order to exclude from discovery limited

                                  24   information—whether in the form of deposition testimony or documents—relating to (i) purely

                                  25   internal Microsoft deliberations or communications regarding its negotiating strategy or positions

                                  26   vis-à-vis OpenAI’s potential restructuring, and (ii) Microsoft’s valuation information underlying

                                  27   those internal analyses that were not shared with OpenAI.” ECF No. 241 at 3.

                                  28           As an initial matter, Microsoft makes a categorical argument that all possible documents
                                                                                          6
                                       Case 4:24-cv-04722-YGR            Document 251         Filed 09/09/25      Page 7 of 9




                                   1   and information that fall within these two categories are per se irrelevant to phase I. The Court is

                                   2   unable to accept that categorical argument. Microsoft’s internal deliberations could reflect or

                                   3   imply its knowledge of things relevant to phase I, such as its understanding of OpenAI’s

                                   4   obligations as a charitable trust and what they mean for what is legally permissible in its business

                                   5   operations, or Microsoft’s understanding of the obligations OpenAI has toward Musk. In its letter

                                   6   brief concerning summary judgment, Microsoft seems to agree these are relevant phase I issues.

                                   7   ECF No. 248. Accordingly, the Court cannot make a categorical finding that all documents or

                                   8   testimony that might be related to these subjects are irrelevant to phase I.

                                   9          As to the business strategy doctrine, Microsoft says nothing about Musk’s bid for OpenAI,

                                  10   and the Court does not see how Microsoft has any sort of argument that the business strategy

                                  11   doctrine applies as to the Plaintiffs. Microsoft’s sole focus is on the potential harm to its

                                  12   negotiating position if its sensitive documents were turned over to OpenAI while their current
Northern District of California
 United States District Court




                                  13   negotiations are ongoing. This is just the flip side of the same argument OpenAI made, and the

                                  14   Court’s conclusion is the same: The business strategy doctrine applies as between Microsoft and

                                  15   OpenAI.

                                  16          3.      What to Do About This
                                  17          As the Court sees it, there are two options about how to handle this situation. One is to

                                  18   take the approach the court took in Gioia and simply deny the requested discovery. Gioia, 1988

                                  19   WL 18224, at *3 (“I do not regard confidentiality orders as providing absolute protection. If they

                                  20   did, there would be no need to attempt to evaluate competing claims in this setting, for the words

                                  21   written on a page would afford the protection of a guarded vault. We must operate, however, in a

                                  22   world more closely aligned with a reality in which mistakes occur and in which trust is sometimes

                                  23   abused for advantage.”). The other is to modify the existing protective order so that the requested

                                  24   documents and information can be produced to the Plaintiffs without them falling into the wrong

                                  25   hands at either OpenAI or Microsoft.

                                  26          In opting for the latter approach, the Court is mindful that this discovery dispute arises in a

                                  27   context that the Court has not seen in any case applying the business strategy doctrine: The

                                  28   transaction that OpenAI and Microsoft are negotiating is the transaction Plaintiffs are seeking to
                                                                                          7
                                       Case 4:24-cv-04722-YGR            Document 251         Filed 09/09/25     Page 8 of 9




                                   1   enjoin in this case. In Defendants’ point of view, they are negotiating a high stakes investment in

                                   2   groundbreaking technology. In Plaintiffs’ point of view, they are culminating years of legal

                                   3   wrongdoing in a conspiracy to harm the Plaintiffs. Denying Plaintiffs this discovery seems to

                                   4   prejudge the case in Defendants’ favor. It seems profoundly unfair to deny Plaintiffs discovery

                                   5   that may be important to their claims just because Defendants are finalizing their plans on how to

                                   6   do the very thing Plaintiffs seek the Court’s aid to stop.

                                   7          The Court also has a structural concern that if the Court were to allow OpenAI and

                                   8   Microsoft to withhold documents and information not just from each other but also from Plaintiffs,

                                   9   this would incentivize broad claims of privilege aimed at denying the Plaintiffs discovery rather

                                  10   than genuinely protecting information from their co-Defendant. OpenAI and Microsoft’s

                                  11   positions on the business strategy doctrine do not seem to differentiate between documents that

                                  12   each does not want the other to see (a legitimate concern) and documents they don’t want
Northern District of California
 United States District Court




                                  13   Plaintiffs to have (not a legitimate concern, and one that the Court has rejected because Musk does

                                  14   not have a live bid). OpenAI has justified all of its claims of business strategy privilege on both

                                  15   grounds. Likewise, Microsoft seeks a broad protective order that would bar discovery into its

                                  16   internal deliberations and communications regarding its negotiating strategy or positions vis-à-vis

                                  17   OpenAI’s potential restructuring. Despite Microsoft characterizing that as a “narrow” request, it

                                  18   could be construed to capture all internal communications about OpenAI’s potential restructuring.

                                  19   The Gioia court wisely noted that “[w]e must operate . . . in a world more closely aligned with a

                                  20   reality in which mistakes occur and in which trust is sometimes abused for advantage.” Gioia,

                                  21   1988 WL 18224, at *3. Here, that concern counsels against letting OpenAI and Microsoft use the

                                  22   pending negotiation between them as a basis for denying Plaintiffs discovery to support the claims

                                  23   against them.

                                  24          For these reasons, the better approach is to allow OpenAI and Microsoft to withhold

                                  25   documents and information from each other pursuant to the business strategy doctrine, but not

                                  26   from Plaintiffs. Of course, OpenAI and Microsoft’s trial counsel in this action will need to receive

                                  27   those items so they can defend the case; the issue is appropriate restrictions beyond that to ensure

                                  28   that these items do not fall into the hands of people who should not have them. Accordingly, the
