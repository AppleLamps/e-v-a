 1          Pursuant to Civil Local Rule 79-5(f )(3), Plaintiff Elon Musk and non-party Shivon Zilis

 2 respectfully file this Response to the OpenAI Defendants’ (“OpenAI’s”) Administrative Motion To

 3 Consider Whether Another Party’s Material Should Be Sealed. Dkt. 426. Plaintiff and Ms. Zilis

 4 request limited redactions to OpenAI’s Opposition to Plaintiff ’s Motion in Limine No. 4 (the

 5 “Opposition”), as well as Exhibit B thereto (a text chain between Ms. Zilis and Mr. Musk) and

 6 Exhibit C (an excerpt from Ms. Zilis’s deposition transcript). The three documents are currently

 7 provisionally filed under seal at Dkts. 426-2, 426-5, and 426-6, respectively.

 8          Documents like these that are “not related, or only tangentially related, to the merits of a

 9 case” are not subject to any strong presumption of public access. Ctr. for Auto Safety v. Chrysler

10 Grp., LLC, 809 F.3d 1092, 1099 (9th Cir. 2016). Redactions are justified where “compelling reasons

11 sufficient to outweigh the public’s interest in disclosure” exist, including when “court files might

12 have become a vehicle for improper purposes, such as the use of records to gratify private spite,

13 promote public scandal, [or] circulate libelous statements.”      Kamakana v. City & County of

14 Honolulu, 447 F.3d 1172, 1179 (9th Cir. 2006) (internal quotation marks omitted). Compelling

15 reasons for redaction exist where “disclosure of the material could result in improper use of the

16 material.” Foltz v. State Farm Mut. Auto. Ins. Co., 331 F.3d 1122, 1135 (9th Cir. 2003).

17          Redaction is also justified where documents contain personal content, the disclosure of

18 which would be an unwarranted invasion of privacy. Courts regularly permit redaction on those

19 grounds. See, e.g., United States v. Nosal, No. 08-cr-0237, 2013 WL 11327121, at *9 (N.D. Cal.

20 Mar. 29, 2013) (permitting redactions to voicemails that “contain[ed] a combination of personal and

21 business information”); VLSI Tech. LLC v. Intel Corp., No. 17-cv-05671, 2023 WL 7472953, at *2

22 (N.D. Cal. Oct. 24, 2023) (permitting sealing of “personal information that is not relevant to any

23 matter in this case”); Apple Inc. v. Rivos, Inc., No. 22-cv-02637, 2024 WL 748394, at *5 (N.D. Cal.

24 Feb. 23, 2024) (similar). Courts similarly permit the redaction of personal identifying information

25 such as personal phone numbers. See Epic Games, Inc. v. Apple Inc., No. 4:20-cv-05640, 2021 WL

26 1925460, at *3 (N.D. Cal. Apr. 30, 2021) (Gonzalez Rogers, J.) (granting motion to seal that was

27 “narrowly tailored in redacting personal identifying information, including names, phone numbers,

28 and email addresses”).

 1          Redactions are justified for all those reasons here. Plaintiff explained the compelling reasons

 2 for sealing this type of material in his Administrative Motion To Seal Plaintiff ’s Motion in Limine

 3 No. 4 (the “Sealing Motion”), Dkt. 405, and Plaintiff ’s Motion in Limine No. 4 To Exclude

 4 Irrelevant Information About Plaintiff Elon Musk (the “Motion in Limine”), Dkt. 406. As described

 5 in the Motion in Limine, Plaintiff seeks to exclude from trial documents and testimony that include

 6 prejudicial, personal information that is irrelevant to the merits of any claim or defense. Dkt. 406

 7 at 1-2. OpenAI’s Opposition to the Motion references the same inflammatory material Plaintiff

 8 seeks to exclude from trial, and Exhibits B and C contain statements from Ms. Zilis that are personal

 9 in nature. See Dkts. 426-2, 426-5, 426-6. Given the heightened media attention to this case and the

10 nearness of trial, disclosure would not only be an unwarranted invasion of privacy but would also

11 risk tainting the jury pool’s ability to form an impartial view of the evidence. See Doe v. Rose, No.

12 CV-15-07503, 2016 WL 9107137, at *3-4 (C.D. Cal. Sept. 30, 2016) (“As the date set for trial

13 begins to approach, ‘the potential for prejudice becomes particularly acute.’ ”). Disclosure would

14 therefore deprive Plaintiff of the very relief he seeks in the Motion in Limine. At a minimum, the

15 Opposition and Exhibits B and C should remain sealed or redacted until the Motion in Limine is

16 decided.

17          This Court has already approved redaction of the same or similar material on personal

18 privacy grounds. During discovery, Magistrate Judge Hixson approved the same redactions to

19 Exhibit B that Plaintiff and Ms. Zilis seek here. Dkt. 288 (order); Dkt. 290-1 (redacted exhibit).

20 And at summary judgment, the Court permitted Plaintiff to redact portions from both Plaintiff ’s

21 deposition transcript and Ms. Zilis’s deposition transcript based on similar personal privacy

22 concerns. Dkt. 368-98 ¶¶ 6-7 (sealing declaration); Dkt. 375 (order); Dkt. 390 at 2 n.2 (order); Dkt.

23 391-1 (redacted exhibit); Dkt. 391-38 (redacted exhibit). Plaintiff and Ms. Zilis seek to redact the

24 same sorts of personal information here.

25          Consistent with Civil Local Rule 79-5(a), Plaintiff and Ms. Zilis request limited redactions

26 to OpenAI’s Opposition and Exhibits B and C. The proposed redactions are reflected in Exhibits 1,

27 2, and 3, attached to this motion. The redactions relate to the following content:
