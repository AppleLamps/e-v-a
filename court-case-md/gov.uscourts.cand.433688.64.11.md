25          A.     The Risk Of Artificial General Intelligence

26          15.    Over the course of the 20th century, the United States gradually shifted from a

27 primarily human labor-based economy to a primarily human knowledge-based economy, with

28 economic value increasingly created primarily by human intelligence. As the century progressed,

 1 another paradigm shift was already underway: value creation through artificial intelligence (AI).

 2 Early AI programs were capable of outperforming humans in certain discrete tasks. Almost

 3 immediately after the invention of the programmable computer, AI could show super-human

 4 performance on a highly formalized problem like finding the fastest path through a network of roads.

 5 It took longer for AI to reach superiority for problems requiring more creativity. In 1996, IBM’s

 6 Deep Blue AI program beat Gary Kasparov, the then-world champion in chess. These programs,

 7 while useful, were essentially one-trick-ponies—their intelligence was not general. Deep Blue had

 8 massive arrays of processors that could only play chess. Algorithms for path finding could solve a

 9 maze or route a car, but they could not paint a painting.

10          16.    Starting in the late 2000s and early 2010s, an older algorithm called “deep learning”

11 became practical to implement on low-cost hardware for the first time. This caused an almost

12 overnight revolution in performance across nearly all AI projects. New, top of class algorithms were

13 developed for turning speech into text, translating between languages, and recognizing what kind of

14 food is shown in a photo. One of the hallmarks of deep learning is that algorithms do not need to be

15 designed with significant knowledge of the task at hand. They learn each task from training

16 examples, essentially programming themselves. This means that they are far more general-purpose

17 than earlier systems like Deep Blue.

18          17.    As deep learning algorithms became increasingly sophisticated, some of the world’s

19 leading AI researchers set their sights on what has come to be called Artificial General Intelligence

20 (AGI). The basic concept of AGI is a general purpose artificial intelligence system—a machine

21 having intelligence for a wide variety of tasks like a human.

22          18.    Mr. Musk has long recognized that AGI poses a grave threat to humanity—perhaps

23 the greatest existential threat we face today. His concerns mirrored those raised before him by

24 luminaries like Stephen Hawking and Sun Microsystems founder Bill Joy. Our entire economy is

25 based around the fact that humans work together and come up with the best solutions to a hard task.

26 If a machine can solve nearly any task better than we can, that machine becomes more economically

27 useful than we are. As Mr. Joy warned, with strong AGI, “the future doesn’t need us.” Mr. Musk

 3          19.     But where some like Mr. Musk see an existential threat in AGI, others see AGI as a

 4 source of profit and power.

 5          20.     In 2014, Google acquired DeepMind, a research group focused on deep learning.

 6 One of DeepMind’s initial developments was AlphaZero, a chess playing algorithm. Unlike

 7 previous algorithms, however, AlphaZero used “reinforcement learning,” wherein the program

 8 learns to play chess by playing itself with different versions of the software. It starts by playing

 9 randomly, with no understanding of the game’s strategy. When one version of the software wins a

10 game against another, the winning program’s internal pathways are “reinforced” and the process

11 repeats.

12          21.     AlphaZero rapidly became the strongest chess playing system in the world.

13 Shockingly, it was also announced that the same program was also the strongest in the world for

14 playing two other extremely difficult games. In Google/DeepMind’s words, “Starting from random

15 play, and given no domain knowledge except the game rules, AlphaZero achieved within 24 hours

16 a superhuman level of play in the games of chess and shogi (Japanese chess) as well as Go, and

17 convincingly defeated a world-champion program in each case.”

18          22.     With the DeepMind team, Google immediately catapulted to the front of the race for

19 AGI. Mr. Musk was deeply troubled by this development. He believed (and still does) that in the

20 hands of a closed, for-profit company like Google, AGI poses a particularly acute and noxious

21 danger to humanity. In 2014, it was already difficult enough to compete with Google in its core

22 businesses. Google had collected a uniquely large set of data from our searches, our emails, and

23 nearly every book in our libraries. Nevertheless, up to this point, everyone had the potential to

24 compete with Google through superior human intelligence and hard work. AGI would make

25 competition nearly impossible.
