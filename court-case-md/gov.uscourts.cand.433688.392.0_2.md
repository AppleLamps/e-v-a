19          Pursuant to this Court’s direction following the January 7, 2026 summary judgment hearing,
20 Plaintiff Elon Musk respectfully submits this notice to set forth the principal remedies he seeks on

21 each of his claims.

22          The primary monetary remedy that Plaintiff seeks is disgorgement of wrongful gains, which
23 are substantial in this case. Under California law, disgorgement of wrongful gains is available for

24 each of the claims Plaintiff asserts. See Restatement (Third) of Restitution § 51(2), (4) (2011) (all

25 claims); In re Facebook, Inc. Internet Tracking Litig., 956 F.3d 589, 600 (9th Cir. 2020) (unjust

26 enrichment); Ward v. Taggart, 51 Cal. 2d 736, 741-42 (1959) (fraud); Am. Master Lease LLC v.

27 Idanta Partners, Ltd., 225 Cal. App. 4th 1451, 1483 (2014) (aiding and abetting); County of San

28 Bernardino v. Walsh, 158 Cal. App. 4th 533, 543 (2007) (breach of fiduciary duty). Wrongful gains

 4          Plaintiff ’s expert on this topic is Dr. C. Paul Wazzan. Dr. Wazzan is a financial economist

 5 with decades of professional and academic experience who has managed his own successful venture

 6 capital firm that provided seed-level funding to technology startups. Dr. Wazzan analyzes the

 7 wrongful gains that OpenAI and Microsoft earned as a result of Mr. Musk’s critical contributions to

 8 OpenAI from the time that Mr. Musk helped found it. Those contributions include the $38 million

 9 that Mr. Musk contributed during OpenAI’s early years: roughly 60% of the nonprofit’s seed

10 funding. They also include Mr. Musk’s numerous nonmonetary contributions: recruiting key

11 employees, introducing business contacts, teaching his cofounders everything he knew about

12 running a successful startup, and lending his prestige and reputation to the venture.

13          Canvassing the finance literature and his own professional experience, Dr. Wazzan explains

14 that Mr. Musk’s early contributions were critical to OpenAI’s success and ability to achieve the

15 $500 billion valuation that it reached this past year. As a result, just as an early investor in a startup

16 company may realize gains many orders of magnitude greater than the investor’s initial investment,

17 the wrongful gains that OpenAI and Microsoft have earned – and which Mr. Musk is now entitled

18 to disgorge – are much larger than Mr. Musk’s initial contributions.

19          Dr. Wazzan calculates the amount of OpenAI’s wrongful gains as the product of three

20 numbers: (1) the current value of the OpenAI for-profit entity, times (2) the OpenAI nonprofit’s

21 share of the for-profit, times (3) the portion of the nonprofit’s value that is fairly attributable to Mr.

22 Musk’s monetary and nonmonetary contributions (which Dr. Wazzan estimates at 50% to 75%). As

23 stated in Dr. Wazzan’s supplemental report, this equates to $65.50 billion to $109.43 billion in

24 wrongful gains for OpenAI. Dr. Wazzan makes a similar calculation for Microsoft’s wrongful gains,

25 although Microsoft’s gains are adjusted to deduct the costs of its investments and to adjust for

26 Microsoft’s ownership stake. This equates to $13.30 billion to $25.06 billion in wrongful gains for

27 Microsoft.

 1          Although Plaintiff seeks the same basic measure of wrongful gains on each count, the jury

 2 may need to adjust its award for certain claims depending in part on its findings concerning the

 3 statute of limitations, as discussed below. In addition, the jury should be instructed that it may

 4 award an amount different from what Dr. Wazzan calculates if it determines that Mr. Musk’s

 5 contributions to OpenAI were something other than 50% to 75% of the nonprofit’s value.

 6          The claim-by-claim analysis of Plaintiff ’s claims is as follows:

 7          1. Breach of Trust Against OpenAI. Plaintiff seeks disgorgement of wrongful gains in

 8 the full amount calculated by Dr. Wazzan for OpenAI as set forth above. In the event the jury finds

 9 that Mr. Musk discovered or should have discovered the breach of trust more than four years before

10 filing suit, the jury should be instructed to limit its award to any wrongful gains that resulted from

11 OpenAI’s ongoing or additional breaches of trust within four years of filing. Cal. Code Civ. Proc.

12 § 343; Aryeh v. Canon Bus. Sols., Inc., 55 Cal. 4th 1185, 1198-99 (2013).

13          2. Unjust Enrichment Against OpenAI. Plaintiff seeks disgorgement of wrongful gains

14 in the full amount calculated by Dr. Wazzan for OpenAI as set forth above. In the event the jury

15 finds that Mr. Musk discovered or should have discovered the unjust enrichment more than two

16 years before filing suit, the jury should be instructed to limit its award to any wrongful gains that

17 resulted from OpenAI’s ongoing unjust retention of benefits within two years of filing. Cal. Code

18 Civ. Proc. § 339 (per Dkt. 390 at 22-23); Aryeh, 55 Cal. 4th at 1198-99.

19          3. Fraud or Constructive Fraud Against OpenAI. Plaintiff seeks disgorgement of

20 wrongful gains. The jury should be instructed to award wrongful gains on this claim with respect

21 to the portion of contributions that Mr. Musk was induced to make following the date of the fraud.

22 In the event the jury finds that Mr. Musk discovered or should have discovered the fraud more than

23 three years before filing suit, the jury may be instructed to return a verdict on liability consistent

24 with that finding. Cal. Code Civ. Proc. § 338(d).

27 jury finds that Mr. Musk discovered or should have discovered the aiding and abetting more than
