                    1          Non-party Ilya Sutskever respectfully moves this court to shorten time for the Court to hear
                    2   his Motion to Stay (Dkt. 337), pursuant to Civil Local Rules 6–3(a)(4) and 7–11. Counsel for
                    3   Plaintiffs have agreed in writing that the Motion to Stay and Mr. Sutskever’s related Rule 72(a)
                    4   objection to the underlying discovery order should both be heard urgently.
                    5          The Motion to Stay seeks to stay an underlying discovery ordered issued by the Magistrate
                    6   Judge so that this Court has time to consider Mr. Sutskever’s objection to the order; that underlying
                    7   discovery order would require production of highly sensitive material to Plaintiffs and a second
                    8   deposition of Mr. Sutskever, so only a stay could obviate the prejudice of having to produce while
                    9   this Court considers Mr. Sutskever’s objection.
                   10          Unfortunately, Mr. Sutskever must request a ruling by November 4, 2025. Expedited
                   11   review is necessary because earlier today, after the Motion to Stay was filed, the Magistrate Judge
                   12   ordered Mr. Sutskever to comply with the underlying discovery order (Dkt. 324), whether or not
                   13   this Court has ruled on the Motion to Stay; the new order sets a deadline for compliance of
                   14   November 5. Dkt. 339. Mr. Sutskever reluctantly makes such a request, especially during the
                   15   government shutdown. But the effect of the Magistrate Judge’s new order is to, essentially, set a
                   16   deadline for this Court to act, lest Mr. Sutskever’s rights be infringed without this Court having an
                   17   opportunity to pass on his objection to the Magistrate Judge’s underlying discovery order.
                   18          The new deadline forces Mr. Sutskever to comply long before this Court can review his
                   19   Rule 72 objections. As courts in this Circuit have found, this would effectively deny him the relief
                   20   he seeks. See Alvarez v. Larose, 2020 WL 5632659, at *2 (S.D. Cal. Sept. 21, 2020) (“[R]equiring
                   21   the [compelled discovery] to go forward by the existing deadline . . . might preclude the district
                   22   judge’s ability to review Respondents’ anticipated Rule 72 objection, which would effectively
                   23   deprive Respondents of their right to reconsideration of the Discovery Order by the district judge
                   24   altogether.”); see also Jones v. PGA Tour, Inc., 2023 WL 2541326, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 15, 2023);
                   25   Forrest v. Facebook, Inc., 2023 WL 1931356, at *2 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 18, 2023). Compliance without
                   26   a ruling from this Court will thus cause irreparable harm and moot Mr. Sutskever’s Rule 72(a)
                   27   objection.

                    1   I, Eduardo E. Santacana, declare as follows:
                    2          1.      On October 28, 2025, Plaintiffs filed a Motion For Order To Show Cause Why
                    3   Contempt Citation Should Not Issue Against Non-Party Ilya Sutskever (Dkt. 335) (“Motion for
                    4   Contempt”). Plaintiffs filed this motion without meeting and conferring with Mr. Sutskever’s
                    5   counsel regarding contempt or sanctions. Plaintiffs’ Motion for Contempt seeks immediate
                    6   compliance with the October 17, 2025 Discovery Order (Dkt. 324) and requests sanctions against
                    7   Mr. Sutskever, even though Mr. Sutskever is exercising his right to seek review of that Order
                    8   under Rule 72(a).
                    9          2.      Due to Plaintiffs’ Motion for Contempt, Mr. Sutskever was compelled to
                   10   immediately file the Motion to Stay and the Rule 72(a) Objection to protect his rights before they
                   11   were mooted or prejudiced by the then-pending contempt proceedings.
                   12          3.      Mr. Sutskever filed that Motion to Stay on the night of October 28, 2025, just after
                   13   midnight. The next morning, at 4:13 a.m. pacific time, counsel for Plaintiffs agreed in writing,
                   14   pursuant to this Court’s temporary standing order regarding the government shutdown, that
                   15   Plaintiffs also wish for the Motion to Stay and Mr. Sutskever’s Rule 72(a) objection to the
                   16   underlying discovery order both be heard urgently.
                   17          4.      On October 29, Magistrate Judge Hixson ordered Mr. Sutskever to comply with
                   18   the underlying discovery order (Dkt. 324), whether or not this Court has ruled on the Motion to
                   19   Stay, by November 5. Dkt. 339.
                   20          5.      Thus, if this Court has not ruled on Mr. Sutskever’s Motion to Stay before
                   21   November 5, Mr. Sutskever faces the possibility of being effectively denied his right to review
                   22   under Rule 72(a).
                   23          6.      At 8:10 p.m. pacific time on October 29, 2025, my team emailed Plaintiffs’
                   24   counsel, Jennifer Schubert, to meet and confer regarding the urgency of the Motion to Stay in
                   25   light of the new deadline for compliance. In that email, my team requested that Ms. Schubert
                   26   confirm whether they would stipulate to an expedited briefing schedule on Mr. Sutskever’s
                   27   Motion to Stay with Plaintiffs’ response due October 31, 2025, and a hearing set for November 3,
                   28   2025, at 10:00 a.m.
