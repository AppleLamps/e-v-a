 1                            STATEMENT OF ISSUES TO BE DECIDED

 2            1.     Whether Musk’s contract claims (Counts I, II, III and V) should be dismissed for

 3   failure to adequately allege the formation of an enforceable contract through mutual assent

 4   and bargained-for consideration, or the breach of any contractual terms.

 5            2.     Whether Plaintiffs’ fraud claims (Counts IV, VII, and VIII) should be dismissed

 6   (a) for failure to plead with particularity that any OpenAI Defendant made a cognizable promise to

 7   Musk, (b) for failure to plead with particularity the other elements of fraud, including falsity and

 8   fraudulent intent, and (c) as time-barred under the applicable three-year statute of limitations for

 9   fraud.

10            3.     Whether Plaintiffs’ civil RICO claims (Counts XXV and XXVI) should be

11   dismissed for failure to plead any underlying predicate act of wire fraud with particularity, and for

12   the independent reasons that Plaintiffs have failed to adequately plead (a) conduct of an enterprise

13   under 18 U.S.C. § 1962(c); (b) injury proximately caused by the investment of racketeering income

14   under 18 U.S.C. § 1962(a); (c) injury proximately caused by control of OpenAI under 18 U.S.C.

15   § 1962(b); or (d) a conspiracy to violate civil RICO under 18 U.S.C. § 1962(d).

16            4.     Whether Musk’s claims for breach of charitable trust (Count XX), constructive

17   fraud (Count VI), and aiding and abetting breach of fiduciary duty to Musk (Count XXI) should be

18   dismissed for lack of standing and any well-pleaded allegation of breach.

19            5.     Whether Plaintiffs’ putative derivative claims (Counts XXII-XXIV) should be

20   dismissed for lack of standing and for failure to adequately plead breach of fiduciary duty or

21   self-dealing.

22            6.     Whether Plaintiffs’ false advertising claims (Counts XVIII and XIX) should be

23   dismissed because Plaintiffs: (a) identify no commercial advertisement or statement made primarily

24   out of economic motivation; and (b) fail to allege particularized facts to support these claims.

25            7.     Whether Plaintiffs’ antitrust claims (Counts IX-XVI) should be dismissed for failure

26   to plead (a) injury to competition, as necessary to establish antitrust standing; and (b) any

27   well-pleaded antitrust violation.

28            8.     Whether Plaintiffs’ accessary claims for tortious interference (Count V); aiding and

11    III.   MUSK’S BREACH OF CHARITABLE TRUST CLAIM FAILS.

12           A.      Musk lacks standing to bring a breach of charitable trust claim (Count XX).

13           By statute, only a member, officer, or director of a non-profit, a party “with a reversionary,

14    contractual, or property interest in” the entity’s assets, or the Attorney General (or a relator) has

15    standing to sue a non-profit for breach of trust. Cal. Corp. Code § 5142(a)(1)-(5); Horiike v.

16    Humane Soc’y of the U.S., 2016 WL 11744969, at *16-17 (C.D. Cal. June 20, 2016). This statutory

17    scheme “limits the persons who can sue for mismanagement of a charitable corporation’s assets,

18    and a donor like [Musk] is not among them”—“[d]onors who ‘parted with their interest in’ and

19    ‘control over’ their donated assets” generally have “no standing to complain.” Pinkert v. Schwab

27    property.” Patton v. Sherwood, 152 Cal. App. 4th 339, 342 (2007). Musk now admits he has no

28    such interest. See Dkt. 73, at 18 (disclaiming “reversionary interest”). That is dispositive.

 1    VIII. PLAINTIFFS’ UCL CLAIM (COUNT XVII) FAILS.

 2           Plaintiffs’ claim under California’s Unfair Competition Law, Cal Bus. & Prof. Code

 3    §§ 17200 et seq. (Count XVII) is the pinnacle of “shotgun pleading” and should be dismissed on

 4    that basis alone. See Dreifort v. DJO Global Inc., 2019 WL 5578240, at *7 (S.D. Cal. Oct. 28,

 5    2019); Baba v. Hewlett-Packard Co., 2010 WL 2486353, at *6 (N.D. Cal. June 16, 2010). The

 6    claim quite literally alleges “everyone did everything,” Destfino, 630 F.3d at 958, naming 26

 7    identifiable defendants and 100 “John Does,” while throwing the spaghetti and the kitchen sink at

 8    all of them—supposed tax violations (13 state and federal statutes); merger notification issues (for

 9    a non-existent merger); employment law claims; copyright infringement; trademark infringement,

10    and many others (¶¶ 384-87)—all without any attempt to cogently differentiate who did what,

11    when, or why, let alone how any of this could run afoul of any recognized legal prohibition.

12           The indiscriminate pleading is all the more egregious because Plaintiffs do not even have

13    standing. Their attempt to reassert breach of charitable trust (¶ 384) has no grounding in law. See

14    Pinkert, 2021 WL 2476869, at *6 (plaintiff’s “lack[] [of] standing” to bring claims for breach of

15    charitable trust “dispos[ed] of [his] UCL claim too”); Berryman v. Merit Property Management,

16    152 Cal. App. 4th 1544, 1555 (2007) (plaintiff cannot “bootstrap” a claim it lacks standing to pursue

17    into a UCL claim). And Plaintiffs cannot plausibly allege “injury in fact and a loss of money or

18    property caused by” their grab-bag of other theories. Durell v. Sharp Healthcare, 183 Cal. App.

19    4th 1350, 1359 (2010); Drum v. San Fernando Valley Bar Ass’n, 182 Cal. App. 4th 247, 252 (2010).

20           Equally fatal, Plaintiffs do not bother to specify which prong of the UCL they intend to

21    assert, relying instead on an improper “and/or” formulation (¶ 387). See Campbell v. eBay, Inc.,

22    2013 WL 4764526, at *5 (N.D. Cal. Sept. 5, 2013). And Plaintiffs’ “laundry list of state and federal”

23    statutory violations are improperly alleged as pure conclusions of law. Berryman, 152 Cal. App.

24    4th at 1554; see also Kearns v. Ford Motor Co., 567 F.3d 1120, 1127 (9th Cir. 2009). Finally, to

25    the extent it is predicated on the FAC’s other defective claims (¶ 387(a)), the UCL claims fails for

26    all of the reasons above. See Baba, 2010 WL 2486353, at *6.

27                                             CONCLUSION

28           The Court should grant the motion to dismiss without leave to amend.
