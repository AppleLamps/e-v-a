           DECLARATION OF DAVID J. WIENER IN SUPPORT OF OPENAI DEFENDANTS’ ADMINISTRATIVE MOTION
                                        CASE NO. 4:24-CV-04722-YGR
 1           I, DAVID J. WIENER, declare as follows:
 2           1.     I am a member of the bar of the State of California and am admitted to practice
 3   before this Court. I am a partner at the law firm of Morrison & Foerster LLP and counsel of record
 4   for the OpenAI Defendants. I submit this declaration in support of the OpenAI Defendants’
 5   administrative motion to set a briefing and hearing schedule for Daubert motions. Unless otherwise
 6   stated, I have personal knowledge of the facts set forth below and could competently testify to them.
 7           2.     By agreement of Plaintiff Elon Musk, the OpenAI Defendants, and Defendant
 8   Microsoft Corporation, expert discovery in this action concluded on December 23, 2025.
 9           3.     On December 31, 2025, counsel for the OpenAI Defendants emailed counsel for
10   Musk and Microsoft to discuss a proposed briefing schedule for Daubert motions, among other
11   pretrial matters. Attached as Exhibit 1 is a true and correct copy of this email.
12           4.     Counsel for the parties met and conferred on January 2, 2026, regarding a stipulated
13   schedule for pretrial Daubert motions. During that meet and confer, the OpenAI Defendants
14   proposed the schedule set forth in their accompanying motion. While counsel for Microsoft
15   expressed support for the proposal, counsel for Musk indicated they needed additional time to
16   consider it.
17           5.     The next day, on January 3, 2026, counsel for Musk expressed reservation about
18   setting a schedule for briefing Daubert motions in advance of trial and suggested that the parties
19   raise the topic with the Court during the motion for summary judgment hearing scheduled for
20   January 7, 2026. Specifically, Musk’s counsel wrote: “Given the absence of a schedule for Daubert
21   briefing in the current case scheduling order and the language about ‘[n]o other pretrial motions are
22   authorized’ in the standing pretrial order, we are inclined to defer to the Court about whether she
23   wants to receive Daubert briefing before trial in this expedited matter. We suggest raising the issue
24   on Wednesday. If the Court wants to receive Daubert briefing, your proposed schedule is fine by
25   us.” Attached as Exhibit 2 is a true and correct copy of this email.
26           6.     Because the parties were unable to reach agreement on a stipulated pretrial briefing
27   schedule for Daubert motions, on January 5, 2026, the OpenAI Defendants informed Musk’s
28   counsel that they intended to file an administrative motion requesting that the Court adopt the

 1   proposed schedule and page limits for Daubert motions. The OpenAI Defendants requested Musk’s

 2   position on the administrative motion. Attached as Exhibit 3 is a true and correct copy of this email.

 3            7.      Later on January 5, 2026, Musk’s counsel asked the OpenAI Defendants to clarify

 4   their proposal with respect to the page limits applicable to Daubert briefing. Attached as Exhibit 4

 5   is a true and correct copy of this email.

 6            8.      After observing that the Court’s Standing Order in Civil Cases allows each side to

 7   file three Daubert motions without leave of Court, the OpenAI Defendants stated that their

 8   proposed page limits were per motion. Attached as Exhibit 5 is a true and correct copy of this

 9   email.

10            9.      At 2:00 p.m. ET on January 5, 2026, Musk’s counsel provided his position regarding

11   the proposed Daubert schedule and offered language for possible inclusion in the administrative

12   motion:

13            Plaintiffs defer to this Court’s judgment over whether pretrial Daubert briefing
              would be helpful or desirable given the expedited case schedule in this matter. The
14            existing schedule does not expressly provide for Daubert briefing, and Plaintiffs are
              concerned that Daubert briefing at this point (along with another round of omnibus
15
              sealing submissions) would impose significant burdens on the Court and the parties
16            given trial preparation obligations and the many rapidly approaching pretrial
              deadlines. Although Plaintiffs object to certain expert testimony that OpenAI and
17            Microsoft propose to offer, Plaintiffs are confident that they can adequately address
              those issues at trial. If the Court would nonetheless prefer to address these issues
18            before trial, Plaintiffs do not object to OpenAI’s proposed briefing schedule, but the
              proposed page limits are too long given the expedited nature of this case. The briefs
19
              should instead be limited to one 25-page opening brief, one 25-page opposition
20            brief, and one 10-page reply per side for all Daubert motions combined.

21   Attached as Exhibit 6 is a true and correct copy of this email.

22            10.     The OpenAI Defendants therefore filed this administrative motion because the

23   parties were unable to agree to a stipulated pretrial schedule for Daubert motions after meeting and

24   conferring.
