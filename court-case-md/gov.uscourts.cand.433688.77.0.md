7    I.       LEGAL STANDARD

8             District courts have broad discretion to grant leave to participate as amicus curiae. See
9    Hoptowit v. Ray, 682 F.2d 1237, 1260 (9th Cir. 1982), abrogated on other grounds by Sandin
10   v. Conner, 515 U.S. 472 (1995); see also Woodfin Suite Hotels, LLC v. City of Emeryville, No.
11   C-06-1254, 2007 WL 81911, at *3 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 9, 2007) (“Whether to allow Amici to file a
12   brief is solely within the Court’s discretion, and generally courts have exercised great
13   liberality” in granting amicus participation. (internal quotation marks and citation omitted)).
14   “District courts frequently welcome amicus briefs from non-parties concerning legal issues
15   that have potential ramifications beyond the parties directly involved or if the amicus has
16   unique information or perspective that can help the court beyond the help that the lawyers for
17   the parties are able to provide.” NGV Gaming, Ltd. v. Upstream Point Molate, LLC, 355 F.
18   Supp. 2d 1061, 1067 (N.D. Cal. 2005) (internal quotation marks and citation omitted). “[T]he

19   classic role of amicus curiae” is to “assist[] in a case of general public

20   interest, . . . supplement[] the efforts of counsel, and draw[] the court’s attention to law that

3    II.       THE DELAWARE ATTORNEY GENERAL’S INTEREST IN THIS MATTER

4              The Delaware Attorney General has an interest in the subject matter and legal

5    implications of the controversy before this Court because one of the Defendants in this matter,

6    OpenAI, Inc. (“OpenAI”), is a Delaware charitable nonstock corporation that was organized

7    entirely for not-for-profit purposes and Plaintiffs are seeking, inter alia, to preliminarily enjoin

8    a transaction alleged to involve the conversion of OpenAI into a for-profit entity (the
9    “Proposed Transaction”). See ECF 46. 2
10             The Delaware Attorney General has the authority to review transactions by charitable

11   nonstock corporations incorporated in the State. Oberly v. Kirby, 592 A.2d 445, 468 (Del.

12   1991) (citing Wier v. Howard Hughes Med. Inst., 407 A.2d 1051, 1057 (Del. Ch. 1979)).

13   “[T]he test as to the legality of action taken by the governing board of a [charitable] corporation

14   is to be determined in accordance with principles of corporate law.” Id. at 466 (quoting

15   Denckla v. Indep. Found., 193 A.2d 538, 541 (Del. 1963)). In the event the Delaware Attorney

16   General determines that the transaction is contrary to Delaware law, she has the power to seek

17   an injunction in Delaware state court. See id. at 468.

18             The Delaware Attorney General moves to submit an amicus brief to make the Court

19   aware that she and her office are actively reviewing the Proposed Transaction and will take

20   appropriate actions to protect the public interest, including seeking an injunction, if she

21   determines that the Proposed Transaction is contrary to Delaware law or the public good.
