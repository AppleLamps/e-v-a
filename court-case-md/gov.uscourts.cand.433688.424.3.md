 3          RESPONSE TO INTERROGATORY NO. 13:

 4          Subject to and without waiving the foregoing objections, in response to Interrogatory

 5   No. 13, OpenAI, Inc. states that on November 16, 2023, Adam D’Angelo, Tasha McCauley, Ilya

 6   Sutskever, and Helen Toner, acting as members of OpenAI, Inc., voted to remove Altman and

 7   Brockman as members and board directors of OpenAI, Inc. The same day, pursuant to a consent

 8   of the sole and managing member of OpenAI OpCo, LLC (“OpCo”), signed by D’Angelo,

 9   McCauley, Sutskever, and Toner as representatives of OpenAI, Inc., Altman was terminated as

10   CEO of OpCo. The remaining board members of OpenAI, Inc. subsequently informed then-Chief

11   Technology Officer, Mira Murati, that they had appointed her as interim CEO.

12          On November 17, 2023, D’Angelo, McCauley, Sutskever, and Toner communicated their

13   actions to Altman and Brockman by videoconference. Altman and Brockman had no advance

14   notice of the actions or of the deliberations that preceded them. The leadership changes were

15   announced the same day in a post to the OpenAI blog which said that “Mr. Altman’s departure

16   follows a deliberative review process by the board, which concluded that he was not consistently

17   candid in his communications with the board, hindering its ability to exercise its responsibilities.”

18          On November 18, 2023, Murati, OpCo Chief Strategy Officer Jason Kwon, and OpCo Chief

19   Operating Officer Brad Lightcap sought to negotiate with D’Angelo, McCauley, Sutskever, and

20   Toner for the reinstatement of Altman and Brockman and for the current directors’ resignations.

21   That same day, members of the OpenAI, Inc. board asked to negotiate directly with Altman

22   concerning the return of Altman and Brockman and a reconstitution of the board. With the assent

23   of both the board and OpCo management, former Salesforce Co-CEO and former Twitter board

24   chair Bret Taylor became involved in the negotiations for a reconstituted OpenAI, Inc. board. On

25   November 19, 2023, Taylor confirmed he was willing to be chair of a reconstituted board—an

26   outcome supported by both OpCo management and the outside directors.

 1          By November 20, 2023, more than 700 of OpenAI’s 770 employees, including Sutskever

 2   and Murati, had signed an open letter seeking reinstatement of Altman and Brockman and urging

 3   the current board to resign.

 4          On November 21, 2023, Altman and Brockman reached an agreement in principle with

 5   interim OpCo management and the board to reinstate Altman as CEO, seat a reconstituted board

 6   consisting of Taylor, Lawrence Summers, and D’Angelo (with accompanying resignations from

 7   McCauley, Sutskever, and Toner), and commit to an independent review of the events surrounding

 8   the removal of Altman and Brockman. The agreement in principle was announced publicly.

 9          From November 21 through November 29, 2023, D’Angelo, McCauley, Sutskever, and

10   Toner, through counsel, negotiated with Altman, Brockman, Taylor, and Summers the detailed

11   terms of the agreement that had been reached in principle. On November 29, 2023, the agreement

12   that had been reached in principle on November 21 was finalized. Altman announced on OpenAI’s

13   blog that he would be returning as CEO, that the new board would be comprised of Taylor,

14   Summers, and D’Angelo, and that Microsoft would have a non-voting observer role on the board.

15   Taylor, as chair of the reconstituted board, made an announcement on the blog as well, expressing

16   the board’s plan to expand the board and strengthen corporate governance.

17          On November 29, 2023, Altman once again became CEO of OpCo. The same day, the

18   OpenAI, Inc. board resolved to form a committee comprised of Taylor and Summers to conduct a

19   review, with the scope of the review to be determined by the committee. On December 8, 2023,

20   the committee retained the law firm of WilmerHale to assist with the review.

21          On March 8, 2024, following dozens of interviews with current and former members of the

22   OpenAI, Inc. board, OpenAI executives, advisors to the prior board, and other witnesses, as well

23   as review of more than 30,000 documents, WilmerHale concluded its review and delivered to the

24   committee its findings. As OpenAI, Inc. disclosed at the time: “WilmerHale reviewed the public

25   post issued by the prior Board on November 17 and concluded that the statement accurately

26   recounted the prior Board’s decision and rationales. WilmerHale found that the prior Board

27   believed at the time that its actions would mitigate internal management challenges and did not

28   anticipate that its actions would destabilize the Company. WilmerHale also found that the prior
