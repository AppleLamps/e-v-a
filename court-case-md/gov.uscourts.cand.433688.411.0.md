 1   The Court, having considered Motions in Limine filed by the OpenAI Defendants (“OpenAI”) and
 2   Microsoft, and all papers and arguments submitted in support and opposition thereto, hereby
 3   ORDERS as follows:
 4          OpenAI Defendants’ Motion in Limine No. 1: The Court GRANTS OpenAI’s Motion in
 5   Limine No. 1 and precludes all testimony from Plaintiff’s proffered expert Dr. Stuart Russell. Dr.
 6   Russell’s Opinions 1-4 do not satisfy the “fit” requirement of Federal Rule of Evidence 702(a) and
 7   will not “help the trier of fact to understand the evidence or to determine a fact in issue.” Fed. R.
 8   Evid. 702(a). Dr. Russell’s Opinions 1-4 are independently excluded under Federal Rule of
 9   Evidence 403. Dr. Russell’s Opinion 3 is independently excluded under Federal Rule of Evidence
10   702(a)-(c) and Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 26(a)(2)(B) as unreliable and unsupported. Dr.
11   Russell’s Opinion 4 is independently excluded under Federal Rule of Evidence 702(a)-(c) as
12   unreliable, speculative, and unsupported.
13          OpenAI Defendants’ Motion in Limine No. 2: The Court GRANTS OpenAI’s Motion in
14   Limine No. 2. Plaintiff’s trial exhibits 324 (MIL 2, Ex. A), 363 (MIL 2, Ex. B), and 362 (MIL 2,
15   Ex. C) are excluded as inadmissible hearsay. Plaintiff’s counsel is precluded from questioning
16   witnesses concerning these exhibits at trial, or from designating deposition testimony based on or
17   incorporating the contents of these exhibits (including MIL 2 Ex. E 81:21-82:8; MIL 2 Ex. G at
18   155:5-157:14 and similar designated testimony) for use at trial.
19          Microsoft’s Motion in Limine No. 1: The Court GRANTS Microsoft’s Motion in Limine
20   No. 1 and precludes Plaintiff from offering any evidence or argument relating to Microsoft’s
21   Damages expert Dr. Arnold’s family law related litigations and associated judicial findings.
22          Microsoft’s Motion in Limine No. 2: The Court GRANTS Microsoft’s Motion in Limine
23   No. 2 and precludes Plaintiff’s expert Professor David M. Schizer from offering testimony
24   assessing the propriety of Microsoft’s conduct in its dealings with OpenAI. This includes but is
25   not limited to the express testimony that he provided in his deposition that Microsoft took advantage
26   of OpenAI’s alleged misconduct and acted improperly by: (1) offering to hire OpenAI employees
27   after Mr. Altman’s November 2023 firing; (2) suggesting or “vetoing” board candidates for
