 1 I.       BREACH OF CHARITABLE TRUST

 2          A.     Existence of a Charitable Trust

 3          1.     For many years, Elon Musk harbored deep concerns about the development of

 4 artificial intelligence (“AI”). He feared that for-profit companies developing AI – especially the

 5 industry leader, Google – would obtain unilateral control over the technology, creating grave risks

 6 for humanity. Musk was concerned that those companies were not taking those risks seriously.

 7          2.     In early 2015, Musk expressed his concerns about AI safety to Sam Altman, who

 8 professed to share Musk’s fears. Over the next several months, Musk, Altman, and Greg Brockman

 9 discussed how to develop artificial intelligence safely.

10          3.     Musk, Altman, and Brockman ultimately agreed to found a nonprofit organization

11 dedicated to developing AI safely for the benefit of all humanity. During their discussions, Altman

12 repeatedly assured Musk that he agreed with Musk’s priority of establishing a nonprofit that would

13 open-source its technology, sharing it freely for others to use.

14          4.     In May 2015, Altman wrote Musk, proposing that they “structure [the entity] so that

15 the tech belongs to the world via some sort of nonprofit.” PX003. He later told Musk that the

16 “technology would be owned by the foundation and used ‘for the good of the world’ ” and that

17 “safety should be a first-class requirement.” PX005.

18          5.     Musk agreed to be the nonprofit’s primary donor. Musk explicitly conditioned his

19 funding on the organization being structured as a nonprofit committed to distributing its technology

20 broadly for the benefit of humanity. Altman agreed, writing to Musk that the charity’s mission

21 would be “to create the first general AI and use it for individual empowerment – ie, the distributed

22 version of the future that seems the safest.” PX005.

23          6.     Musk reiterated the importance of those funding conditions throughout 2015. He

24 explained that “governance” was “critical” because he “d[id]n’t want to fund something that goes

25 in what turns out to be the wrong direction.” PX007. Altman confirmed that an independent

26 501(c)(3) structure was “correct.” PX012.

21          B.      Breach of Charitable Trust

22          21.     In 2017, OpenAI’s founders became concerned that developing artificial general

23 intelligence (“AGI”) – advanced AI technology surpassing ordinary human intelligence – would

24 require more resources than a nonprofit could raise through charitable donations. In particular,

25 OpenAI needed vast amounts of computing power, or “compute,” to develop and train its AI models.

26          22.     Musk, Altman, Brockman, and OpenAI’s Chief Scientist Ilya Sutskever discussed

27 various ways to obtain more funding. Among the ideas they considered were partnering with an

28 existing for-profit company, forming a for-profit affiliate, or restructuring in some other manner.
