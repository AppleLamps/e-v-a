 1                                           INTRODUCTION
 2           Plaintiffs’ motion to strike every one of OpenAI’s defenses is nothing more than a tactical

 3   maneuver to continue to frustrate discovery in an expedited case that Plaintiffs brought. Motions to

 4   strike are “generally disfavored” by courts, including in this District, because they are “often used

 5   as delaying tactics.” That is what is happening here.

 6           The substantial completion deadline is four days away, and neither Elon Musk nor xAI has

 7   produced a single document that was collected to respond to discovery served in this litigation.

 8   Plaintiffs refused to even collect and search Musk’s email accounts until Magistrate Judge Hixson

 9   ordered them to do so nine days ago. Plaintiffs still have not produced any of those documents.

10           This motion—filed 18 days before the substantial completion deadline, with an

11   accompanying hearing date after the close of fact discovery—is part of this pattern of recalcitrance.

12   Plaintiffs are now deploying the motion to justify withholding all discovery into each of the OpenAI

13   Defendants’ defenses. This gamesmanship should not be countenanced; the motion provides no

14   such basis:

15           First, the motion’s premise is false. The OpenAI Defendants did not respond to a “narrowed

16   pleading” with an “exponentially expanded Answer.” MTS 2. The OpenAI Defendants’ answer to

17   the Second Amended Complaint contended with eight times as many claims as its original answer,

18   which     responded     to   only    Plaintiffs’   claim    for   breach    of    charitable   trust.

19   See infra Point I.

20           Second, Plaintiffs are wrong that pleading so-called “negative defenses” is impermissible,

21   as Rule 8 and case law make clear. In any event, Plaintiffs cannot show the prejudice required to

22   justify their motion to strike: as Plaintiffs concede, these defenses target elements of their own

23   claims, which they need to establish and on which they bear the burden. See infra Point II.

24           Third, far from lacking “the most rudimentary factual basis,” MTS 1, the OpenAI

25   Defendants’ affirmative defenses are supported by over 100 paragraphs of detailed factual

26   allegations and more than 400 responses to Plaintiffs’ verified allegations—all ignored by

27   Plaintiffs’ motion. These factual allegations and responses more than suffice to put Plaintiffs on

28   notice of these defenses, particularly given that discovery remains ongoing. See infra Point III.

 2                                               ARGUMENT

 3           “Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 12(f) provides that a court ‘may strike from a pleading an

 4    insufficient defense or any redundant, immaterial, impertinent, or scandalous matter.’” Free Speech

 5    Sys., LLC v. Menzel, 390 F. Supp. 3d 1162, 1168-69 (N.D. Cal. 2019) (quoting Fed. R. Civ.

 6    P. 12(f)). “Motions to strike” under this Rule “are generally disfavored because they are often used

 7    as delaying tactics,” especially where they do not serve their principal “function” of “avoid[ing]

 8    the expenditure of time and money that must arise from litigating spurious issues by dispensing of

 9    those issues before trial.” Id. at 1169 (cleaned up). “[B]ecause Rule 12(f) motions are disfavored,

10    courts often require a showing of prejudice by the moving party before granting the requested

11    relief.” Fabian v. LeMahieu, 2020 WL 3402800, at *4 (N.D. Cal. June 19, 2020).

12           “The key to determining the sufficiency of pleading an affirmative defense” under

13    Rule 12(f) “is whether it gives plaintiff fair notice of the defense.” Id. “[S]trik[ing] an affirmative

14    defense” is appropriate only where “under no set of circumstances could the defense succeed,”

15    “view[ing] the pleadings in a light most favorable to the pleading party.” Rodgers v. Claim Jumper

16    Rest., LLC, 2014 WL 1760959, at *1 (N.D. Cal. May 1, 2014). “Where a court strikes an affirmative

17    defense, leave to amend should be freely given so long as there is no prejudice to the moving party.”

18    Id. But “[i]n most cases, a motion to strike should not be granted unless the matter to be stricken

19    clearly could have no possible bearing on the subject of the litigation.” Free Speech Sys., 390 F.

20    Supp. 3d at 1169.
