 1                      MEMORANDUM OF POINTS AND AUTHORITIES

 2   I.      INTRODUCTION

 3           Microsoft brings this administrative motion pursuant to Civil Local Rules 7-11 and 79-

 4   5 to protect its employees’ security and privacy during the upcoming public trial.1

 5           Pursuant to Pretrial Order No. 1, the parties met and conferred on trial exhibit sealing.

 6   Dkt. 446 at 9(c) (March 16, 2026). Those discussions were productive: Microsoft and Plaintiff

 7   reached agreement on Microsoft’s narrow redactions of competitively sensitive business

 8   information. Only one issue remains unresolved as to Microsoft: how to handle its employees’

 9   business contact information—particularly email addresses—on exhibits displayed to the

10   public during trial.

11           Plaintiff’s position on that issue is inconsistent and inexplicable. Plaintiff has agreed to

12   redact personal contact information from publicly posted exhibits and from the public-facing

13   screens. Plaintiff has also agreed that business contact information may be redacted from

14   publicly posted exhibits. But for whatever reason, Plaintiff insists that the parties display that

15   same sensitive information on public-facing gallery screens, claiming that technical reasons

16   and purported burden make it infeasible to do otherwise. Plaintiff has provided no defensible

17   rationale for this distinction, and there is none.

18           Microsoft employees’ email addresses and contact information are not necessary or

19   relevant to Plaintiff’s case and the requested redactions will not prejudice his case in any way.

20   But not redacting them will expose these Microsoft employees—many of whom are not even

21   witnesses in the case—to serious security and privacy risks. And it will do so at a time when

22   the threat of doxxing is very real, and the animosity and temperature around AI is high.

23   Plaintiff’s refusal to take a consistent redaction approach to Microsoft employees’ email

24   addresses completely disregards these serious risks. Nor are these risks merely hypothetical:

25   this is a high-profile, highly public case that involves issues of heightened attention in the

26   public discourse. There is no question that public disclosure of Microsoft employees’ email

     II.       LEGAL STANDARD
16
               While there is a “strong presumption in favor of public access” to trial records, the
17
     Ninth Circuit recognizes that the right is not absolute. Kamakana v. City & Cty. Of Honolulu,
18
     447 F.3d 1172, 1178-79 (9th Cir. 2006). Redacting trial records requires that the requesting
19
     party demonstrates there are “compelling reasons supported by specific factual findings that
20
     outweigh the general history of access and the public policies favoring disclosure.” Id.
21
     III.      ARGUMENT

23

24
     consistently recognized that redacting this type of information satisfies the “compelling
25
     reasons” standard. Snapkeys, Ltd. v. Google LLC, No. 19-CV-02658-LHK, 2021 WL 1951250,

 5   12977024, at *3 (N.D. Cal. Dec. 2, 2015) (work email addresses of party employees “not

 6   relevant to the merits”); In re Hydroxycut Mktg. & Sales Pracs. Litig., No. 09MD2087 BTM

 8   because it “could expose [deponent] to unnecessary harassment”). Indeed, “publicizing” an

 9   employee’s work contact information “cannot be remedied after the fact if there is risk of

10   harassment or other unwanted contact by third parties.” In re Soc. Media Adolescent

11   Addiction/Pers. Inj. Prods. Liab. Litig., No. 22MD03047YGRPHK, 2024 WL 1808607, at *3

12   (N.D. Cal. Apr. 25, 2024). This is especially so in a high-profile, highly public case such as

13   this one, where security and privacy risks are heightened and the potential for doxxing is real.

14          Plaintiff has offered three arguments for why this information should not be redacted:

15   (1) the redactions obscure information and will reduce the exhibits’ impact with the jury; (2)

16   jurors are generally skeptical of redactions; and (3) there is no time to make the redactions.

17          First, Microsoft’s proposed redactions obscure nothing substantive. For emails,

18   Microsoft seeks to redact only the username portion of email addresses—the Microsoft domain

19   and the full name of the sender/recipient remain visible. As shown with the exemplary exhibit

20   PX-221 below, the jury will see exactly who sent and received each email and that they are

26   nothing substantive precedes the “@” in an email address. And to the extent there is any
