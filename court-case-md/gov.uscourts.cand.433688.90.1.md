 1 I.        INTRODUCTION

 2           Defendants’ L.R. 7-3(d)(1) Objections, Dkt. Nos. 83 and 84 (collectively, “Objections”),

 3 should be overruled for two independent reasons: First, Plaintiffs’ Motion for a Preliminary

 4 Injunction (“Motion”) established their prima facie case for injunctive relief. The Reply merely
 5 provides evidence that directly rebuts arguments raised in Defendants’ Oppositions that Plaintiffs
 6 could not have anticipated at the time they filed the Motion. For example, Plaintiffs could not
 7 have known that Hoffman would (falsely) swear he has no operational involvement with AI at
 8 Microsoft, or that Microsoft and OpenAI would try to obfuscate their well-known Fund No
 9 Competitors edict with carefully crafted declarations. Second, the Objections fail to establish that
10 the challenged declarations are inadmissible for the purposes offered, particularly in light of the
11 relaxed evidentiary standard applicable to a motion for a preliminary injunction.
12 II.       ARGUMENT

13           A.     Rebuttal Evidence Is Permitted on Reply.

14           There is no prohibition on the submission of rebuttal evidence with a reply. Just the

15 opposite; a “reply to an opposition may include affidavits or declarations, as well as a
16 supplemental brief or memorandum.” L.R. 7-3(c); Applied Materials, Inc. v. Demaray LLC, No.
17 2020 WL 7642869, at *1 n.1 (N.D. Cal. Dec. 23, 2020) (exercising discretion to consider “new
18 evidence presented on reply” because it “appears to be a reasonable response to the opposition”).
19 This is consistent with the general rule that “rebuttal evidence is [permitted] to contradict,
20 impeach or defuse the impact of the evidence offered by an adverse party” even if it may be
21 otherwise inadmissible. Huawei Techs., Co., Ltd. v. Samsung Elec. Co., Ltd ., 340 F. Supp. 3d
22 934, 996 (N.D. Cal. 2018); U.S. v. Mendoza-Prado, 314 F.3d 1099, 1105 (9th Cir. 2022)
23 (explaining a party may “introduce otherwise inadmissible evidence when the defendant ‘opens
24 the door’ by introducing potentially misleading testimony” at trial) (internal quotations omitted).1
25
26   To argue to the contrary, Defendants selectively quote from numerous decisions, including

19 have already responded) provided Defendants raises no new arguments, such as attempting to
20 revive their waived Section 8 safe-harbor defense, which they failed to prove in their Oppositions

 2 preliminary injunction is further supported by the fact that a court may well consider evidence
 3 that would be inadmissible at trial. See, e.g., Fellowship of Christian Athletes v. San Jose Unif.

 4 Sch. Dist., 82 F.4th 664, 682 (9th Cir. 2023) (“Legally, that the declarations are hearsay is
 5 irrelevant because a court may exercise its discretion to accept hearsay and make inferences in

 8 cite. See, e.g., Flynt Distrib. Co. v. Harvey, 734 F.2d 1389, 1394 (9th Cir. 1984).
 9           Defendants’ argument that statements made on information and belief must be disregarded

10 is also without merit. “[T]he Court has wide discretion to consider sworn testimony made on
11 information and belief when evaluating a motion for preliminary injunction.” Language Line
12 Servs., Inc. v. Language Servs. Assocs., LLC, 2010 WL 2764714, at *4 n.5 (N.D. Cal. July 13,
13 2010) (citations omitted). “[S]uch issues . . . properly go to weight rather than admissibility.”
14 Disney Enters. v. VidAngel, Inc., 224 F. Supp. 3d 957, 966 (C.D. Cal. 2016)).

19 all relevant evidence before filing would defeat the very purpose of preliminary relief, as it would

 9 III.     CONCLUSION

10          For the foregoing reasons, Defendants’ Objections should be overruled in their entirety.

11 The evidence submitted with Plaintiffs’ Reply is proper rebuttal evidence that directly responds to
12 Defendants’ arguments and declarations first raised in their Oppositions. Even if the Court were
13 to find any of the challenged evidence to be “new,” the appropriate remedy would be to permit
14 Defendants a limited response to such evidence rather than to strike critical evidence. Given the
15 well-established relaxed evidentiary standards governing preliminary injunction proceedings,
16 Defendants’ technical objections regarding hearsay and statements made on information and
17 belief are misplaced and do not undermine the admissibility of Plaintiffs’ rebuttal evidence.
18 Plaintiffs respectfully request that the Court overrule Defendants’ Objections and consider the
19 complete record in evaluating Plaintiffs’ Motion for Preliminary Injunction.
