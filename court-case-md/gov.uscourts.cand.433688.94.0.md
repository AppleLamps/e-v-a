 1                                INTEREST OF AMICUS CURIAE
 2          Encode is a youth-led organization advocating for safe and responsible artificial
 3   intelligence (AI). It is a network of over 1000 volunteers across 40 countries focused on
 4   ensuring the voices of younger generations are heard in critical conversations about how AI
 5   impacts society. Encode works on the issues of today, like deepfakes, children’s safety online,
 6   and oversight of algorithmic weapons systems, while also preparing for emerging challenges,
 7   like the possibility of transformative AI.
 8          Encode’s work on various pieces of state and federal legislation, including co-
 9   sponsoring the landmark Safe and Secure Innovation for Frontier Models Act (SB 1047) in
10   California, gives it insight into the governance challenges posed by the commercialization of
11   advanced AI systems. The organization has helped shape AI policy to foster innovation while
12   protecting the public, having contributed to the White House AI Bill of Rights 1 and President
13   Biden’s Executive Order on AI. 2 Encode is uniquely qualified as a voice representing the
14   public interest in this case because Encode represents a movement of young people—the
15   generation that will inherit the world shaped by the technology that OpenAI is building.
16          No counsel for any party authored this brief in whole or in part, and no person or
17   entity, other than the amicus curiae or its counsel, made a monetary contribution intended to
18   fund the preparation or submission of this brief.

 9   I.      Ensuring the Safety of Advanced AI Systems is Critical to the Public Interest
10           “If . . . the impact of an injunction reaches beyond the parties, carrying with it a
11   potential for public consequences, the public interest will be relevant to whether the district
12   court grants the preliminary injunction.” Stormans, Inc. v. Selecky, 586 F.3d 1109, 1139 (9th
13   Cir. 2009) (citing Sammartano v. First Judicial Dist. Court, 303 F.3d 920, 931 (9th Cir.
14   2002)); see also Alliance for the Wild Rockies v. Cottrell, 632 F.3d 1127, 1138-39 (9th Cir.
15   2011) (overruling district court decision denying request for preliminary injunction in part
16   because “issuing the injunction is in the public interest”); Softketeers, Inc. v. Regal West
17   Corp., 788 F. App’x 468, 469 (9th Cir. 2019) (upholding district court decision granting
18   preliminary injunction, noting that the district court “concluded that . . . the injunction was in
19   the public interest”).
20           “Mitigating the risk of extinction from AI should be a global priority alongside other
21   societal-scale risks such as pandemics and nuclear war.” 4 OpenAI’s CEO Sam Altman joined
22   a long list of luminaries, including recent winners of Nobel Prizes for their work on AI, in
23   signing on to that statement recognizing that AI could pose an existential threat to humanity.
24   While the public’s interest in continuing to exist is clear, one need not agree that AI poses an
25   existential risk to recognize the public interest in ensuring AI is developed and deployed so
26   that it is safe and beneficial. The public already finds itself face-to-face with tangible, wide-

 1          OpenAI plans to transfer control of its operations to a Delaware public benefit
 2   corporation (PBC).12 That would do more than shift control from one kind of “inc.” to
 3   another, leaving the organization’s mission in place. It would convert an organization bound
 4   by law to ensure the safety of advanced AI into one bound by law to “balance” its
 5   consideration of any public benefit against “the pecuniary interests of [its] stockholders.” 8
 6   Del. C. § 365(a). OpenAI’s touted fiduciary duty to humanity would evaporate, as Delaware
 7   law is clear that the directors of a PBC owe no duty to the public at all. 8 Del. C. § 365(b).13
 8          Transferring control of the development and deployment of OpenAI’s technology to a
 9   for-profit entity would also undermine specific safety-focused commitments the nonprofit has
10   made to the public. For example, OpenAI has emblazoned on its website the following
11   commitment to the public it serves as a charity:
12                  We are committed to doing the research required to make AGI
13                  safe, and to driving the broad adoption of such research across
                    the AI community. We are concerned about late-stage AGI
14                  development becoming a competitive race without time for
                    adequate safety precautions. Therefore, if a value-aligned,
15                  safety-conscious project comes close to building AGI before
                    we do, we commit to stop competing with and start assisting

 3            The existing nonprofit-controlled structure provides at least one other safety-focused

12   humanity,’” one wonders who they plan to give it to if permitted to abandon that structure.
13   III.     Control Over Development and Deployment of AGI Is a Charitable Asset that
14            Should Not Be Sold for Any Price
15            OpenAI Inc. currently controls all of the other entities in the OpenAI corporate family,
16   including those entities directly engaged in developing safe and beneficial AGI per OpenAI’s
17   charitable mission. That control is itself a charitable asset of the nonprofit.
18            OpenAI touts the technology it is developing as capable of totally transforming
19   society. It warns potential investors in the existing capped-profit subsidiary controlled by the
20   nonprofit that “[i]t would be wise to view any investment in OpenAI Global, LLC in the spirit
21   of a donation, with the understanding that it may be difficult to know what role money will
22   play in a post-AGI world.”16 In other words, OpenAI believes its technology may so alter our
23   society that money itself ceases to have value. And the company views this transformation as
24   a one-time occurrence. As Altman notably put it, “AGI [is] going to get built exactly once.” 17
