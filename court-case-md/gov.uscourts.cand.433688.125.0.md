 4          A. The OpenAI Defendants’ Position

 5          The OpenAI Defendants’ position is that the expedited proceedings contemplated by the

 6 Court’s Order should be limited to: (i) Count 20; (ii) any of Counts 1-8, 19, 21-22, and 24-26 that

 7 remain following the Court’s disposition of Defendants’ pending motions to dismiss; and (iii) any

 8 counterclaims that share a common nucleus of operative facts with the claims described in (i) or

 9 (ii) (collectively claims (i)-(iii) the “Subject Claims”), subject in all cases to disposition of any

10 future motion(s) for summary judgment.

11          The OpenAI Defendants submit that each of Counts 1-8, 19, 21-22, and 24-26—alleging

12 breach of contract, fraud, false advertising under California law, breach of fiduciary duty, and civil

13 violations of the Racketeer Influenced and Corrupt Organizations Act—share a common nucleus

14 of operative facts with Plaintiff Musk’s breach of charitable trust claim. Like Count 20, these

15 claims concern the circumstances of Musk’s donations to OpenAI, Inc. and the OpenAI

16 Defendants’ alleged breach of purported commitments to Musk in connection with those

17 donations. Accordingly, these claims implicate common questions of fact and law, and should, to

18 the extent they survive Defendants’ pending motions to dismiss and any summary judgment

19 motion, be tried together with Count 20. This approach not only would promote efficiency and

20 judicial economy, but also would avoid unwarranted prejudice to the OpenAI Defendants in the

21 form of exposure to serial proceedings and repetitive overlapping discovery on claims grounded in

22 common facts. (Plaintiffs’ antitrust claims stand on a different footing, because they center not on

 9          B. Microsoft’s Position

10          Microsoft takes no position on whether the scope of the expedited proceedings

11 contemplated by the Court’s Order extends beyond Count 20 (Breach of Charitable Trust).

12 Microsoft, however, has moved to dismiss all the claims against it and the Court’s Order did not

13 determine that any of those claims could go forward. To the extent any claims against Microsoft

14 are incorporated into an expedited proceeding, it would add even more complexity to an already

15 compressed schedule.

16          But even if the proceedings include only Counts brought against the OpenAI Defendants,

17 they would still implicate Microsoft’s interests in at least two important ways: (1) any potential

18 remedy would impact Microsoft, its developers, and/or its customers, as previewed in Microsoft’s

19 Opposition to Plaintiffs’ P.I. Motion (ECF No. 65); and (2) discovery and any factual or legal

20 decision related to the in-scope Count(s) could bear on Microsoft’s alleged liability or defenses on

21 any stayed Microsoft Counts. For these reasons, even if Microsoft is not a defendant in any of the

22 expedited Counts, it seeks procedural rights necessary to protect its interests, including the right to

23 participate in limited discovery, submit limited briefing, cross examine witnesses on a limited

24 basis, and present limited evidence at trial, as appropriate.
