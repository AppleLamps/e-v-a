                                                A.         DISGORGEMENT OF PROFITS

                                  17
                                       Microsoft to disgorge their ill-gotten gains—is an equitable or legal remedy. The distinction

                                   6   definite and damages may be calculated without an accounting, the action is legal.” Am. Master

                                  16   not seek to measure harm to the plaintiff.

                                  24   not persuade. Fundamentally, Wazzan analyzes the wrongful gains that OpenAI and Microsoft

                                  25   earned as a result of “Musk’s critical contributions to OpenAI from the time that Mr. Musk helped

                                  26   found it”—i.e., the $38 million that Musk donated. (Dkt. No. 392 at 2.) Wazzan then expressly

                                  27   tethers Musk’s remedy to those donations and any profit made from those donations, not the

                                  28   amount by which Musk was injured. That Musk’s donations serve as the linchpin indicates

                                  14   II.     JURY INSTRUCTIONS
                                  15           A.      INSTRUCTION NO. 30-P: CONTINUOUS ACCRUAL
                                  16           The parties dispute whether Musk is entitled to a “continuous accrual” jury instruction. 4

                                  17   Musk seeks to instruct the jury that his claims were timely if the defendants were under a

                                  18   “continuing or recurring obligation and continued to engage in the wrongful acts after the

                                  19   applicable date.” (Dkt. No. 433 at 73, Proposed Jury Instruction 30-P.) Musk argues that

                                  20   continuous accrual applies under Aryeh v. Canon Business Solutions, Inc., 55 Cal.4th 1185, 1198–

                                  21   99 (2013), because his claims “accrued every time Defendants used the fruits of Musk’s

                                  22   contributions to pursue purposes other than the charitable purposes for which those contributions

                                  23   were given.” (Dkt. No. 427 at 44.) Defendants disagree, arguing that continuous accrual is

                                  24   inapplicable because the doctrine applies to only alleged severable obligations. The Court agrees.

                                  25           “Generally speaking, continuous accrual applies whenever there is a continuing or

                                  26   recurring obligation: ‘When an obligation or liability arises on a recurring basis, a cause of action

                                  27
                                               4
                                  28             At the pretrial conference, Musk stated that he is not seeking a “continuous violation”
                                       instruction. As such, the Court does not reach the applicability of that doctrine.
                                                                                         5
                                       Case 4:24-cv-04722-YGR            Document 456         Filed 03/31/26     Page 6 of 7




                                   1   accrues each time a wrongful act occurs, triggering a new limitations period.’” Aryeh, 55 Cal.4th

                                   2   at 1199 (quoting Hogar Dulce Hogar v. Cmty. Dev. Comm’n, 110 Cal.App.4th 1288, 1295

                                   3   (2003)). “Because each new breach of such an obligation provides all the elements of a claim—

                                   4   wrongdoing, harm, and causation—each may be treated as an independently actionable wrong with

                                   5   its own time limit for recovery.” Id. (citation omitted).

                                   6          Courts routinely find that continuous accrual is inapplicable when the defendant’s

                                   7   obligations are not “periodic,” “recurring,” or “severed into intervals.” See, e.g., Lamont v. Time

                                   8   Warner, Inc., 586 F.App’x 357, 357 (9th Cir. 2014) (no continuous accrual “where performance of

                                   9   contractual obligations [was not] severed into intervals”); Ryan v. Microsoft Corp., 147 F.Supp.3d

                                  10   868, 896 (N.D. Cal. 2015) (no continuous accrual because defendant’s “continuing obligation to

                                  11   avoid anticompetitive behavior [was] not a periodic, recurring obligation”); State of California ex

                                  12   rel. Metz v. CCC Info. Servs., Inc., 149 Cal.App.4th 402, 418 (2007) (no continuous accrual where

 United States District Court




                                  13   the alleged fraud did “not involve a recurring obligation or any such periodic payment

                                  14   obligations”).

                                  15          Aryeh is instructive. There, the defendant, a lessor of copy machines, was allegedly

                                  16   charging plaintiff for defendant’s own test copies under the parties’ monthly rent contract. The

                                  17   California Supreme Court held that continuous accrual applied because defendant’s duty “not to

                                  18   impose unfair charges in monthly bills . . . was a continuing one, susceptible to recurring breaches.

                                  19   Accordingly, each alleged breach must be treated as triggering a new statute of limitations.”

                                  20   Aryeh, 55 Cal.4th at 1200.

                                  21          None of Musk’s authorities apply continuous accrual outside the context of a recurring,

                                  22   severable obligation. See Gilkyson v. Disney Enters., Inc., 244 Cal.App.4th 1336, 1343 (2016)

                                  23   (continuous accrual applied where defendant had “obligation to pay periodic royalties”); J.B.

                                  24   Painting & Waterproofing, Inc. v. RGP Holdings, LLC, 650 F.App’x 450, 453-54 (9th Cir. 2016)

                                  25   (continuous accrual applied for “each delivery . . . in a series of orders placed”); POGA Mgmt.

                                  26   Partners LLC v. Medfiler LLC, 2014 WL 3963854, at *1, *11 (N.D. Cal. Aug. 12, 2014)

                                  27   (continuous accrual applied where defendants were obligated “to make quarterly distributions of

                                  28   income” to plaintiff); Water Audit Cal. v. Merced Irrigation Dist., 111 Cal.App.5th 1147, 1192
                                                                                          6

                                   1   (2025) (continuous accrual applied where the statute at issue set forth a violation as recurring

                                   2   “[e]ach day that a violation . . . occurs or continues”); Washington v. CVS Pharmacy, Inc., No.

                                   3   4:15-cv-03504-YGR, Dkt. No. 610 (issuing jury instruction that claims for purchases older than

                                   4   three years were time-barred where defendants were obliged to offer lowest price that provider

                                   5   would charge on a “particular day”).

                                   6          Here, Musk does not argue that defendants owed him recurring, severable obligations or

                                   7   duties. Rather, Musk argues that the jury should receive a continuous accrual instruction merely

                                   8   because OpenAI’s obligation to Musk to stay open source and nonprofit remained ongoing. In

                                   9   Musk’s view, OpenAI has breached that obligation “on a daily basis by continuing to operate its

                                  10   for-profit subsidiary for private gain.” (Dkt. No. 427 at 44–45.) Under this theory, each such

                                  11   breach—and each of Microsoft’s investments that allegedly aided and abetted the breach—would

                                  12   be subject to its own limitations period. However, continuous accrual does not apply just because

 United States District Court




                                  13   the alleged wrong has “continuing impact.” Armstrong Petroleum Corp. v. Tri-Valley Oil & Gas

                                  14   Co., 116 Cal.App.4th 1375, 1389 (2004). Instructing otherwise would vitiate the statute and allow

                                  18          Accordingly, the Court will not instruct the jury on continuous accrual.
