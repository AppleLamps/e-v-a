 1            The OpenAI Defendants respectfully submit this memorandum of law in support of their
 2   application to elicit through the examination of Greg Brockman evidence concerning a message
 3   that Plaintiff Elon Musk sent Mr. Brockman on the eve of trial in this case.
 4            On or about April 25, 2026, two days before trial began, Mr. Musk sent a message to Mr.
 5   Brockman to gauge interest in settlement. When Mr. Brockman responded with a suggestion that
 6   both sides drop their respective claims, Mr. Musk shot back: “By the end of this week, you and
 7   Sam will be the most hated men in America. If you insist, so it will be.” 1
 8            This statement—like similar menacing statements Mr. Musk has made in litigation—is
 9   admissible under the Federal Rules of Evidence. See Giuseppe Pampena v. Elon R. Musk, No. 3:22-
10   cv-05937-CRB, Trial Tr. vol. 7 1405:23-1409:14, 1499:16-1500:14 (N.D. Cal. Mar. 9, 2026)
11   (excerpt attached as Exhibit A hereto) (admitting evidence of threat conveyed by Mr. Musk through
12   his attorneys in the context of litigation over his acquisition of Twitter). It tends to prove motive
13   and bias, and, in particular, that Mr. Musk’s motivation in pursuing this lawsuit is to attack a
14   competitor and its principals.
15            That the statement was made following a settlement overture does not prevent its admission.
16   Even assuming it qualifies as a “statement made during compromise negotiations about [a] claim,”
17   Federal Rule of Evidence 408 prohibits the admission of such a statement only where the party
18   seeking exclusion carries its burden of establishing that the statement is offered to “prove or
19   disprove the validity or amount of a disputed claim or to impeach by a prior inconsistent statement
20   or a contradiction.” Fed. R. Evid. 408(a)(2); Rondor Music Intern. Inc. v. TVT Records LLC, 2006
21   WL 5105272, at *11 (C.D. Cal. Aug. 21, 2006). The Rule expressly allows admission of this
22   evidence “for another purpose, such as proving a witness’s bias or prejudice[.]” Fed. R. Evid.
23   408(b); see also Rhoades v. Avon Prod., Inc., 504 F.3d 1151, 1161-62 (9th Cir. 2007) (Rule 408 is
24   “of limited applicability,” such that “[w]hen statements made during settlement are introduced for
25   a purpose unrelated to liability, the policy underlying the Rule is not injured.”); Hudspeth v.

 3          Plaintiff’s statement of menace falls outside of Rule 408(a)(2)’s prohibition. The OpenAI
 4   Defendants do not intend to offer Mr. Musk’s statement to demonstrate lack of evidence on any
 5   claim or as a prior inconsistent statement. As noted, the statement is offered instead as admissible
 6   evidence of Plaintiff’s motive in pursuing this lawsuit. See, e.g., Rhoades, 504 F.3d at 1161
 7   (evidence of threats made during settlement negotiations was admissible so long as not used to
 8   establish liability or show prior inconsistent statement); Corbrus, LLC v. 8th Bridge Capital, Inc.,
 9   2021 WL 4439220, at *7-8 (C.D. Cal. Aug. 9, 2021) (allowing use of statements made during
10   settlement negotiations to show prejudice or bias); Cleverley v. Ballantine, 2013 WL 12320792, at
11   *2 (D. Nev. Aug. 29, 2013) (statement made during settlement negotiations admissible to support
12   argument that plaintiff “intended to punish the Defendants”); Justice v. Meares, 2021 WL 3410045,
13   at *5 (E.D. Tenn. Aug. 4, 2021) (extortionate threats during negotiations not protected under Rule
14   408); Levenstein v. Salafsky, 2002 WL 849594, at *1 (N.D. Ill. May 2, 2002) (evidence of
15   statements made during negotiations that are offered to show a pattern of coercion fall outside the
16   scope of Rule 408). “Rule 408 is designed to ensure that parties may make offers during settlement
17   negotiations without fear that those same offers will be used to establish liability should settlement
18   efforts fail. When statements made during settlement are introduced for a purpose unrelated to
19   liability, the policy underlying the Rule is not injured.” Rhoades, 504 F.3d at 1161-62. Such is the
20   case here; Mr. Musk’s threat that Mr. Brockman and Mr. Altman would become the most hated
21   men in America if they continued to defend against his claims was “coercive rather than
22   conciliatory.” See Cassino v. Reichhold Chem., Inc., 817 F.2d 1338, 1342 (9th Cir.1987) (“Rule
23   408 should not be used to bar relevant evidence simply because one party calls its communication
24   with the other party a settlement offer.”). The statement should therefore be admitted.
