 1            As the OpenAI Defendants note, Plaintiffs’ application for relator status has received a

 2 preliminary denial from the California Attorney General’s deputy, Dkt. 157, Ex. A, but that decision
 3 is not yet final. Plaintiffs have requested the Attorney General reconsider his deputy’s decision for

 4 several key reasons. See Ex. 8. First, there is substantial public interest in this matter as
 5 demonstrated by multiple formal petitions from diverse coalitions, including a recent petition from
 6 fifty-five labor and non-profit organizations, urging the Attorney General’s intervention. Exs. 1-7.1
 7 Second, his deputy’s decision appears to misapprehend Musk and Zilis’s First Amended Complaint
 8 and their derivative claims in this case. Third, the decision mischaracterizes or misunderstands
 9 Musk’s recent assemblage of a consortium of investors to bid on OpenAI, Inc.’s assets to ensure fair
10 market value for such assets and responsible control thereof—contingent upon Altman and other
11 Board members persisting with their plan to sell those assets to Altman’s for-profit company in
12 what is undeniably a self-dealing transaction. In and outside this case, Musk has repeatedly asserted
13 that he strongly prefers that the charity retain its current control of OpenAI and adhere to its
14 founding principles and mission. See Dkt. 117 at 2. It is not clear whether Plaintiffs will secure a
15 final decision from the Attorney General on these issues in time to include the self-dealing claim
16 (Count 23) in the Phase One trial, but they are diligently seeking an expeditious resolution.
17            Failing reconsideration, Plaintiffs are evaluating the potential for state judicial review of the

18 Attorney General’s decision, as it prevents due representation of the People’s interest in these
19 proceedings concerning issues of public importance.
