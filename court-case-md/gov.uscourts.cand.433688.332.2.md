 1                                            DECLARATION

 2          I, Jaymie Parkkinen, declare as follows:

 3          1.       I am a member of the bar of the State of California and an associate at the law firm

 4 Toberoff & Associates, P.C., counsel of record for Elon Musk (“Musk” or “Plaintiff”), Shivon

 5 Zilis (“Zilis”), and Jared Birchall (“Birchall”). I submit this Declaration pursuant to the Court’s

 6 September 22, 2025 order. Dkt. 276. Except where otherwise stated, I have personal knowledge of

 7 the facts set forth herein and, if called as a witness, could and would so testify.

 8          Zilis Requests for Production

 9          2.       OpenAI Defendants (“OpenAI”) served Requests for Production of Documents on

10 non-party Zilis (“RFPs”) on April 7, 2025. OpenAI did not serve Zilis with a document subpoena.

11          3.       Zilis served responses and objections to OpenAI’s RFPs on April 28, 2025.

12          4.       On or around May 8, 2025, we prepared a list of search terms to run against

13 collected data.

14          5.       On or around May 9, 2025, we began coordinating a plan to obtain Zilis’s cell

15 phone data and personal emails.

16          6.       In late May and early June 2025, counsel for the parties began to meet and confer

17 on the scope of the RFPs.

18          7.       I believe the scope of the RFPs was largely agreed upon on or around June 18,

19 2025.

20          8.       On or around June 25, 2025, we were informed that in the process of collecting the

21 cell data from Zilis’s iPhone, Apple, the phone manufacturer, placed an automatic security hold on

22 the data due to its large volume, preventing it from being transmitted. We were further informed

23 that this hold was due to end on or around July 7, 2025.

24          9.       After this Apple security hold was lifted, our e-discovery team attempted to obtain

25 the data from Zilis’s iPhone. We were then informed that numerous bugs in the extraction software

26 had caused repeated issues in the data extraction process.

27          10.      We were further informed that once the data extraction was obtained, the issues

28 with the software continued during application of search terms to the extracted data. For instance,

 3          Birchall Subpoena

 4          24.    On June 27, 2025, we accepted service of the Birchall document subpoena.

12          28.    On or around July 22, 2025, we initiated efforts to obtain data from Birchall’s cell

14          29.    On August 1, 2025, we informed OpenAI’s counsel that Birchall’s personal email

15 accounts were not reasonably expected to house responsive documents.

17 schedule, there were challenges in coordinating the data extraction from Birchall’s cell phone

18 resulting in the rescheduling of plans to conduct such extraction.
19          31.    We were informed that on or around August 27, 2025, the data from Birchall’s cell

20 phone was extracted.

22 messages.

24          Musk’s Text Messages

25          34.    We were informed that after Musk’s cell data was extracted and the search terms

26 run, that subset of data was transmitted to Tesla’s legal department.

27          35.    We were informed that Tesla’s in-house counsel conducted an initial review of the

28 data for Tesla’s sensitive business information as well as Musk’s sensitive personal information.

 1          36.    We were informed that the resulting data was then transmitted to Plaintiff’s

 2 discovery platform.

 3          37.    No other Musk-affiliated company has played any role in the collection, review, or

 4 production of Musk’s texts.

 5          38.    In the afternoon on September 18, 2025, OpenAI’s counsel sent us its half of a

 6 court-ordered status update. Dkt. 263.

 7          39.    In OpenAI’s half of the letter, it identified two text messages between Musk and

 8 Zilis that Zilis produced, but Musk had not.

 9          40.    As of September 19, 2025, we believed this production discrepancy may have been

10 a result of an unknown transmission issue between Tesla’s and Plaintiff’s discovery vendors that

11 had occurred after Tesla had completed its initial review of Musk’s texts.

12          41.    Our investigation continued over the weekend, and by September 23, 2025 we were

13 able to conclude that there had not been a transmission issue.

14          42.    Rather, as described during the September 23 hearing before Judge Hixson, we

15 believe that the Musk side of the text thread beginning with ZILIS-0002342 had been

16 inadvertently omitted from Musk’s production due to a coding error when it should have been

17 produced. We have no reason to believe that this error had any broader effect beyond this specific

18 text message thread. The document beginning with ZILIS-0002319, while containing some
19 responsive material, primarily contained a screenshot of a different text message exchange

20 between Musk and Sam Altman. Musk had already produced the duplicate native file associated

21 with the original Musk/Altman text exchange. See 2024MUSK-0006375. We are informed that the

22 Musk side of the text exchange was withheld pending redaction, since it contained personal photos

23 and information about Musk’s young children.
