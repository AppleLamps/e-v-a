 2          Judicial notice is appropriate under these governing principles with respect to each of the

 4          The Court may take judicial notice of the complaint, motion to strike, and opposition to

 5   demurrer filed in Musk v. Altman (Exhibits A, B, and C), as they are publicly available court filings

 6   in a related legal proceeding. It is well established that courts “may take judicial notice of court

 7   filings” in other actions. See, e.g., Reyn’s Pasta Bella, LLC v. Visa USA, Inc., 442 F.3d 741, 746

 8   n.6 (9th Cir. 2006) (taking judicial notice of briefs, a hearing transcript, and “several other

 9   pleadings, memoranda, [and] expert reports” filed in related litigation); Burbank-Glendale-

10   Pasadena Airport Auth. v. City of Burbank, 136 F.3d 1360, 1364 (9th Cir. 1998) (taking “judicial

11   notice of pleadings filed in a related state court action”); In re Icenhower, 755 F.3d 1130, 1142 (9th

12   Cir. 2014) (taking judicial notice of court filings from bankruptcy proceeding).

13          Musk’s complaint in California Superior Court (Ex. A), defendants’ motion to strike certain

14   demands for relief from that complaint (Ex. B), and Musk’s opposition to defendants’ demurrer to

15   that complaint (Ex. C) all furnish relevant context for the claims Musk has asserted in this action,

16   which are substantially similar but not identical to the ones he asserted in the earlier state court

17   action. Because these prior filings are publicly available and not reasonably subject to dispute, the

18   Court can take notice of them in considering the OpenAI Defendants’ concurrently-filed motion to

19   dismiss.

20          The Court may likewise take judicial notice of the webpage titled OpenAI and Elon Musk

21   (Ex. D) because it is publicly available and its contents “can be accurately and readily determined

22   from sources whose accuracy cannot reasonably be questioned.” Fed. R. Evid. 201(b)(2). Courts

23   have repeatedly recognized that publicly available materials on broadly accessible websites may be

24   considered in ruling on a motion to dismiss. See Perkins v. LinkedIn Corp., 53 F. Supp. 3d 1190,

25   1204-06 (N.D. Cal. 2014) (taking judicial notice of the contents of public webpages and explaining

26   that “[p]roper subjects of judicial notice when ruling on a motion to dismiss include . . . publically

27   accessible websites”); see also In re Google Assistant Priv. Litig., 457 F. Supp. 3d 797, 813-14

28   (N.D. Cal. 2020) (taking judicial notice of a news report, Google’s Terms of Service, Privacy
