                   14     I.    LEGAL STANDARD
                   15           “A motion to stay a magistrate judge’s order pending review by the assigned district judge
                   16   is evaluated under the same standard as a motion to stay pending appeal.” Forrest v. Facebook,
                   17   Inc., No. 5:22-CV-03699-EJD, 2023 WL 1931356, at *1 (N.D. Cal. Jan. 18, 2023). Issuing a stay
                   18   pending appeal is “an exercise of judicial discretion.” Nken v. Holder, 556 U.S. 418, 433, (2009)
                   19   (internal quotations omitted). In deciding whether a stay is appropriate, courts generally look to
                   20   four factors: (1) whether the moving party will be irreparably injured absent a stay; (2) whether the
                   21   moving party has made a strong showing that they are likely to succeed on the merits; (3) whether
                   22   issuance of the stay will substantially injure the other parties; and (4) whether public interest favors
                   23   a stay. Id. at 434.

                   12    Perez, 640 F.3d at 966.
                   13               D. Public Interest Favors Granting A Stay
                   14                   Finally, the public interest supports issuing a temporary stay to ensure that judicial
                   15    proceedings remain fair, efficient, and protective of individual, non-party rights. The core purpose
                   16    of Rule 72(a) is to provide meaningful district court review of magistrate discovery orders. This
                   17    review is particularly important where, as here, the discovery intrudes upon a non-party’s
                   18    constitutional privacy interests. Forcing compliance before review would undermine the Federal
                   19    Rules of Civil Procedure and undermine confidence in the judicial system. Conversely, a short stay
                   20    pending review advances the public’s interest in orderly, just, and economical resolution of
                   21    disputes, consistent with Federal Rule of Civil Procedure 1. See Canchola, 2025 WL 1377817, at
                   22    *4 (C.D. Cal. Apr. 17, 2025) (public interest factor favored stay in light of interest in “limiting the
                   23    unnecessary sharing and use” of private information); Gamino, 2021 WL 2309972, at *1 (same).
                   24    III.   CONCLUSION
                   25           For all these reasons, the Court should stay compliance with the Discovery Order pending
                   26    this Court’s review of Mr. Sutskever’s forthcoming Rule 72(a) Motion for Relief
